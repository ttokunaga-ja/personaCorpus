# Aster Legal privacy clause playbook

Reusable drafting guide for privacy, procurement, and product counsel. It uses only synthetic examples: Matter Alpha, preservation population **42**, and exception review date **2026-07-12**. Replace bracketed terms and confirm current law and contract context before use.

## Intake before drafting

1. Identify the parties, service, processing role, data categories, locations, and commercial agreement.
2. Confirm whether the clause is a controller–processor term, an independent-controller arrangement, or a limited service-provider commitment.
3. Log operational dependencies: security schedule, subprocessor process, transfer mechanism, assistance workflow, return/deletion path, and legal-hold exception.

## Negotiation positions

- Ask for a precise service description instead of a broad catch-all purpose.
- Keep audit language proportionate: remote evidence review first, safeguards for confidential material, and reasonable frequency.
- Attach the processing schedule rather than embedding changing detail in the master clause.
- Treat preservation exceptions as narrow, documented, access-restricted, and time-bounded. Example only: Matter Alpha / 42 / review by 2026-07-12.

## Review questions

1. Does every operational promise have an owner and implementable procedure?
2. Are onward transfers and subprocessors aligned with the service architecture?
3. Does the deletion clause account for backups and valid legal-retention exceptions?
4. Are placeholders removed, cross-references correct, and negotiated changes reflected in all schedules?
