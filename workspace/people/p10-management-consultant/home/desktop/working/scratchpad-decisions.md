**Workspace:** Summit Vale Advisory / Client Alpha Phase 1

**Status:** Active working notes; confirm before presenting externally

## Why this scratchpad exists

This is the team’s short-form decision surface for work in motion. It is not a polished recommendation and it is not a replacement for the question log, the staffing plan, or the Finance tracker. Its purpose is to capture the thinking that connects those artifacts: what the team believes, what would change that belief, and what should happen next.

The central constraint is useful discipline. Client Alpha Phase 1 needs a practical next decision. Client Beta Phase 2 needs to remain visible because the same people and Finance controls touch both efforts. The Summit Vale Advisory portfolio budget is ¥42.8M. That number is an anchor for trade-offs, not a claim that all work has equal priority.

## Current working view

The strongest emerging theme is that Alpha’s immediate value will come from making decision ownership, evidence confidence, and finance implications visible in the same operating rhythm. The team does not yet have evidence to argue for a large transformation program. We do have enough directional information to test a narrower proposition: a repeatable decision-and-control cadence can reduce delay and make scope or spend exceptions easier to surface.

This view is useful only if it survives four checks. First, the proposed cadence must solve a real delay, not simply add process. Second, Finance must agree that the evidence pack is sufficient for the next release. Third, the required specialist effort must fit with Beta Phase 2. Fourth, stakeholders must understand the decision without needing a long explanation.

## Decisions made today

### 1. Treat the recommendation as a sequence, not a finished state

The Alpha story will lead with a practical first move. The working deck should not imply that every future design detail has been settled. This keeps the recommendation credible and gives the client a clear decision to make now: approve the next routine, owner, and evidence gate.

### 2. Use evidence tiers in every synthesis discussion

Theo’s proposed labels are decision-ready, directional, and unavailable. The labels are intentionally plain. They allow the team to use what is known without overstating precision. Any conclusion that rests materially on directional evidence must name the follow-up that would strengthen or reverse it.

### 3. Keep the control gate lightweight

Nina’s current minimum is owner, evidence, spend implication, and due date. The gate should be tested against a recent decision path before being described as a solution. If the test shows that delay came from an external dependency instead of unclear ownership, the recommendation will need to change.

### 4. Reconcile capacity at portfolio level

Keiko will use the weekly staffing plan to show Alpha, Beta, and portfolio demand together. The team will not solve a shared-capacity issue by silently borrowing days. If capacity is constrained, the decision should name the work deferred, the risk accepted, or the budget implication.

## Open tensions

There is a healthy tension between moving quickly and making the recommendation auditable. The team should not resolve this by collecting every conceivable input. Instead, the threshold is decision usefulness: would an additional piece of evidence change the option, the spend view, the owner, or the timing? If not, it belongs in a later backlog.

There is also a potential tension between Alpha urgency and Beta readiness. Alpha is farther along in its immediate synthesis. Beta’s Phase 2 work can benefit from the control routines and evidence tiers developed here, but Beta should not be forced into Alpha’s timing. The portfolio conversation should make that distinction explicit.

## Recommended next moves

1. Complete the four short tests: decision-path, Finance control, data confidence, and capacity reconciliation.
2. Refresh the Alpha working deck so each claim carries either evidence or a visible limitation.
3. Confirm whether the next client discussion is a decision meeting or a discovery checkpoint; the agenda changes materially depending on that answer.
4. Update the consolidated tracker after the Finance and capacity sessions, not before.
5. Preserve any unresolved item in the question log with a named owner and a date. An unresolved item without either is not a managed risk.

## What would change the current view

The team should revisit the focused-cadence hypothesis if the evidence shows that decisions are already clear but blocked by external client dependencies, if the Finance review requires a materially more complex control model, or if the capacity analysis shows no meaningful Alpha–Beta overlap. In those cases, the recommendation should pivot toward the true bottleneck rather than defend the current framing.

For now, the direction remains sound: make the next decision smaller, clearer, and easier to govern. The goal is a working recommendation that can survive scrutiny from the client, Finance, and the broader portfolio team without hiding uncertainty.
