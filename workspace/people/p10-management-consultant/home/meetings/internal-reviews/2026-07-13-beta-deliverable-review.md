# Client Beta Phase 2 deliverable review

## Review mandate

This Summit Vale Advisory review assessed the Client Beta Phase 2 design pack on 2026-07-13. Client Beta is considering a portfolio reporting and finance-governance expansion. The pack must stand on its own: Client Beta has separate sponsors, decisions, and funding. It nevertheless links to Client Alpha Phase 1 because both engagements test related Finance workstream practices and share the same portfolio learning agenda. The reference point for that portfolio is Client Alpha’s controlled pilot within its budget ¥42.8M; it is not a funding source or a commitment by Client Beta.

The review concluded that the Beta pack can progress to sponsor pre-read after clarity is added on decision rights, data responsibility, and the distinction between a Phase 2 design recommendation and an implementation authorization. The current recommendation is to approve a 10-week design-and-readiness increment, with a gated choice between a limited reporting pilot and broader rollout planning at its end.

## Review basis

Evidence included Beta sponsor interviews, a portfolio reporting prototype, Finance workstream workshop notes, issue-register extracts, and a draft dependency map. The team considers the qualitative evidence strong on pain points: business leads lack a shared view of commitments, finance teams repeat reconciliations, and escalation choices arrive late. Quantitative evidence is preliminary. The prototype has been tested using representative synthetic data and has not yet been proven with full client source records.

Nina Solberg highlighted that the Beta team should not borrow Alpha’s performance assumptions. Alpha Phase 1 is still validating its own baseline as of 2026-07-13. The pack may say that Alpha will generate transferable lessons on exception taxonomy and review cadence, but it must not represent Alpha outcomes as established savings or controls. This boundary is especially important because both narratives use the term “portfolio visibility” differently.

Keiko Ward reviewed the document’s traceability and found that the proposed ten-week sequence is reasonable if two dependencies are recognized: source-system data ownership and the sponsor’s availability for weekly decision reviews. The existing draft assumes both. The revised dependency map should label them assumptions, show a fallback sequence, and specify the decision needed if access is delayed.

## Key review discussion

### Scope and decision rights

Avery Calder asked the team to simplify the scope statement. The pack should state that Phase 2 designs governance, reporting requirements, and implementation options. It does not select a technology vendor, rewrite accounting policy, or authorize organizational restructuring. The difference matters because the current executive summary implies that a platform decision is imminent.

Theo proposed a decision-rights ladder: sponsors decide scope trade-offs and gate progression; Finance workstream owners validate control implications; data owners confirm feasibility and definitions; Summit Vale Advisory provides analysis, facilitation, and recommendation. The group agreed. This language prevents ambiguity when workshop participants offer preferences that are not formal approvals.

### Financial framing

The Beta pack contains no approved implementation budget. The team agreed not to introduce a speculative total. It will instead present resource ranges for the design increment and explain that later cost estimates depend on data remediation, integration requirements, and the client’s chosen rollout pattern. A footnote will anchor the portfolio context: Client Alpha Phase 1 has a separate ¥42.8M envelope, which is monitored by the Alpha Finance workstream and cannot be reallocated through this review.

Nina recommended a benefits frame with three tests: can the client reduce reconciliation rework, can it improve the timeliness of decision escalation, and can it maintain required finance controls? None should be declared achieved during Phase 2 design. This protects the sponsor from mistaking an attractive prototype for verified operational value.

### Operating model and data

The most substantive risk is inconsistent definition ownership. Today, business teams can alter status labels without a documented finance review. The proposed governance model assigns definition stewardship to a named client data owner, with Finance workstream sign-off for measures that influence reporting. Keiko requested an explicit change-control path, including evidence of approval and communication to report users.

Theo noted that weekly decision reviews could become performative if the pre-read is not stable. The pack will therefore define a minimum pre-read standard: updated issue register, variance commentary, decision requests, and source confidence label. A review may be deferred if material source confidence is low, but the deferral itself must be recorded and escalated after two consecutive occurrences.

## Review comments

The team asked Avery to replace “single version of truth” with more precise wording. The work aims for governed, traceable reporting for defined decisions, not universal agreement across every operational data element. Nina asked that the finance-control appendix show which measures are management indicators and which are subject to reconciliation. Keiko asked for an accessibility check on the prototype screenshots and clearer alt text before sponsor circulation.

The group also questioned a chart that combines backlog volume and financial exposure on one axis. These variables have different units and can create an overstated visual relationship. Theo will replace it with two charts and an explanatory note describing when the measures are expected to move together and when they are not.

## Open questions

Client Beta must confirm the accountable data owner before the first design workshop. It must also identify the sponsor who can resolve cross-functional priority conflicts within five working days. A third question concerns cadence: should weekly reviews align to the existing finance close calendar, or should they operate independently during Phase 2? The recommendation is to align to close where possible, while preserving an urgent escalation route for material reporting risk.

The team must decide how Alpha learning will be exchanged. The appropriate mechanism is an internal Summit Vale Advisory learning note approved by each engagement lead, not shared client documentation or combined governance. This maintains confidentiality boundaries and ensures that any claimed learning has a source and limitation.

## Recommendation

Issue the Beta sponsor pre-read after the stated corrections. Request approval for the ten-week design-and-readiness increment and a formal gate at its conclusion. At that gate, present evidence for either a limited reporting pilot, additional data remediation, or a pause. The recommendation is credible because it maintains Finance workstream control requirements while acknowledging that outcome evidence is still emerging.

Before final circulation, verify that the Alpha reference retains the date 2026-07-13, the Client Alpha Phase 1 label, and the separate budget ¥42.8M. These details are portfolio context only. The Beta pack must avoid presenting them as Beta assumptions, benefits, or available funds.
