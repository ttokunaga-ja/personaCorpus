## Evidence reviewed

The review used the Phase 1 diagnostic interviews, the current-state process map, the finance cost model v0.7, workshop notes, and the draft executive pack. The evidence indicates that manual exception handling, unclear approval thresholds, and fragmented portfolio reporting are consuming management attention. It does not establish that a full automation deployment will recover all estimated effort; the model currently treats recoverable time as a planning range rather than a booked saving.

Nina reported that the Finance workstream has reconciled 91 percent of the addressable cost categories to source extracts. The remaining nine percent relates to cross-charged contractor support and timing differences in accruals. The aggregate budget of ¥42.8M is unchanged. Within that envelope, the pilot design reserves ¥9.6M for process redesign and training, ¥7.4M for data remediation and reporting configuration, and ¥4.1M for contingency and assurance. These are planning allocations, not client-approved purchase orders.

Theo noted that the process-map evidence is adequate for the first pilot wave but thin on hand-offs between local finance analysts and the central reporting team. The draft correctly identifies the hand-off as a risk but presently assigns it a generic mitigation. The final version should name the acceptance artifact: a reconciled weekly exception report signed by the receiving finance manager. That revision turns an intention into a testable control.

Keiko found that 18 of 22 key claims in the executive summary have a source reference or explicit hypothesis label. Four claims need repair. Two benefits statements cite workshop consensus without stating its limitations. One timing statement assumes access to a client reporting environment. One recommendation calls an approval matrix “standard practice” but does not specify the control objective. None requires a change in recommendation, but all need clearer provenance.

## Review comments and resolution

### Executive narrative

Avery’s principal comment was that the draft reaches the recommendation too slowly. Senior readers should first see the decision requested, the reason it is needed now, and the consequence of deferral. The revised opening should state that the Phase 1 pilot is an evidence-building investment within the ¥42.8M envelope, not a commitment to a larger transformation. It should also show the portfolio link: Client Alpha Phase 1 generates reusable finance-control patterns that inform Client Beta Phase 2, while each client retains separate decisions, budgets, and governance.

The team rejected language suggesting “guaranteed savings.” The preferred formulation is that the pilot is expected to test whether cycle-time and rework reductions are achievable under actual control requirements. Benefits should be expressed as a range and converted to a target only after Client Alpha selects the baseline period.

### Finance workstream

Nina challenged the current chart that displays a single net-benefit figure. It blends cash avoidance, capacity release, and risk reduction in a way that could be read as a financial commitment. The updated exhibit will separate: cash costs avoided, capacity potentially redeployed, one-off implementation costs, and qualitative control benefits. The Finance workstream will include a footnote explaining that opportunity cost is excluded until the client verifies role-level demand.

Theo asked whether the ¥4.1M contingency is sufficient given the unresolved data lineage issue. Nina’s response was that the reserve is reasonable for the proposed pilot, provided the scope excludes legacy archive cleansing and no new reporting platform is procured. If either trigger occurs, the team must return to steering governance with an options paper rather than absorbing work within contingency.

### Delivery and controls

Keiko observed that the plan currently lists “stakeholder alignment” as a milestone. This is not measurable. The replacement milestone will be “Finance control owners approve pilot exception taxonomy and escalation thresholds.” The completion evidence will be a dated decision record, with Theo responsible for assembling it and Nina responsible for validating its finance content.

The team also agreed to add a red-flag test before the pilot starts: if weekly exceptions exceed the agreed tolerance for two consecutive weeks, the pilot will pause for control review. This is not a failure criterion; it is a governance safeguard. The draft should clearly distinguish pause, remediation, and termination decisions.

## Recommendation and release criteria

The recommended path is to retain the current Phase 1 scope, complete the evidence repairs, and request a decision to begin the staged pilot. Do not expand scope to include enterprise automation selection. The immediate value is a governed test of the Finance workstream controls, with transparent use of the ¥42.8M budget and a credible decision point for later investment.

Before release, every executive claim must be evidence-backed or tagged as an assumption; every budget amount must reconcile to the Finance workstream model; and every milestone must identify an accountable client or Summit Vale Advisory owner. Avery will issue the final internal approval only after Keiko confirms these criteria in writing. If reconciliation identifies a material variance, the team will return to this review rather than adjusting narrative language to fit the previous recommendation.
