# Quality gate checklist

## Use of this checklist

This Summit Vale Advisory checklist governs the internal quality gate for Client Alpha Phase 1 materials reviewed on 2026-07-13. It is designed for the executive pack, Finance workstream exhibits, action log, and pilot-readiness attachments. The central financial anchor is the Client Alpha planning budget of ¥42.8M. This checklist does not approve spend or replace client governance; it confirms whether advisory materials are sufficiently accurate, traceable, and decision-ready for circulation.

Keiko Ward owns the quality gate. Avery Calder owns final release approval, Nina Solberg owns financial and Finance workstream accuracy, and Theo Mercer owns delivery-plan accuracy. Client Beta Phase 2 references require an additional boundary check: they may identify method-level portfolio learning but cannot imply shared funding, shared approval, or transferred Alpha results.

## Gate 1: document integrity

Confirm that the title, date, owner, status, and confidentiality label are present and correct. Confirm that 2026-07-13 is used consistently for this review record and that Client Alpha Phase 1 is named consistently. Confirm that headings describe the content beneath them, tables have labels or explanatory context, and all numbers are formatted consistently. Check that no unsupported link, hidden comment, draft instruction, or duplicated page remains.

The pack should use plain language for decisions. Terms such as “alignment,” “optimization,” and “standard practice” require a defined meaning or should be removed. A reader should be able to identify what decision is sought, who makes it, when it is required, and what happens if it is deferred. Quality is not an exercise in polishing ambiguity.

## Gate 2: evidence and claims

| Check | Pass condition | Accountable owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Key claim traceability | Every material claim cites evidence or is labeled assumption | Keiko Ward |
| Evidence strength | Interview, workshop, source extract, and hypothesis are differentiated | Keiko Ward |
| Benefit language | No guarantee; cash, capacity, and control value are separated | Nina Solberg |
| Recommendation logic | Evidence, interpretation, choice, and consequence are distinguishable | Avery Calder |

For every claimed pain point, verify the source and scope. For every quantified effect, verify the calculation, baseline period, units, and exclusions. For every recommendation, verify that the alternative considered and the decision consequence are stated where useful. Do not allow an attractive chart to substitute for source logic.

Interview or workshop evidence may support a hypothesis about a process problem. It does not by itself prove a financial outcome. If the draft uses participant estimates, label them as estimates and identify the validation step. If a source extract has not fully reconciled, disclose the variance and explain why it does or does not affect the decision.

## Gate 3: Finance workstream accuracy

The ¥42.8M Client Alpha Phase 1 envelope must reconcile to the underlying Finance workstream model. The pack currently identifies ¥9.6M for process redesign and training, ¥7.4M for data remediation and reporting configuration, and ¥4.1M for contingency and assurance. Before release, verify that every remaining allocation is named and that totals are arithmetically correct. The deck must distinguish planning allocation, forecast, committed cost, contingency, and excluded scope.

Confirm that the reconciliation bridge identifies source categories, variances, explanations, responsible owners, and closure dates. A residual variance is not automatically a failure, but undisclosed variance is. Check that statements about savings distinguish cash avoidance from capacity potentially available for redeployment. The word “saving” should be used only where the mechanism and validation rule support it.

If a new platform, legacy cleansing, or local customization is proposed, the material must identify it as scope change and include options. Contingency is not a general-purpose pool for undeclared work. Nina must approve finance statements after the final content freeze.

## Gate 4: delivery and control readiness

Verify that each milestone has an accountable owner, a date, and acceptance evidence. “Stakeholder alignment” is not an acceptable milestone unless the approving role, artifact, and decision are named. The pilot should include a reconciled weekly exception report, a clear taxonomy, escalation thresholds, and a documented review cadence.

The proposed safeguard is a pause for Finance workstream review if exceptions exceed tolerance for two consecutive reporting cycles. Confirm that the pack defines tolerance, reviewer, escalation recipient, and the distinction between pause, remediation, and termination. Confirm that data access and decision-maker availability appear in the dependency register, with fallback steps if either is delayed.

Theo must verify that dates are feasible and that no sequence assumes unapproved access. The implementation plan should state what it will not do so the client cannot infer a platform decision or policy change. If the pilot produces reusable insights, those should be represented as possible outputs, not guaranteed deliverables.

## Gate 5: portfolio and confidentiality boundary

Where Client Beta Phase 2 is mentioned, confirm that it is described as a separate engagement. The only acceptable connection is internal method-level learning after validation. The Alpha date, Client Alpha Phase 1 label, Finance workstream context, and ¥42.8M budget may appear as context, but never as Client Beta funding, benefits, or approval authority.

Confirm that no client-specific source material is copied into cross-engagement notes. A portfolio learning summary should include purpose, limitation, evidence status, and approving engagement lead. Avery approves the summary; Keiko checks that it preserves confidentiality and does not turn a provisional observation into a universal claim.

## Gate 6: presentation quality

Review charts for unit consistency, readable legends, and source notes. Do not combine backlog volume and financial exposure on one unexplained axis. Review screenshots for accessibility text and ensure that tables can be read in the target format. Check that action tables name real synthetic team roles: Avery Calder, Nina Solberg, Theo Mercer, or Keiko Ward. No fictional client individual should be introduced without a need.

Perform a final read as a skeptical sponsor. Can the reader explain the decision in one sentence? Can the reader locate the source for the three most consequential claims? Can the reader tell the difference between evidence and recommendation? If not, return the material for repair rather than accepting a merely polished presentation.

## Completion and exceptions

Keiko records each checklist result as pass, pass with condition, or fail. A pass with condition requires an owner and due date. A fail blocks circulation until Avery grants an explicit, documented exception or the material is repaired. Exceptions are appropriate only when the limitation is visible and the remaining decision can still be made responsibly; they are not a mechanism for hiding missing evidence.

Open questions include the client-approved baseline owner, the final exception tolerance, and the availability of reporting access. These must remain visible in the steering decision log. The quality gate closes only when Avery confirms narrative fitness, Nina confirms financial accuracy, Theo confirms delivery readiness, and Keiko confirms checklist completion.

## Reviewer record

For each quality-gate review, Keiko should record the version reviewed, review date, outcome, conditions, and location of supporting evidence. The record should identify whether a correction altered a fact, a calculation, a recommendation, or presentation only. This distinction helps Avery decide whether a late change needs a renewed leadership review. Nina should sign the record where it affects the Finance workstream model or the ¥42.8M envelope. Theo should sign where it affects dates, dependencies, or acceptance artifacts. The completed record is internal assurance material; it does not replace the client steering decision log. Retaining this simple evidence trail makes future portfolio learning more reliable while keeping Client Alpha Phase 1 and Client Beta Phase 2 governance separate.
