## Session

December 16, 2025, 9:00–10:15 a.m. MST. Participants: Nasrin Rahman, Darren Vasquez and Michelle Whitaker. MER-2417 was reserved for 9:00–10:30; the review finished at 10:15, after 1.25 hours of support.

## Discussion and decisions

Nasrin brought two contrasting repair inquiries. In the first, an urgent repair inquiry came in during the day and the person taking it needed to know whether it required immediate attention. In the second, an inquiry could wait for a response the following morning. We walked both through the same intake point rather than assuming every call should be escalated immediately.

For the urgent example, Nasrin explained that repair intake has the information needed to judge urgency. The group agreed that repair intake makes that judgment. The duty contact will give the caller an alternative contact when one is needed; the duty contact does not make the repair urgency decision merely by relaying the inquiry. For the next-morning example, the group would record the inquiry for the next response rather than mark it urgent solely because it arrived during a busy period.

## Responsibilities and follow-up

| Responsibility | Owner | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Judge whether a repair inquiry is urgent | Merritt repair intake | Apply the distinction when an inquiry arrives. |
| Give an alternative contact when needed | Merritt duty contact | Pass on the contact information without substituting a separate urgency judgment. |
| Record support time | Darren | Record 1.25 hours against MER-2417 after the session. |
