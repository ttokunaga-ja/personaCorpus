# Slide 2 — 10:00–10:25 | Urgent inquiry
Layout: Title and Body

A caller asks for an urgent repair update while intake is busy.

- What information does the team need to judge urgency?
- Who makes the judgment and how does the request reach that person?
- What can the person receiving the call tell the caller while the judgment is pending?

Take away: Nasrin and the administrator identify the judgment route and the information staff need to capture.

Speaker notes: Ask for the current route first. Use the example to expose a gap; do not assume the person answering can make every decision.

# Slide 3 — 10:25–10:50 | Hold when the decision-maker is absent
Layout: Title and Body

The administrator is away and an inquiry needs a decision beyond the receiving person's authority.

- Which information can staff collect without making the decision?
- What must be held, and who should be told it is waiting?
- Is there an authorized alternative decision-maker, and what are that person's limits?

Take away: Confirm the boundary of a hold and the route for urgent exceptions with the administrator.

Speaker notes: Distinguish collecting information from deciding. Any proxy authority and exception route are questions for Merritt, not established permissions.

# Slide 4 — 10:50–11:15 | Return the held inquiry
Layout: Title and Body

A held inquiry is ready for the administrator to review. The caller is awaiting a response.

- What one-line record tells the next shift what decision is outstanding?
- Who returns the inquiry to the decision-maker and who calls the requester?
- How does the next shift know whether the callback is complete?

Take away: Check the proposed handoff record and callback owner with Nasrin; list any route still needing confirmation.

Speaker notes: Invite Nasrin and the administrator to walk through the example in their own terms. Close by reading back the proposed next checks and their owners, without assuming agreement before the discussion.
