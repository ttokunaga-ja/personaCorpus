# Application Desk R7: investigation guidance for administrators

Document: ABS-2026-EX-003　Version: 0.3  
21 May 2026　Miyu Tanaka  
Intended readers: Norimi Watanabe and the investigation desk

## Scope and intended readers

This draft describes six points for administrators who investigate enquiries about Application Desk submissions. Reception staff use separate guidance for checking, resending and enquiries. The points below organise what the investigation side needs to compare or hand over; they do not ask reception staff to change settings or inspect logs.

The explanations describe candidates for R7 and do not establish that a change has been implemented, verified or approved for release.

## Changes and user actions

### Comparing the records

**A01 — Possible duplicate receipt.** When a second receipt is reported, gather the receipt numbers, the reported situation and the time of occurrence. Compare the available receipt records to establish whether they concern the same submission before describing the result. The exact matching keys and the outcome of a resend still require confirmation; a receipt number alone is not proof of a duplicate or of completed business processing.

**A02 — Destination for an application submitted on another person's behalf.** If the destination is questioned on a proxy application, identify the destination shown to the person preparing that application and compare it with the internal routing destination in the records available to the investigation team. Record which display and routing information were compared and any difference that needs follow-up. The reception action is to review the displayed destination before confirming; it is not an instruction to change routing settings. This explanation does not assert that a proposed correction has been verified.

**A03 — Attachment replacement history.** For a question about a replacement, distinguish the attachment name currently displayed from the history of replacements available to the investigation team. Compare the relevant record and the reported time before explaining what can be established. The current name by itself does not describe every earlier replacement, and reception staff are not asked to investigate the history.

### Recording context and handing over an investigation

**A04 — Conditions for a performance statement.** When a performance result is supplied, identify the version measured, the concurrent usage and the sample to which the result applies. Keep the measurement conditions with the result so that unlike runs are not presented as a comparison. The conditions and any achieved performance for this draft remain to be confirmed; no performance figure is promised here.

**A05 — Scope of receipt records.** For an enquiry that needs reproduction, determine which receipt records are available and whether their scope is sufficient to investigate the reported event. Record the relevant version and responsibility for the records with the enquiry. The scope and retention period have not been settled by this explanation; do not infer a number of days from a different service.

**A06 — Incident handover.** When passing an incident to the investigation desk, provide the receipt number, the situation observed and the time it occurred. If a receipt number is unavailable, say so rather than inventing one, and describe the situation and time that are known. The investigation desk can then identify what further records are needed and report back through the enquiry.

## Open questions and review status

Confirm the available comparison information, the version and conditions for any measurement, and the scope and responsibility for receipt records. The six points do not grant additional permissions, change user settings, establish a retention period or guarantee performance. Behaviour remains unverified, the reception method remains open, and release is a separate decision. This draft is for audience and language review.
