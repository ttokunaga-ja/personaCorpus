# Which clock makes twenty-three?

Document: HRA-PIO-BUY-REV-2025-001  
Date: 11 July 2025  
Prepared by: Nicholas Hargreaves  
To: Elizabeth Hargreaves

## Point raised

Elizabeth, your marked v0.5 retains the v0.4 slide heading “23 urgent requests: late demand” and asks beside it, “Late to whom? Who had the quantity?” You also note that these 23 sit within the 37 urgent requests and cannot be generalised to all 93. I agree that the heading can be read as demand arising late, even though the speaker note describes a different sequence. A reader should not have to rely on the note to distinguish those clocks.

## Evidence and response

The 23 are requests for which the first evidence of both quantity and required date precedes the previous business day's 15:00 EDT cut-off, while the first evidence of receipt by Buying follows it. EX-052 illustrates the distinction: RP-6118 recorded 240 cases for 11 June at 14:20 EDT on 10 June; Buying's first receipt was the 11 June 08:42 request. Meera confirmed the quantity and date on 10 July. This supports a late sharing classification, not a claim that the exception would have been avoided.

| Place in next version | Change and reason |
|---|---|
| Urgent reasons table | Replace “late demand” with “Known before cut-off, shared late”. Put all four categories in the same table: 23 known before cut-off and shared late; 8 sudden demand; 4 supplier-side changes; 2 insufficient evidence. State that the denominator is 37 urgent requests. |
| Immediately beneath the urgent table | Say that the 23 are within the 37 urgent requests and are not an estimate for all 93 requests or a count of avoidable exceptions. The 56 other-priority requests are in the intake, but their causes were not counted in this breakdown. |
| Intake table, next to the priority split | State that Urgent is the priority assigned at submission and a route through Buying, not a cause label. Show 37 urgent and 56 other requests within the 93 unique requests. |
| Evidence note to the urgent table | Define the cut-off and the two observations: when quantity and required date were first evidenced, and when Buying first received the demand. Keep the two insufficient-evidence cases separate; a forwarding time alone does not establish the original demand time. |
