# What counts as knowing demand

Document: HRA-PIO-BUY-EV-2025-001  
Date: 14 May 2025  
Author: Neha Patel  
For: Nicholas Hargreaves and Oliver Hewitt

## The question

The first pass through the wording showed that “stock needed today” can describe both an expected replenishment and a genuinely new increase. The request text tells us what was said at submission, not when the quantity and required date became known. Please keep that wording, but do not use it alone as a cause.

## Finding the first usable record

Read back through the available demand records for the first entry that identifies both the quantity and the required date. Record its original reference, local timestamp and the two values. A request created later, or a forwarded copy with its own timestamp, is not proof that the demand was first known at that time. Separately identify the first evidence that Buying received the demand and the request submission time.

Use the preceding business day at 15:00 EDT as the arrangement cut-off for the required date. A demand known before that cut-off and first shared with Buying afterwards can be investigated as a planning delay. A new requirement or increase first known after it may instead be sudden demand. A change to an existing order by the supplier is a different question. Do not infer any of these from urgency alone.

## Two ways the same wording can arise

| Example | Evidence to look for | What the comparison can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Routine replenishment | A dated replenishment record with quantity and required date, then the first transmission to Buying | If the record precedes the cut-off and the first transmission follows it, retain both references for the planning-delay assessment. The request's own creation time is not the demand time. |
| Same-day increase | The original requirement and a later record specifying the additional quantity and date, plus its transmission to Buying | Compare the time of the increase with the cut-off. Do not use the original requirement's time as the time of the additional quantity. |

## Scope and unresolved records

Close the receipt window at 30 June: include requests received from 1 April through 30 June across suppliers, counting each request ID once even if resent. Take the urgent priority from the request as submitted; it is not a cause label. Where either quantity, required date, original timestamp or first receipt by Buying cannot be established, retain the gap and refer the record for enquiry. Do not fill it from the time a copy was forwarded.
