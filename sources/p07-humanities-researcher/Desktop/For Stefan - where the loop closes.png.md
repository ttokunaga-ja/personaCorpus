# For Stefan — where the loop closes

CR/14/A · f. 176r · 19 February 1866

## Comparison

Use just the secretary’s recto. Across the top, place three equally enlarged crops from the same scan, with enough of each word left to see how the initial joins the following letters. Keep the fold in the first crop. Preserve the interruption where it crosses the stroke.

- Left, “Address · l. 4 · Val d’Erne”. Point to the returning stroke beside the fold. Label it “Return stroke visible either side of the fold”.
- Centre, “Same hand · l. 2 · Vu”. Point to the corresponding turn of the V. Label it “Compare the turn in Vu”.
- Right, “Same hand · l. 8 · Berger”. Use two pointers, one to each swelling of the B. Label them “Upper bowl” and “Lower bowl”.

Under the enlargements, place the three complete line strips at original scale, aligned at their left edges in manuscript order. Put the line numbers outside the strips. The strips read as follows.

| Line | Whole line |
|---|---|
| 2 | Vu la demande présentée, le délai de cotisation est accordé. |
| 4 | Val d’Erne, à remettre avec les autres avis de dispense. |
| 8 | Berger pourra conserver l'autre pli jusqu'à votre passage. |

## Caption

I would file the reading under “Val d’Erne” for now. The V in “Vu” is the closer match. The crease still interrupts the first letter. The address line gives us the words on the packet; there is no address endorsement on the back, and “Berger” belongs to the other packet. I would leave the place and onward handover open.
