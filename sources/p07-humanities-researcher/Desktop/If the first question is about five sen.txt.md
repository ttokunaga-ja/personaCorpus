29 April — if the five sen comes up first

Keep p.15 open. Start with the guest: a member's introduction permits reading on the meeting day. That is not a statement that the guest pays five sen. The separate clause on taking books away concerns members. In York the member signs and accompanies the evening guest; borrowing is for paid-up members. Name the occasion before comparing the conditions.

Then turn back to p.9. Five sen is the monthly amount in the April 1887 rules. The May cash account contains seven receipt entries, one covering May and June together. Seven entries are not seven people in the room, and the account cannot be matched to an attendance register. The York attendant's marks belong to a different record, for four evenings, not a matching set of receipts.

If time is short, leave out the exchange-rate discussion and go straight to p.25: the earlier date of the York rules is not evidence that the Japanese drafters read them. On p.26 distinguish evidence of receipt and possible reading before adoption from minutes showing the clauses were considered. Both questions need documents.
