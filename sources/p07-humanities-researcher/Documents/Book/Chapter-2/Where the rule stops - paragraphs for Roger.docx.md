# Where the rule stops

LHT-20250124-EVD-001 | 24 January 2025 | Judith Morris | For Roger Hewitt | v0.1

## Two paragraphs for Chapter 2

The secretary's invitation of 28 October 1862 allows each member to bring one companion to the Saturday reading. The member is asked to introduce that person to the attendant at the entrance. The money clause gives both members and guests the option of making an *offrande libre* for heating; the following sentence extends the welcome to those who give nothing. The instruction at the entrance concerns the companion's introduction. The offer for heating belongs to the arrangements for supporting the reading. The notice describes whom the secretary intended to welcome.¹

On 19 March 1866 the secretary writes to an attendant about a particular member with unpaid dues. The member may continue to consult books in the room. If he asks to take a volume away, the attendant is to receive the outstanding subscription before releasing it and hand the money to the treasurer with the member's name. Settlement is thus a condition of taking a book off the premises; the earlier permission remains in force for reading there. Nor does the instruction establish that the member borrowed a book.²

Attendance was probably higher under the less demanding condition. Roger, could we support any statement about actual visits here? I have left the sentence for discussion before it goes into the chapter.
