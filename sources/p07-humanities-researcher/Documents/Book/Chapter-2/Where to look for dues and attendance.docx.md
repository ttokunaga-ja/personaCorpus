# Where to look for dues and attendance

LHT-20240606-EVD-001 | 6 June 2024 | Judith Morris
For discussion with Susan Atkinson | Chapter 2

## Purpose

Susan and I need to establish whether the three boxes can support a comparison of dues and participation. The question is not simply what rate was prescribed, but how it was collected, what traces remain of people using the room, and whether exceptions changed the terms. These are separate observations, even when names or dates appear to match.

## Four checks

| Check | Where to look | What to record and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Stated rules | Box 12: rules and subscription notices | Amount, interval, due date and stated conditions; a rule is not proof of collection. |
| Evidence of payment | Box 12: collection and settlement records | Date, amount, name and whether the entry concerns an individual or a combined payment. A receipt is not proof of a visit. |
| Evidence of actual visits or use | Box 13: attendance or use records | Date, place, name and the unit recorded: person, visit or request. A name on an invitation is not itself attendance. |
| Treatment of exceptions | Box 14: remission and operating correspondence | Who authorised an exception, its period and terms, and which rule, collection or use record it might qualify. A letter is not proof of a general practice. |

## Reading across the boxes

Retain the reference and wording for each observation. Use date, name and place as leads for a possible match, recording uncertainty rather than joining entries by name alone. If a collection entry is blank, first establish its unit and coverage; do not insert a zero for attendance. Record missing material separately from an empty entry in an extant record.

Reading only the correspondence in box 14 would give useful cases of exceptions but would leave the stated rules, collection and evidence of use untested. We should therefore request all three boxes and examine each series separately before putting the observations together. Susan and I should check the catalogue descriptions and any gaps before deciding which comparisons the surviving material can support.
