# After Abe: what to ask to see

LHT-20250430-PAP-001 | v1.0 | 30 April 2025 | Judith Morris | Research note

## What stays in the comparison

Abe's questions on 29 April are a useful guide to the next enquiry. Retain the comparison in §§2–3 of *Dues and terms of entry* (LHT-20250327-CHP-001 v0.2), but keep the subject and occasion attached to each condition. The Japanese rules of April 1887 prescribe monthly dues of 5 sen and permit a guest introduced by a member to read on a meeting day; the extract does not establish that the guest pays. Borrowing outside the room is limited to members. The York rules of February 1874 prescribe 6 pence quarterly, require a member to sign for and accompany an evening guest, and reserve borrowing to paid-up members. These are comparisons of specified terms, not a ranking of accessibility or evidence of a transmission route.

At §2 p.9 the seven May receipt entries are entries, not visitors. One covers May and June together. At §3 p.15 the guest's reading and the member's borrowing should remain separate. Neither the cash account nor the four evenings in the York attendant's record supplies a comparable count of individual visits.

## First enquiry: receipt and opportunity to read

For §6 p.25, ask whether any translation, covering letter, dispatch list or acknowledgement identifies British rules sent or received before the April 1887 adoption. Request the date of receipt, the intended recipient and the source from which any translation was made. A date of composition alone would not establish when the intended recipient had the text. A dated, addressed receipt or associated correspondence could place the wording in the recipient's hands before adoption; examine the actual wording and provenance as well. Even that would establish opportunity to read, not the discussion of a clause or influence by itself.

## Second enquiry: the clauses under discussion

For §6 p.26, ask separately for minutes of the drafting and adoption meetings, together with any drafts or papers identified in them. Look for the history of consideration of each relevant clause: dues, guest reading and borrowing. Note who proposed or revised a clause and whether a source or text is identified. Minutes that merely record adoption would not by themselves show how each clause was considered. Compare any identified text with the dated evidence of receipt; do not substitute verbal resemblance for that link.

## If attendance is to be reconsidered

Ask for cash and attendance records covering the same dates, with identifiers that permit an individual receipt to be matched to an individual visit. First establish what each record counts and whether its coverage is sufficient for the proposed rate. The May account's seven receipts include one combined payment, and no matching attendance register was available. The York attendant's marks for four March evenings lack guest names and accompanying members' signatures. These observations cannot supply either a common total or a rate of visits.

Reference: notes of the discussion with Yukiko Abe, 29 April 2025; *Dues and terms of entry*, §§2–4 and §6, LHT-20250327-CHP-001 v0.2.
