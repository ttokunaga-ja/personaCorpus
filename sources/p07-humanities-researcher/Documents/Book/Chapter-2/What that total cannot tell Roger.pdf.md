# What that total cannot tell Roger

LHT-20260119-PAP-001 | 19 January 2026 | Judith Morris, Langley Historical Trust | For Roger Hewitt | v1.0

## The order of evidence in §2

Roger, the revised heading of the working Table 2.1 allows us to say what the box 12 series counts, but it does not turn a financial record into a register of attendance. The three kinds of evidence below should stay in this order in the discussion: a demand, an instruction for collection in a particular circumstance, and entries recording money received. They do not describe successive stages of a single identified person's payment.

At CR/12/A f. 023r, ll. 4–7, the secretary asks for the quarterly subscription of 1 franc 50 centimes, distinguishes the lamp offering, and says the treasurer will issue a receipt. This is a reminder and a statement of the intended acknowledgement. It does not establish that the member paid. An identifiable receipt or settled account entry would be needed for that claim.

At CR/14/A f. 186r, ll. 3–9, the secretary allows a member in arrears to consult books on the premises, then directs the attendant to receive the unpaid subscription before a book is taken away and to pass the money and name to the treasurer. This is a condition for removal, not evidence that the member was refused admission, that a book was borrowed, or that payment occurred. Its circumstances are different from those of the 1861 reminder.

The surviving receipt papers CR/12/B ff. 043r–054r do record money received. In the working table the four receipt quarters contain 42, 49, 45 and 48 entries: 184 in all. Bernard's two payments on 10 April and 8 May 1863 are two entries towards the same term (043r l. 4; 044r l. 6). Roux's single 4.50-franc remittance for three people on 9 October is one entry, without an allocation among them (049r l. 5). The figure therefore describes acts of receipt as entered, not 184 different payers or members. It also does not attach a receipt to the person addressed in 023r or to the instruction at 186r.

## Why R10 remains open

The reader's R10 asks for visit rates among paid-up members and members in arrears over a common period. To calculate them we would need an independent record of actual visits for that period; reliable links from those visits to the dated payment status of the same members; and the number eligible in each payment group, including members with no visit. The surviving material does not provide those linked observations or denominators. A payment entry is neither a person in an eligible group nor a visit. Multiple entries can concern one account, while one entry can cover several people.

The 184 entries accordingly cannot serve as either denominator for the proposed rates. The instruction at 186r supplies a condition of borrowing for one case; it is not a series of arrivals. The December attendant's report (CR/13/B f. 123r) distinguishes two requests in one visit, but does not give the reader's payment status. I propose retaining the R10 qualification in the working chapter: the evidence supports an account of specified dues and access conditions, not a comparison of attendance frequencies.
