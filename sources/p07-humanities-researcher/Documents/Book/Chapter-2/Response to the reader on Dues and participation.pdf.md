# Response to the reader on Dues and participation

LHT-20250227-PAP-001 | 27 February 2025 | Judith Morris, Langley Historical Trust | For Edward Fairclough, Ainsworth Scholarly Press | v1.0

Dear Edward,

Please pass on my thanks to the reader for the report of 21 February (ASP-2025-REV-001) on the 8,000-word Chapter 2 submitted on 12 February (LHT-20250212-CHP-001 v1.0). The section and note references below are to the revised chapter of 26 February, LHT-20250212-CHP-001 v1.1. I have made nine changes to the text or notes. The last two replies give the evidence I would need to take the chapter further.

### R01 · Unit of comparison

The reader asks whether the chapter compares members’ experiences, occurrences in correspondence or the conditions recorded by officers. In §1, opening paragraph, I now specify the recorded condition as the unit of comparison and name the four matters compared across the boxes: payment of dues, invitation of a guest, consultation in the room and removal of a book. The examples of the secretary, treasurer and attendant explain what each writer was in a position to record. A member’s appearance in a letter supplies evidence only for the occasion and purpose of that letter.

### R02 · Assessment and collection

The reader asks what would show that the amount demanded was paid. In §2, opening paragraph and note 4, I distinguish an assessment, a reminder and evidence of receipt. The secretary’s letter of 4 March 1861 (CR/12/A f. 023r, ll. 4–7) asks for the quarterly subscription of one franc fifty centimes, keeps the lamp offering separate and says the treasurer will issue a receipt. I count the letter as a reminder. A corresponding receipt or an account entry recording settlement would establish payment by the member.

### R03 · Invitation and voluntary gift

The reader asks for the words following *offrande libre* and their relation to the companion allowance. I have put both terms in the opening paragraph of §3, with the reference in note 9. In the Saturday invitation, each member may bring one companion and members and guests may give towards heating. The secretary adds « Nous accueillerons aussi ceux qui ne donneront rien » (“We shall also welcome those who give nothing”, CR/13/A f. 085r, l. 5). That sentence makes explicit that the welcome extends to guests who make no gift.

### R04 · Reading and borrowing

The reader asks to see the permission that precedes the instruction to collect arrears. In §4, opening paragraph and note 13, I give the secretary’s directions in their order on CR/14/A f. 186r. Lines 3–4 allow the member in arrears to continue consulting books on the premises. Lines 5–9 tell the attendant to collect the outstanding subscription before releasing a book for removal and to pass the money and the member’s name to the treasurer. The collection condition applies to borrowing in this case. Permission to read in the room remains in force.

### R05 · Deferment

The reader asks when the ordinary reminders were to resume and what the *dispense* did to the debt. In §2, the paragraph beginning “The account had other ways to remain open”, and in note 8, I give both directions in the committee’s decision (CR/14/A f. 173r, ll. 5–7). The treasurer is to withhold the demand before 12 February 1866 and resume ordinary reminders after the 12th. Those reminders concern the same subscription. I describe the decision as a deferment of collection because the obligation survives the interval.

### R06 · Missing leaves

The reader asks how much is missing and how much was available to copy. In §1, note 1 now gives 235 numbered positions across boxes 12–14, comprising 216 leaves available for copying and 19 missing leaves. The note locates the gaps and leaves their contents open. It directs the reader to note 2 for the ten leaves I selected in six sets in July. This gives the reader both the extent of the available material and the scope of my selection when checking where a sequence breaks off.

### R07 · Clairpré

The reader asks what connects the two notices and what the later one changes. In §5, the paragraph beginning “The notices of 4 and 6 November 1862”, and in note 18, I give the 4 November instruction to meet at ten by the stone bridge at Clairpré (CR/13/A f. 089r, ll. 2–3). The notice of 6 November opens « L’avis du 4 novembre demande une correction d’heure » (f. 090r, l. 1). It writes *Clerpré*, keeps the bridge and replaces ten with eleven. Line 5 leaves the other arrangements in force. The dated reference and the correction limited to the hour are the grounds for filing both spellings under Clairpré.

### R08 · Unidentified names

The reader asks how to retrieve an occurrence that has no identified heading. The paragraph immediately above the “Unidentified occurrences” table in §5 explains how to use its eight independent entries. Look up the spelling as written, keeping any article and accents, and go straight to the shelfmark, leaf face and body line. *Bas-Maret* takes the reader to the expected delivery at CR/13/A f. 089r, l. 7. *le Clos Nerlin* takes the reader to the temporary key-box collection point at CR/14/B f. 206r, l. 4. Neither entry sends the reader to an identified heading.

### R09 · Evidence in the conclusion

The reader asks which records warrant statements about rules, collection and actual use. The final paragraph of §6 now treats each claim in turn. A rule or an officer’s dated instruction establishes the terms for the people and occasion it covers. A receipt, completed entry or acknowledgement identifying money actually received establishes collection to the extent recorded. An attendant’s account of an arrival or an entry for a completed loan gives evidence of use. The December report in note 3 illustrates the last distinction: the attendant explains that two requests came from one visit (CR/13/B f. 123r).

### R10 · Attendance and payment

The reader asks for visit rates for paid-up members and members in arrears over a common period. In §6, the paragraph beginning “There is no basis here for comparing visit rates” explains why I have left the rates out. I would need an independent record of actual visits in that period, identifiers that link those visits to dated subscription status for the same members, and the number eligible to visit in each payment group, including non-visitors. The December attendant’s report gives an instance of use without a corresponding payment status. The March instruction gives the terms of access for a member in arrears without recording a dated arrival. The surviving papers supply neither the linked series nor the denominators. I have kept the chapter’s claim about dues and access to the terms for reading in the room and borrowing described in §4.

### R11 · Val d’Erne

The reader asks for a location and a diagram of successive handovers. The index reference I can give is *Val d’Erne*, CR/14/A f. 176r, l. 4, under that spelling in the §5 table. The preceding discussion of the folded leaf and note 20 give the grounds for reading the initial. To locate the address I would need a contemporary endorsement or description tying it to a specific place. To draw the route I would also need a record linking this item to an identified relay or onward handover. Berger is named in connection with another packet, and the instruction at f. 177r, l. 6 to leave an envelope at *la Côte aux Ifs* gives no onward destination or link to this notice. I have left both the position and the route undetermined.

Judith Morris
