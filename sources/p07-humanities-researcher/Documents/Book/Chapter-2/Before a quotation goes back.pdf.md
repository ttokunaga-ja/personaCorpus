# Before a quotation goes back

LHT-20251119-PAP-001 | 19 November 2025 | Judith Morris | To Florian Schulte and Roger Hewitt | v1.0

## The check

1. The person drafting sets down the proposed wording in its context and the shelfmark, leaf number, face and lines offered in support. Keep the existing reference visible if it is being questioned.
2. The checker opens the source independently, locates the passage and reads the adjacent lines as well as the words to be quoted. A search result in a transcription is a pointer, not the check itself.
3. Compare the two readings. Once both agree, record both names, the date, the agreed shelfmark, leaf number, face and line range, and the precise destination in the working chapter. Keep the evidence for the decision with that record.
4. Only then compare the wording of the body and its note against the agreed passage. Carry the change to the stated working draft and record when it has been made. Keep a submitted version as submitted.

## If the readings differ

Leave the reference and the wording open. Record the competing readings and where the difficulty lies; do not silently choose a leaf or return the quotation to the chapter. Reopen the source together, or ask for a further view if the available copy cannot resolve the question. Record agreement only when both can identify the relevant passage.

## Example: CR/14/A 186r

In the 17 November exercise the candidate was 185r. Florian and I reopened 186r together: lines 3–4 allow consultation in the room, while lines 5–9 address removal of a book and the subsequent handover. The condition about collecting arrears belongs to removal, not admission to the room. We checked both parts before agreeing CR/14/A f. 186r, ll. 3–9, on 17 November. The destination is the next working draft of Chapter 2 §4, note 13; incorporation remains pending in the decisions record. The submitted chapter is retained.
