# What payment might have controlled

LHT-20241016-PAP-001 | 16 October 2024 | Judith Morris | v0.1

## Two provisional paragraphs for Chapter 2

The invitation of 28 October 1862 appears to join the member's entitlement to bring one companion to a payment for heating. On the present translation of CR/13/A f. 085r, ll. 3–4, that payment reads as an admission charge attached to the invited guest. If so, the invitation describes not merely how many companions a member might bring but a financial condition on their entry. The short proof of 26 September isolates those two lines; the sentence following them needs to be checked before this reading can stand.¹

The secretary's direction to the attendant in CR/14/A f. 186r, ll. 7–9, can at present be read as requiring an indebted member to pay before entering the room. On that reading, arrears would have controlled access to reading on the premises, rather than only the terms on which a book could be taken away. The surrounding instruction and the recipient of the money need checking against the French before this is incorporated in the chapter.¹

## Note

1. Even if these readings of the conditions are sustained, neither an invitation nor an instruction to collect money establishes that anyone came or used the room. Attendance and use require independent records; see *July: what the copies can support*, LHT-20240730-PAP-001. Both paragraphs remain provisional pending Julie Perrin's comparison with the copies.
