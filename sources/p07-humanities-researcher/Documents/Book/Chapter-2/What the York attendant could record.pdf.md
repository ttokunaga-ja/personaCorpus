# What the York attendant could record

LHT-20250317-PAP-001 | v1.0 | 17 March 2025  
Judith Morris | Citation check

## 1. Two records at the door

The February 1874 rules of the York members' reading room require an evening guest to be accompanied by a member, who signs for the guest. The rule describes a procedure; it does not establish that any particular person attended. The March duty sheets are a separate attendant's record. The four evenings examined have a guest column in which the attendant made marks, but no space for a guest's name or for the accompanying member's signature. The marks should therefore be cited as the attendant's entries, not as the signatures required of members by the rule.

## 2. What can be counted

The unit visible on a sheet is a mark on an evening. The four evenings delimit the material examined; they are not four guests. Even if the marks on all four sheets were added, the result would be a sum of marks across occasions. With no names in the guest column, a returning guest cannot be distinguished from someone appearing only once. The sum cannot be converted into a number of distinct people, nor can a particular mark be matched to a person in the subscription book.

The absence of a name on this record does not show that the member failed to sign elsewhere. Conversely, the rule's requirement does not supply names missing from the attendant's sheets. A comparison with a record of receipts must identify what each record counts before any totals are placed beside each other.

## Source note

York members' reading-room rules, February 1874, provisions on evening guests and members' signatures; attendant's duty sheets, four evenings in March 1874, guest column. Compare the subscription provision and lending condition in the February rules; the sheets themselves do not identify payment status.
