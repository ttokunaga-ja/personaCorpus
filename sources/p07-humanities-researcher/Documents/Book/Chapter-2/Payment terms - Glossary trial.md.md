# One gloss for every payment — parked

3 October 2024

I tried using “contribution” throughout the paragraph on money passing through the society. It made the prose easy to follow at the price of losing who owes what to whom. I am putting that wording aside.

In CR/12/B f. 037r, the attendant carries the two francs for coal to the treasurer. The attendant is the intermediary, not necessarily the person whose money it is; the separate instruction to enter subscriptions is not evidence that these two francs settle the attendant's own dues. “Contribution” alone leaves that direction hidden.

In CR/14/A f. 182r the member says that the subscription is still outstanding and offers one franc for the stove. Calling both sums a contribution makes the offer look like a payment towards the existing obligation. The letter keeps the arrears and the offer apart.

The lamp papers add a third movement: money left over is to go back to its giver. That is not a fresh payment by a member. For the chapter, name the obligation, gift, handover or return as the context warrants, and keep the subject and recipient beside the verb. Ask Julie to test the difficult lines against the preceding and following sentences before settling the English.
