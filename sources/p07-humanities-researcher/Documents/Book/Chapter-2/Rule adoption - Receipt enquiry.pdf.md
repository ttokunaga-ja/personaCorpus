# What receipt before adoption would show

LHT-20250416-PAP-001 | 16 April 2025 | Judith Morris | For discussion with Yukiko Abe | v1.0

## A sequence is not a route

The York rules date from February 1874 and the Japanese monthly reading group's rules from April 1887. Their chronological order makes a journey of words possible in principle. It does not establish that a copy was sent, received or read. Nor does a resemblance between conditions tell us which text the Japanese drafters consulted. The comparison can stand on its own as a comparison of provisions without turning that order into an account of influence.

## Evidence of access before adoption

A dated translation naming its recipient and identifying the text translated would be useful if it existed before the April 1887 adoption. A dispatch record could identify what was sent, when and to whom; a matching receipt could establish its arrival. Together such records could make it possible to show that the recipient had the relevant text available to read before adoption. A dated translation on its own might show that someone prepared a text, but not necessarily that the people drafting the rules received it. A receipt for an unspecified parcel would not identify its contents. Each link would need to be checked against the document itself.

## Evidence of consideration

Access is not the same as consideration. Drafting or adoption minutes recording the relevant clauses, the text placed before the meeting and the discussion would give a separate basis for asking whether the British provision entered the Japanese group's deliberations. Even that evidence would have to be read with any other texts discussed and with the wording of the adopted rule; a record of a meeting alone does not prove borrowing.

## Present position

Abe's inquiry has not found a pre-adoption translation or receipt record and minutes of the relevant consideration in the material examined. The claim in the March comparison section that resemblance may be a clue to a route invites more than those papers can support. Replace it with the two distinct evidential questions above. Continue to compare the rules on their own terms: Japanese introduction for guest reading, York signature and accompaniment, and the respective conditions for a member's loan.

Source note: York rules, February 1874; Japanese monthly reading group rules, April 1887, working extract supplied by Yukiko Abe; Abe's reply of 21 March and discussion of 2 April 2025.
