# After reading with Julie

24 October 2024, 11.48 — York

Julie and I checked the transcriptions of all 32 letters against the paper copies from 10.00 to 11.20 this morning. Seven translations need changing. The direction of the money matters as much as the noun.

- **CR/12/A 023r, ll. 4–6:** “donation” should be “quarterly subscription”. The secretary reminds the member that the term has passed and asks for 1 franc 50 to go to the treasurer. The lamp offering is kept separately.
- **CR/12/B 037r, ll. 6–8:** “subscription instalment” becomes “gift for coal”. The treasurer asks the attendant to bring him the envelope containing two francs; the subscriptions are entered separately. The attendant carries the gift, not necessarily his own money.
- **CR/13/A 085r, ll. 3–5:** “admission charge” becomes “voluntary gift”. One companion per member is permitted; members and guests may offer money for heating, and the next sentence welcomes those who give nothing. I had made the invited guest's admission depend on payment. The invitation does not make that condition.
- **CR/14/A 173r, ll. 5–7:** “waiver of dues” becomes “deferment of collection”. The secretary conveys the committee's decision to the treasurer: no demand before 12 February, then ordinary reminders resume. No amount or condition of use is stated here.
- **CR/14/A 182r, ll. 4–6:** “subscription instalment” becomes “gift for the stove”. The member asks the secretary to receive one franc for that purpose; the preceding line leaves the subscription unpaid and the following line promises to bring the money on Friday.
- **CR/14/A 186r, ll. 7–9:** “charge at the door” becomes “subscription arrears before borrowing”. The secretary tells the attendant to collect the arrears from the member before a book leaves and pass them to the treasurer. Lines 3–4 already permit reading in the room; lines 5–6 address settling the account if the member wants to take a book away. I had wrongly made payment a condition of reading inside, rather than of taking a book out.
- **CR/14/B 202r, ll. 3–5:** “rebate of dues” becomes “return of the unused gift”. The attendant asks the treasurer to return the remaining 75 centimes to him so that he can give them back to the original donor; this is not a fresh payment by the member or a set-off against dues.

I will revise the parallel passages and both paragraphs of Chapter 2, recording why 085 and 186 change the earlier arguments. Keep Julie's commented version of LHT-20241017-EVD-001 v0.2 alongside the working papers. The invitation and the borrowing instruction still do not establish actual visits or use; those need independent evidence.
