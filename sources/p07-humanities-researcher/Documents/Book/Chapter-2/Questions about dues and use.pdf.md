# Questions about dues and use

LHT-20250319-PAP-001 | v1.0 | 19 March 2025  
Judith Morris | For Yukiko Abe

Dear Abe-san,

Thank you for the extracts from the April 1887 rules and the May account. I am trying to keep the subscription, a guest's consultation in the room, and a member's borrowing outside it as separate questions. Could we look for the following evidence before I use the comparison?

## 1. A guest's payment

The rule extract says that a guest introduced by a member may consult books on a regular meeting day; it gives the monthly subscription for members separately. Is there evidence that a guest was asked to make an additional payment to consult books? Another provision of the rules, a notice for a meeting, or a dated account identifying such a payment and its purpose might answer this. The absence of an amount in the extract alone does not establish that no payment was ever required.

## 2. Receipts and visits

The May account contains seven subscription receipt entries, one explicitly covering May and June. Can any of these entries be followed to an individual visit on a meeting day? A dated attendance or visitors' book, with an identifier that can be matched to the account, would be needed alongside the receipts. The account extract has no guest or visitor column. I cannot treat seven entries as seven people attending, or assign a monthly count of payers from them alone.

## 3. The British rule before adoption

Is there any trace that those adopting the Japanese rules had encountered the British rules before April 1887? A dated translation addressed to a recipient, together with a record of its dispatch or receipt, could help establish that the text was available to be read before adoption. Drafting or adoption minutes identifying discussion of the relevant clauses would be needed separately to examine whether they were considered. Similar wording, or the earlier date of the York rules, would not by itself establish either step.

I would be grateful for the locations that can be checked, including any gaps in the surviving record. A negative answer from the material already seen would be useful too.

## Sources for the questions

Abe, working extracts from the monthly reading society's April 1887 rules and May 1887 account; York members' reading-room rules, February 1874, and attendant's sheets for four evenings in March 1874.
