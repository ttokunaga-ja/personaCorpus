# Where the parallel breaks

LHT-20250403-EVD-001 | 3 April 2025 | Judith Morris | For Judith Morris | v0.2

## Conditions and records

| Question | Monthly reading group, Japan | Members' reading room, York |
|---|---|---|
| Dues in rules | Five sen monthly, April 1887 rules | Sixpence quarterly, February 1874 rules |
| Guest | Member's introduction; reading on meeting day | Member signs and accompanies guest in evening |
| Loan outside | Members only | Paid-up members only |
| Surviving record examined | May cash account: seven dues receipts, one for May and June | March attendant's sheets: guest-column marks over four evenings, no guest names or accompanying signatures |

The clauses can be compared as conditions attached to distinct acts. The Japanese guest's payment is not stated in the extract. A loan by a member is not evidence that a guest could take a book away. The seven receipts and the attendant's marks offer traces from different recording practices; neither supplies the number of individuals who used the room.

## Points to check in the chapter

In §3 replace the undifferentiated “participation” with the act and person for each clause. In §6 remove the suggestion that resemblance itself traces a route. To examine influence, look for evidence that a recipient could read the British text before adoption in April 1887 and, separately, for minutes showing consideration of the clauses. Abe has not found these in the material examined. A translation with a date and addressee and dispatch and receipt records might establish access; drafting or adoption minutes could address consideration. Neither alone establishes the whole chain.
