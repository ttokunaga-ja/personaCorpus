# When an empty entry tells us nothing

LHT-20240620-PAP-001 | 20 June 2024 | Judith Morris | For Roger Hewitt

## The problem

An apparent absence can describe several different states of the evidence. Before placing a zero in a comparison of participation, we need to know what the record was designed to register and whether the relevant record exists. The following distinctions are a proposal for the chapter, not findings about the contents of a particular missing leaf.

## A blank in a collection book

A blank beside a name or date in an extant collection record may mean that no payment was entered there. It does not by itself establish either non-payment or non-attendance. Check the headings and nearby completed entries to determine the unit and period of the column; look for a separate receipt, a combined payment or an account of an exception. Even a confirmed absence of a payment entry answers a question about the record, not whether the person entered the room.

## A day on which the room was closed

If a day has no recorded visits, first ask whether the room was supposed to be open. An opening notice, duty record or correspondence about hours may distinguish a closure from an open day without entries. A closed day supplies no opportunity for a visit at that room in the ordinary recorded hours; it should not be counted as an individual decision not to attend. The notice and the actual operation may differ, so a duty record is valuable where it survives.

## A missing leaf

A gap in leaf numbering is different from an empty field on a surviving page. Check the catalogue and adjoining numbered leaves to locate the interval and establish whether the leaf is absent, rather than merely overlooked. Record its location and source; do not invent its text or treat its unknown entries as none. Other surviving series may answer a related question, but cannot automatically reconstruct the missing one.

## Before comparison

Keep each state distinct: blank field, documented closure and missing leaf. Record the source, dates and unit, together with what additional record would help. I would not bring a table that classifies attendance from blank entries to the comparison until the unit of recording has been identified. Roger, does this distinction give enough space to retain the collection evidence without making it bear an attendance count?
