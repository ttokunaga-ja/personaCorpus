# Two requests I cannot substantiate

LHT-20250224-PAP-001 | 24 February 2025 | Judith Morris | For Roger Hewitt | v0.1

Roger, I can make the distinctions requested in the body and notes, but these two points in ASP-2025-REV-001 would take a different set of records. I suggest answering each with the strongest statement I can retain in the chapter and specifying what would allow me to go further.

## R10 · Attendance by payment status

The rate comparison needs a common period and an independent record of actual arrivals during it. There would need to be an identifier stable enough to join each arrival to the same person's dated subscription record. The two groups would also need denominators, including members who did not visit. A list of names on receipts gives some payments but no population of eligible readers. Grouping the names on those receipts and comparing how often they appear would measure the surviving payment records.

The best example for the present chapter is the secretary's instruction in CR/14/A f. 186r, ll. 1–9. An indebted member is allowed to consult books in the room. If he asks to take one out, the attendant is to collect the outstanding subscription before releasing it and pass the amount with the member's name to the treasurer. That name is part of a proposed accounting handover. It does not identify a dated arrival we can join to the account. Even the attendant's account of two requests made on a single visit in CR/13/B f. 123r supplies no corresponding payment status. In §6 I can distinguish the two forms of access and say what a record of a visit looks like. There is no basis for estimating either group's rate.

## R11 · Position and onward delivery

For a map, I would first need a contemporary address endorsement or a description that ties the written form to a specific location. For the line of delivery I would also need something naming the relay or recording the successive handovers. These are independent questions: a known destination would still leave the intervening stages open.

At CR/14/A f. 176r, l. 4, the address on the forwarding copy reads “Val d’Erne”. In January we compared the stroke crossing the fold with the V of “Vu” in l. 2 and the B of “Berger” in l. 8. The reading is good enough to find the passage again, with the fold noted. Mention of Berger in connection with another packet does not make him a relay for this notice. Nor does the treasurer's later instruction about leaving an envelope at la Côte aux Ifs (f. 177r, l. 6) identify the next stage for the item addressed on f. 176r. In §5 I would cite “Val d’Erne”, CR/14/A f. 176r, l. 4, as an independent entry under that spelling with the heading reference blank.

## Form of the response

For R10, cite the terms on the leaf and state which records, identifiers and denominators are absent. For R11, give the index spelling, reference and grounds for the reading, with the need for a contemporary location and evidence of the onward handover. I would leave both the table of rates and the route figure out.
