# Counting names from receipts — parked

The tempting method was to take each name on a receipt as a participant and enter the count in the comparison. I cannot do that. A treasurer might receive contributions together and put the name of the person delivering the money on the receipt: one name could cover several payers. Conversely the same name may recur in separate receipts, whether for instalments or repeated transactions. Counting lines would then inflate the number of people even before asking whether they came to the room.

Keep the receipt as a record of a transaction, with date, stated amount, name as written and any indication of whose money it represents. Do not split a combined sum without an accompanying breakdown; do not merge repeated names without further identification. Compare these entries with rules on collection and, separately, with records of attendance or use. Until there is evidence from that latter side, the attendance rows of the comparison cannot be filled from receipts. This is a problem with the inference, not a reason to discard the receipts.
