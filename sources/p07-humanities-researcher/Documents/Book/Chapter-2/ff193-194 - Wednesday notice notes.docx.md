# The Wednesday change in the margin

LHT-20251030-EVD-001 | 30 October 2025 | Judith Morris | To Roger Hewitt | v0.1

## What the two leaves can support

Julie and I looked again this morning at the two reading copies of the Wednesday notice. CR/14/B f. 193r is the printed notice of the regular Wednesday opening. The paper copy on f. 194r carries an addition in another hand concerning a change of attendant for that day. The addition belongs to the particular occasion; it does not by itself revise the regular notice on 193r.

On 194r the pencil crosses the printed line. At two points the strokes meet; part of the added stroke can be followed, but the overlapping portion cannot be separated from the type. The diagram marks those points, while the complete second page of the copy shows where the addition sits in relation to the upper notice. The darker display merges the marks and does not resolve them.

## Use in the chapter

| Reference | What can be cited | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| CR/14/B f. 193r, copy p. 1 | The printed notice of regular Wednesday opening. | A notice is an arrangement, not a count of people who came. |
| CR/14/B f. 194r, copy p. 2 | A separate hand records an attendant change for one day. | The crossing cannot be separated securely; do not derive a handover time from it. |

I suggest citing the two leaves separately if we use them together: the first for the ordinary opening, the second for an alteration on an occasion. Neither gives us an actual attendance figure. We should not fill the crossing by inference from the printed notice or use a presumed time of handover as evidence in the chapter.
