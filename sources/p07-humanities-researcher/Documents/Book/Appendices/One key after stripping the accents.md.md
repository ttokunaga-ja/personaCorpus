# One key after stripping the accents

14 January 2025 — before the table with Stefan

Tried a provisional key in lower case, stripping accents, leading articles and hyphens.

| On the leaf | Key | What the paper says |
|---|---|---|
| Clairpré — CR/13/A f. 089r, l. 3 | `clairpre` | The notice of 4 November calls members to the stone bridge at ten. |
| Clerpré — CR/13/A f. 090r, l. 3 | `clerpre` | The notice of 6 November refers back to the notice of the 4th and moves the meeting at the same bridge from ten to eleven. |
| le Clos Nerlin — CR/14/B f. 206r, l. 4 | `clos nerlin` | Temporary place for receiving the keys for that session. |

The two bridge notices still fall under different keys. The change of hour in the second notice is what connects them to one another. I would miss that by asking the key for exact matches.

Looking for near matches brings the opposite trouble. Once I take off the article in `le Clos Nerlin`, looking on the remaining first word `clos` also retrieves « le volet qui donne sur le clos » (206v, l. 6). That's an instruction about a shutter, with no reference to where the keys are to be collected. Even on the back of the same leaf, it tells me nothing about the location of the temporary handover.

Stop the automatic merge. Keep what is written on each leaf, with the line beside it. Tomorrow, open both sources for any proposed link and see whether one actually refers to the other or whether the terms of the handover connect them. Leave the source spelling searchable on its own where I can't make that link.
