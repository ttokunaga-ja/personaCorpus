# Which entries can be counted?

Document: LHT-20260305-EVD-001 v0.1  
Date: 5 March 2026  
Author: Judith Morris  
For: working note for Appendix table B

## Question

Table B should let a reader return from the decision to the passage that supports it. For table A, the unit is an entry recording an actual receipt of subscription money, dated by receipt. A request, instruction or gift does not become a subscription receipt because a familiar word occurs in it. These examples compare decisions; they are not extra entries to add to A.

| Source | What the passage records | Treatment | Detail on which the decision turns |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR/12/A 023r ll. 1–7 | The secretary asks a member to pay the quarterly subscription to the treasurer. | Exclude. | Payment is requested after the deadline; no notice of payment has yet been received. A future receipt is not a recorded receipt. |
| CR/12/B 037r ll. 4–8 | An envelope containing two francs offered for coal is to be handed over; subscriptions received are to be entered separately. | Exclude. | The money is an offering for coal, expressly distinguished from subscriptions. The instruction is not a receipt entry. |
| CR/14/A 186r ll. 2–9 | An unpaid subscription is to be collected before a book leaves the room and passed to the treasurer. | Exclude. | The instruction is conditional on a request to take a book out; it does not record collection. Reading in the room is allowed. |
| CR/12/B 043r l. 4 | Marguerite Bernard, 10 April 1863, 0.50 franc, acompte. | Include as one receipt. | The dated receipt records money received as an instalment. |
| CR/12/B 044r l. 6 | Bernard, 8 May 1863, 1.00 franc, balance on the same account, referring to the earlier receipt. | Include as a second receipt. | The later dated receipt records a further payment; the reference connects the account but does not erase the second receipt. |
| CR/12/B 049r l. 5 | Étienne Roux brings 4.50 francs for three people on 9 October 1863. | Include as one receipt. | The source records one handover; it does not allocate individual amounts among the three. |

## What would change the decision?

For 023r, a dated receipt showing the requested subscription was paid would supply a countable event. For 037r, evidence that the money was a subscription rather than the coal offering would be needed; the passage says otherwise. For 186r, a dated record of the money actually collected would be needed. For each Bernard entry, evidence that the dated sum was merely due rather than received would remove the basis for counting it. For Roux, separately recorded handovers, rather than an inferred division among three people, would be needed to count more than one receipt.

Read the surrounding action and purpose before deciding what “remise”, “cotisation” or “reçu” is doing. A word on its own cannot settle the count.
