# How to cite an unsettled place

LHT-20250117-PAP-001 | 17 January 2025 | Judith Morris | For Susan Atkinson and Florian Schulte | v1.0

## From the index to the paper

Susan and Florian, use the January place list to find the passage before using a heading in a note. Start with the spelling on the leaf, and take the full shelfmark, face and line from that occurrence. On the copy, count lines from the beginning of the body, leaving out the date and salutation. Read either side of the name far enough to see whether the writer is giving a destination, describing where something is to come from, or naming a point at which a person should wait. Put that function in the note. A landmark does useful work within an instruction even when its position cannot be recovered.

## When there is a heading to follow

For a note on the assembly at Clairpré, give both original spellings and both references: “Clairpré”, CR/13/A f. 089r, l. 3 (4 November 1862), and “Clerpré”, CR/13/A f. 090r, l. 3 (6 November). The instruction on f. 089r puts the meeting beside the stone bridge at ten. The first line of f. 090r calls for a correction to the notice of 4 November; ll. 2–5 keep the meeting by the bridge, move it to eleven and leave the other arrangements in force. This is the evidence for following the Clairpré heading from the Clerpré occurrence. Retain the cited form “Clerpré” when transcribing the later notice. The position of the bridge remains outside the claim made by the reference.

## When the heading reference is empty

“Bas-Maret”, CR/13/A f. 089r, l. 7, is in the phrase « Le courrier de Bas-Maret ». Cite it as the stated origin of the delivery expected at the assembly. Search for Bas-Maret itself and compare the shelfmark and line to select the independent entry. Following the Clairpré heading for it would lose the distinction between the expected delivery's point of departure and the place at which it was to arrive.

“le Clos Nerlin”, CR/14/B f. 206r, l. 4, appears in the sentence naming a provisional point for collecting the keys. Search with the article and check the line. The earlier notice gives Brévalette as the deposit for the key box (f. 205r, ll. 4–5). The later notice corrects an opening hour and assigns a temporary collection point. These details let us describe what each attendant was asked to do. The two notices give neither an address nor a route linking the points.

## Returning a correction

The January list has 46 occurrences, 38 with a match and eight with their own entry and an empty heading reference. If you find a better reading, send me the full reference and the words that support it. Preserve the form already recorded so that we can return to the original passage. A change to a heading needs documentary grounds for linking the occurrences; a geographical identification needs evidence of where the place was. The place list records neither a count of settlements nor a count of people.
