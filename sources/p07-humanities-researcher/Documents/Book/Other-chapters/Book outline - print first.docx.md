# Provincial reading societies: working outline

LHT-20240425-OUT-001 | 24 April 2024 | Judith Morris
Circulation: Roger Hewitt | Version 0.1

This order follows the movement of printed matter into provincial reading societies and then considers the institutions that held and used it. For discussion with Roger: can the institutional sections carry enough of the comparison, or am I assuming too much from the route by which a book reached a society?

## 1. Provincial print

Establish the local publishing context and the kinds of printed matter available to societies. Distinguish evidence of production from evidence that a particular society acquired a title.

## 2. Acquisition and distribution of books

Follow orders, suppliers and surviving indications of distribution. Compare the routes by which books entered collections, keeping a destination on a circulation list distinct from a documented meeting.

## 3. Collections and use

Examine catalogues and surviving requests for books and newspapers. What can an inventory tell us about a collection, and what separate traces are needed to say that an item was used?

## 4. The reading room

Consider the premises and the arrangements for reading there: opening notices, the room itself and the people responsible for it. A notice gives an opportunity to attend, not a count of those who came.

## 5. Committees and members

Use committee correspondence and membership records to describe administration. Subscription amounts and their collection belong here as one part of the running of a society. Membership lists may indicate who was entitled to use a room; they do not by themselves establish who actually came to a gathering.

## 6. Comparison between regions

Bring the cases together around print routes, collections and administration. State where the surviving records are too uneven for a direct comparison. The unresolved question is how to recover actual attendance rather than treating the existence of a list as its answer.
