# Questions for the six chapters

LHT-20240429-PAP-001 | 29 April 2024 | Judith Morris | For Roger Hewitt

The revised order places the comparison of operation before the history of supply. Each question below needs a defined unit and a record suited to it; the presence of a name in one series should not be made to answer every question about that person.

## 1. Scope and sources

What is the unit of comparison: a society, its reading room, or a place receiving circulated books? Rules identifying the body that administers a society and notices identifying a place and occasion for reading will help establish the unit. A shared address alone is not enough to join a destination to a meeting. Describe differences in survival before selecting cases.

## 2. Dues and participation

How did subscription terms relate to the possibility of participation, and what can we actually observe of use? Put the stated amount and interval in rules or notices beside receipts or collection accounts, then look separately for attendance or use records. Compare dates and names only where their identification is defensible. Letters about exemption or remission may qualify the rule or its application. A payment is evidence of payment, not automatically of a visit; an absence of a payment entry is not automatically non-attendance.

## 3. Rooms and opening hours

When and under what arrangements could people use a room? Opening notices, duty records and correspondence about hours may establish provision and changes to it. Actual use requires its own traces. A longer opening period is an opportunity, not a demonstrated increase in visits.

## 4. Print and supply

How did books and other printed matter reach the societies? Orders, supplier correspondence, catalogues and records of distribution can be read together, with the distinction between acquisition, destination and use kept visible. This is a history of supply, not a proxy for attendance.

## 5. Committee correspondence

How were decisions and exceptions communicated and put into effect? Read committee letters alongside the rules and the records of the officers who applied them. A request or instruction is not on its own proof that the resulting practice was uniform.

## 6. Limits of comparison

Which contrasts can the surviving evidence bear, and where must the comparison stop? Record the coverage and unit of each series, missing material and uncertain identifications. Compare like observations where possible; identify an unanswerable question rather than turning a gap into a zero.
