# Where the committee trail stops

LHT-20260807-EVD-001 | 7 August 2026 | Judith Morris | For Susan Atkinson | v1.0

## Committee correspondence

Susan, would you check the box list against the passages below before I close the chapter's account of how a decision reached the person expected to carry it out? I can follow some instructions from the secretary to the treasurer or attendant. The letter's author, its addressee and the body said to have decided are three separate entries in my notes.

| Passage | Writer and recipient | What the chapter can say | What I would need from the minutes or a separate record of the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| CR/14/A f.173r, ll. 5–7 | Secretary acting for the committee to the treasurer | The secretary conveys a deferment of collection to 12 February 1866, with ordinary reminders to resume after that date. | The committee's recorded decision, including the terms it adopted. The letter gives the instruction as transmitted. |
| CR/14/A f.158r, 19 March 1868 | Secretary to the room attendant | The sheet is now to go by way of the secretary. The attendant is told to date its receipt and return it. | A minute or retained copy recording whether the committee authorised the change of route. The instruction establishes the route ordered in the letter. |
| CR/14/B ff.193r–194r, notices of 6 October 1868 | Secretary to members. A separate hand adds an attendant change on f.194r. | The printed notice gives the regular Wednesday hours. The addition concerns one Wednesday. The handover time remains unreadable. | A record identifying who approved the change of attendant, if approval was recorded. The notice and addition do not identify a committee decision. |

The receipt on f.157v gives an example of the attendant acknowledging a sheet for transmission. Keep that evidence under receipt. It supplies neither the wording nor the date of any resolution.
