# 10 July — decisions at the table

York, 10.00–11.25 BST. Judith Morris and Roger Hewitt.

We started with the sentences and worked back through all 63 old notes. For 1–54 I found and read the relevant faces in my three bundles of paper reading copies. Keep those numbers in the next version so Roger can find the passage we had open. In the table I want the author's page and the face I opened in separate columns. When there is only an author's page, leave the date of reading the leaf empty.

## What comes out of the chapter

Take out the nine claims and their notes, 55–63. The trail for those stops at Delmas (55–57), Lenoir (58–60) or Hartmann (61–63). I can follow what each author says, but I have not read the original evidence for those sentences. The two passages we put side by side are worth keeping in view while I revise:

- f.088r gives the attendant a way to record guests apart from the members who presented them. The claim at 58 needs the guests' actual names in both books and dates of admission.
- f.199r makes the journal a basis for a decision after the October trial. It supplies none of the winter duty dates the sentence at 62 would need.

Roger stopped me at 126 and 217. The catalogue puts them in the surviving ranges and may help Camille find them. Leave both in the unread column. The other seven are candidates in missing ranges.

## My next two jobs

Finish the note-by-note cross-reference while the marked copy is to hand. Keep a separate enquiry sheet for Camille, one question per dropped claim. For 126 and 217 ask about the leaves available to consult. For the seven missing candidates ask about references to other copies. Bring Roger the new opening with the wording cut back to the 54 passages, even if the archive enquiry is still open.
