# Two requests on one visit

LHT-20250616-PAP-001 | 16 June 2025 | Judith Morris | For Roger Hewitt | v0.1

## What the attendant wanted the secretary to count

In his letter of 5 December 1865 the evening attendant returns the register to the secretary and explains the last requests before closing.

> Avant la fermeture, une même personne a demandé un journal puis un livre. J’ai inscrit ces deux demandes sur deux lignes, dans leur ordre, parce que les objets remis ne sont pas les mêmes. Je vous prie cependant de ne compter là qu’une seule venue : le lecteur est resté dans la salle entre les deux demandes.

The first line is for the newspaper, the second for the book. The attendant gives the reason for keeping two lines and then supplies his own correction to the count: one visit, since the reader stayed in the room between requests. He also says that there is a difficult name on another line and that he will check it separately. I would leave that line unjoined.

## Chapter 1, note 27

Roger, I would keep this as an example of a particular way of making and counting entries on one evening. The letter explains the two entries it refers to. For an estimate of visits from the register as a whole, I would first open the register at other evenings, identify what the columns record and see how that attendant and others marked successive requests by someone who remained in the room. If there is no way to recognise repeat requests consistently across evenings, leave the visit estimate out.

Source: Archives de Saint-Aubin-sur-Rive, CR/13/B f.123r, evening attendant to secretary, 5 December 1865, consultation copy.
