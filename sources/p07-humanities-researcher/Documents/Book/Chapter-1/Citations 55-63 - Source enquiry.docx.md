# What would bring the nine back

LHT-20250714-EVD-001 | 14 July 2025 | Judith Morris | v0.1  
For Roger Hewitt

## Where we stopped on 10 July

These are the nine claims we agreed to take out of the opening. The numbers are those in the marked v0.2. I can take you back to each author's page. Of the nine candidate leaves, seven are in gaps in the inventory and two are in surviving ranges that I have yet to consult. A shelfmark gives us somewhere to ask. I would put a sentence back only after we can point to a passage that supports its wording.

## §1 — Establishment

**55 · “The society at Saint-Aubin-sur-Rive was established with the approval of the town.”** Émile Delmas, *Les débuts de la salle de Saint-Aubin*, p. 41. Candidate CR/12/A f.018, missing. Look for the signed establishment decision or a signed counterpart. Read the date, the name of the approving body and the name of the society covered by the decision. If the town approved the creation of this society, I can name it as the approving authority. A decision on the use of the room would support a narrower statement about the premises. The officers’ duties in my copy of f.012r belong to the working arrangements of the society.

## §2 — Regulations and collection

**56 · “The subscription regulations took effect from the day they were posted.”** Delmas, p. 42. Candidate CR/12/A f.020, missing. I need the date on the posting copy and the commencement clause for the same set of regulations. If the clause makes the rules effective on the date of that posting, we can join those two events in the chapter. The February deadline on f.012r tells members when to pay.

**57 · “The names of those in arrears were displayed at the entrance.”** Delmas, p. 43. Candidate CR/12/B f.061, missing. Start with the collecting record to identify the names, then find the posting copy or the secretary’s direction giving the names to be displayed and specifying the entrance. Look for a dated posted list or a return that says the list was actually put up there. With the instruction alone I could write about an order to post. The completed act is the part that needs the further record.

## §3 — Guests, the courier and evening use

**58 · “The guests were subsequently registered as members.”** Camille Lenoir, *Les lecteurs du samedi*, p. 67. Candidate CR/13/A f.094, missing. Find the names actually entered in the guest book and match them to dated admission entries in the membership register. Use the presenting member or another identifying detail where a name alone would be ambiguous. The entry dates must follow the sitting. A mark in the guest column can help trace a visitor, while f.088r gives the attendant’s instructions for keeping the two lists apart. If the match holds for only one person, I can describe that admission. The sentence about recruitment would need evidence across more than that single case.

**59 · “The delivery from the meeting place formed part of the branch's activity.”** Lenoir, p. 69. Candidate CR/13/A f.098, missing. Look in the society’s records for the branch name and the person responsible for it. Then look for the dispatch or delivery record tying that branch to the particular courier expected at the bridge at Clairpré. My notices on ff.089–090 give a rendezvous and a correction of the hour. A branch record linked to that particular delivery would allow us to name the body responsible for it.

**60 · “Evening opening was a regular public day.”** Lenoir, p. 70. Candidate CR/13/B f.126, within the surviving range, unread. Start with the dates and change of attendant on both faces. Mark separately the shifts assigned and the shifts actually worked. Compare the latter with the service entries on other evenings and look for the conditions of admission. If the records show repeated openings on the same terms and access to the public, I can describe the pattern for the dates the records cover. With only a rota I could report the intended service.

## §4 — Deferment and permission

**61 · “An exemption from subscription also carried permission to borrow books outside the room.”** Friedrich Hartmann, *Lesestunden am Fluss*, p. 112. Candidate CR/14/A f.165, missing. Find the committee decision and read the operative words for remission or deferment, the beneficiary, the period covered and any clause allowing a book to leave the room. A decision granting both remission and outside borrowing to the same person for a stated period would allow an account of that case. The notice of 9 February 1866 on ff.179–180 permits reading in the room during a deferment to month end and sends any request to take out a book to the secretary.

## §5 — Thursday hours

**62 · “The room remained open until eight on Thursdays throughout the winter.”** Hartmann, p. 114. Candidate CR/14/B f.212, missing. Find dated winter notices giving the Thursday closing time and pair them with the attendant’s records of the evenings actually kept open until eight. Write down the dates before deciding how many weeks we can cover. The October instruction at ff.198–199 fixes both the hours and the month of the trial. Any winter claim would need its own dates and records of service for the span claimed.

**63 · “The committee extended the October trial.”** Hartmann, p. 115. Candidate CR/14/B f.217, within the surviving range, unread. Read both faces of any circular for a reference to the decision or to a separate enclosure. Find the adopted wording and compare it with the circular. The vote date, the signatures and the period of application must tie the decision to the October trial and establish what was extended. A dated and signed resolution could restore the committee’s decision to the chapter. Even then, the evenings actually worked would still be a question for the duty record under 62.

## Before writing to Camille

Ask whether both faces of f.126 and f.217 can be consulted and whether either has a linked paper. For the seven missing candidates, ask about former shelfmarks and contemporary duplicates or filed counterparts. If an alternative turns up, record its full reference and the passage it supplies against the old note before deciding how much of the sentence can return.
