# Two captions before I ask

LHT-20260910-EVD-001 | 10 September 2026 | Judith Morris | Personal working note for the 22 September seminar | v0.1

These two French passages help test the categories I am using for the Japanese and York material. Use the first to explain what one receipt means and the second to distinguish a general borrowing condition from permission for an individual removal. Keep both with the explanation of method.

## A receipt against the same account

**Archives de Saint-Aubin-sur-Rive, CR/12/B f.043r, l.4, receipt no. 4.** 10 April 1863, Marguerite Bernard, 0.50 franc, “Acompte du terme d’avril à juin.” The balance of 1.00 franc was received on 8 May. The entry at f.044r l.6, no. 20, refers back to no. 4 for the same account. I need the cross-reference in my notes even if I ask to show only 043r. It explains why the two payments contribute two entries to Table 2.1 without establishing two payers.

Short caption if the copy stays out: “Bernard’s April instalment and May balance are two receipts against one account (CR/12/B 043r l.4 and 044r l.6).”

## Permission for one book

**Archives de Saint-Aubin-sur-Rive, CR/14/A f.184r, LA-042, body ll.2–4, 16 May 1867.** The secretary conveys permission “pour cette fois” to let one book out, asks the attendant to seek its return the following Thursday and leaves the unpaid account with the treasurer. The dated instruction records what was authorised on this occasion. It supplies neither a general rule for unpaid members nor a record that the book was returned. Cite the recto for the instruction. The acknowledgment on the verso concerns receipt of the note.

Short caption if the copy stays out: “The committee authorised one removal on this occasion and asked for return the following Thursday (CR/14/A 184r, LA-042).”
