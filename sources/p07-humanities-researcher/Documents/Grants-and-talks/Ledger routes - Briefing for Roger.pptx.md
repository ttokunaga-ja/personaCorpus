## Slide 2 — Evidence table

# What the nine books yield

| Unit checked on 25 June | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Register books / faces examined | 9 / 9 | One selected face from each book |
| Entries | 117 | One original detail line is one record |
| Destination identified | 103 | The destination can be placed from the entry and its remarks |
| Destination not identified | 14 | Original spelling and location retained for further checking |

The 103 and 14 partition the 117 records. An empty destination field need not be unidentified: MRC/08 f.11r l.4 says « remis au dépôt de Valperche » in the remark. MRC/04 f.21r l.4 gives only « le préposé », without an affiliation.

**Speaker notes:** 103 is a count of records whose destinations we could identify. It is not a count of routes, journeys, distinct people or towns. A return, a planned return and a handover must be read as different acts; the same destination may recur. The 14 are not missing from the denominator.

## Slide 3 — Figure and caption

# One title; an interrupted trail

Figure, chronological sequence rather than a geographic map:

- 5 May 1865: Valperche → Salle de Brévaux. Solid arrow, handover recorded, MRC/02 f.18r l.7.
- 12 May: « B. » — stop mark, destination unidentified, MRC/02 f.18r l.8. No line beyond it.
- 19 May: Dépôt de Rochemont — separate box, independent receipt, MRC/03 f.9v l.3; no connecting line.

Caption: *Les Veillées du jeudi*. The shared title and a week's interval do not establish that the same copy passed between these entries. No copy number or sender connects the 19 May receipt to the earlier entry.

**Speaker notes:** Even a legible destination would not itself establish the missing transfer. Keep the break visible when discussing what the register actually records.
