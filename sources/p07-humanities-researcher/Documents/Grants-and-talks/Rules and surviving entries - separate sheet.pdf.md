# Rules and surviving entries

LHT-20250423-PAP-002 | v1.0 | 23 April 2025 | Judith Morris | Separate sheet for the comparative seminar, 29 April

## Conditions in the rules

The two examples are a monthly reading society in Japan and a members' reading room in York. The table identifies a condition and the occasion to which it applies; it does not establish how often the condition was used in practice.

| Question | Japan: April 1887, rules of the monthly reading society | York: February 1874, rules of the members' reading room | Scope of comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dues | Monthly dues of 5 sen. | Quarterly dues of 6 pence. | The stated amounts and payment periods can be compared as rules. Neither amount alone establishes the relative burden on a household. |
| Guests reading in the room | A member introduces a guest to read on a regular meeting day. The extract does not establish a payment by the guest. | A member signs for and accompanies a guest in the evening. | Compare the member's part in a guest's access, keeping the meeting day and evening occasions distinct. The Japanese extract does not say that the guest pays the member's dues. |
| Borrowing outside the room | Restricted to members. | Restricted to members whose dues are paid. | Both clauses concern members taking books away. The York rule explicitly includes payment; the Japanese extract does not establish that additional condition. |

## Surviving entries: separate observations

| Case and date | Document and unit recorded | What it permits us to say | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan, May 1887 | Cash account: seven entries for dues received. One entry covers both May and June. | Seven receipt entries occur in the extract; the combined payment remains one entry. | Seven visitors, seven separate monthly payers, or attendance on a meeting day. The account cannot be matched to an attendance register. |
| York, March 1874 | Attendant's record for four evenings, with marks in a guest column. | The attendant recorded marks for guests on the four evenings examined. | Distinct guest identities or an individual match to the dues book: the guest column has no names and the accompanying member's signature is not in this record. The separate rule requires a signature. |

The receipt entry and the attendant's mark record different acts. They are not a common measure of use and are not added together. Neither series supplies a rate of attendance.

Source: Yukiko Abe's working extracts of the April 1887 rules and May cash account; Judith Morris's notes on the February 1874 York rules and March attendant's record.
