## Evidence and outstanding checks

The correction in Chapter 2 has two parts. The receipt lines (CR/12/B ff.043r–054r) can be counted by date of receipt. A balance paid on a later date produces a second entry for the same account, as on ff.043r–044r. Nothing in that correction supplies an attendance denominator. For the two July paragraphs, the final figure in the date on f.184r agrees with the 7 of “17” in the same hand. The day given, Thursday 16 May, fits 1867. The 17 May endorsement on f.184v dates receipt of the instruction. The January 1866 deferment and the March 1866 instruction on arrears stay in their respective paragraphs.

For Chapter 5, I can follow transmission on CR/14/A f.158r, where the secretary writes to the attendant and requires the date of receipt to be added. This is evidence of the instruction sent. I still need to establish whether the committee took a decision about the change. The unread faces are CR/14/A ff.161r and 164v and CR/14/B ff.211r and 216r. Susan has the list of places to check. A separate search for the decision is needed even if a clearer view of a letter is possible.

For Chapter 6, the April 1887 rules and the February 1874 York rules support a comparison of provisions for guest reading and borrowing. For a claim of direct influence I would need to show that the recipient had access to the relevant wording before adoption and that the clause was considered when the rule was drawn up. The dues account and the attendant's book give different sorts of observation and cannot supply either of those links.
