# Slide 2

Layout: Two columns

Title: Two openings

Left: Amounts first
- Japan: five sen per month; annualised, 60 sen.
- York: sixpence per quarter; annualised, 24 pence.
- Ask what the rates prescribed.

Right: At the door first
- Japan: a member introduces a guest to read on a meeting day.
- York: a member signs and accompanies an evening guest.
- Ask whose action each rule describes.

Speaker notes: In the trial with Roger, showing the annual amounts first invited a reading of the figures as a ranking of household burdens. Payment timing, concessions and household resources are not aligned. The figures are provisions, not a scale of ease of participation. Use the door first.

# Slide 3

Layout: Evidence table

Title: Conditions are attached to acts

| Question | Japan: April 1887 rules | York: February 1874 rules |
|---|---|---|
| Guest | Member's introduction; reading on meeting day | Member signs and accompanies an evening guest |
| Loan outside | Members only | Paid-up members only |
| Dues | Five sen monthly | Sixpence quarterly |

Speaker notes: Give each clause its subject. No guest payment is specified in the Japanese extract. The York paid-up condition is not to be supplied in the Japanese loan clause.

# Slide 4

Layout: Two columns

Title: What the surviving records count

Left: Japan, May 1887 cash account
- Seven receipts of dues.
- One entry covers May and June.
- Not a list of visitors or a count of May payers.

Right: York, March 1874 attendant's sheets
- Guest-column marks across four evenings.
- No guest names or accompanying signatures.
- Cannot link individuals to the dues book.

Speaker notes: Do not total these together. Receipt and attendant's mark have different recording purposes; neither gives a count of individual room users.

# Slide 5

Layout: Figure and caption

Title: What would connect the rules?

Figure: Four boxes in order: “1874 York rules” → “dated translation / dispatch and receipt before April 1887” → “recipient able to read the text” → “drafting or adoption minutes: clauses considered”. The two central links are labelled “not established in material examined”.

Caption: Chronology and similar wording do not establish receipt or consideration.

Speaker notes: Separate access from consideration. A dated translation with addressee and matching dispatch and receipt could support access; minutes recording the clauses discussed would address consideration. Ask what survives, not whether similarity alone proves a route.
