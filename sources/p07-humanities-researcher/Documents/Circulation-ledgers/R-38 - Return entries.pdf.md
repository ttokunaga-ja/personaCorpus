# When the return is only pencilled in
LHT-20260617-PAP-001 · v0.1 · 17 June 2026
Judith Morris · For Susan Atkinson

## What the entries permit

The number beside an entry can help us follow a volume between a return and a later handover; it is not itself an entry recording either event. In MRC/06 f.14r, R-38 accompanies the cancelled expectation at line 5, the receipt at line 7 and the later handover at line 8. The title is Le jardin familier throughout. On MRC/07 f.22v, R-71 similarly appears with a receipt at line 5 and a subsequent handover at line 6. These are distinct lines, not a single aggregated circulation record.

| Kind of statement | Entry supported for the working route | What remains on hold |
|---|---|---|
| Number correspondence | R-38 is written at MRC/06 f.14r ll.5, 7 and 8; R-71 at MRC/07 f.22v ll.5–6. | A number alone does not prove a movement or fill an unrecorded interval. |
| Expected return | MRC/06 l.5 records an expectation of return on 15 April, subsequently struck through. Retain it as a cancelled plan. | Do not add a completed return on 15 April. The cancellation alone does not establish what happened on that date. |
| Receipt | MRC/06 l.7 records R-38 received back at Valperche on 17 April; MRC/07 l.5 records R-71 received back at Brévaux. | Neither entry supplies the whole preceding journey. |
| Next handover | MRC/06 l.8 records R-38 handed to Rochemont on 19 April; MRC/07 l.6 records R-71 handed to Rochemont. | Do not infer intermediate stops or a complete route. |

If the struck-through expectation at l.5 is treated as an accomplished movement as well as the receipt at l.7, the two lines would be counted as two completed returns for R-38. The evidence states a cancelled expectation and a receipt on 17 April; it does not establish whether an arrival occurred on the expected date. Keep the two lines distinct without turning the first into a second receipt.

## Counting the paper

Count each original detail line once, including a cancelled plan or a line in a column shared by loans and returns. Classify what it says separately from the count. The number of recorded lines is neither the number of completed movements nor the number of people. A return and a later handover may both be actual entries, but the number connecting them does not supply unrecorded legs of a journey.
