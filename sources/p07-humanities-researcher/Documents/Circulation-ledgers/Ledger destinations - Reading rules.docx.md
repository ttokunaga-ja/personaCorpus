# What counts as a destination?
LHT-20260603-EVD-001 · v0.1 · 3 June 2026
Prepared by Judith Morris · For Susan Atkinson and Isabelle Chevalier

## Working rule before the nine registers are compared

Take one original entry line as one record, even where loan and return share a column. Copy the date, title and destination as written, together with the register, folio, side and line. Preserve the spelling and abbreviations; a separate field may carry an identified destination and the reason for the identification. An empty destination field is not automatically an unknown destination if the same line's note names the recipient. Record that note as the evidence.

A place named as the point of departure is not itself the recipient of that handover. Equally, a place to which an item is to be returned is not evidence that it arrived there. Distinguish an intended return from an entry recording actual receipt. A cancelled intention remains legible in the transcription but cannot supply a movement.

| Example on a line | What can be entered | What cannot be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Destination: Salle de Brévaux; note: handed over from Valperche | Salle de Brévaux is the destination; Valperche is the stated origin of this handover | A continuous journey before or after this entry |
| Destination blank; note: return to Valperche next Thursday | A return is planned to Valperche | That it was received there; the blank recipient cannot be filled as an accomplished delivery |

## Index and limits

Keep the exact form on the line alongside any proposed identification. If the note and recipient column do not identify a destination, leave the identification open and retain the location so that the entry can be checked again. A title or a date close to another entry is not enough to establish that the same copy moved between them.

The ledger index is separate from the indexes of the letters and subscription receipts. A shared name or spelling is a reason to inspect the entries, not to merge the people. The total will count records, not people, places or routes.
