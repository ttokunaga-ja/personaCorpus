# Montclair: a reader’s life in the ledgers

LHT-20240801-OUT-001 · 1 August 2024 · Judith Morris

For discussion with Roger Hewitt · v0.1

## Working proposition

Could the nine Montclair ledgers give us a short account of how one person borrowed? I should like to begin with what a reader took out, see whether the books record a change in what that person borrowed, and end at a point the records themselves give us. I have no one in mind yet. The first visit ought to tell me whether there is a name worth following.

## 1. First appearance of the name

Open with the earliest entry I can find for the name. Put the book, the date and the name together so the reader can see what I am starting from. Copy the heading with the entry. If the clerk entered the name, I shall need to explain what the clerk was recording before describing this as the reader’s first loan.

## 2. The name in another volume

Look for the next appearance in a different volume. Set the two entries side by side with their dates and spellings. There may be something to say about a change of reading if the second entry concerns a different book. I would need to know what the name is doing in each entry and what, apart from the spelling, lets me follow it from one book to the next.

## 3. Last return

Try ending with the last recorded return I can place with the reader. The book and date would give the ending something firmer than the point at which the name disappears. If I find only a date when the book was due back, leave the ending open.

## For Roger

Is one reader a workable scale for this, even if there are long gaps? I could bring back one opening and one possible second appearance for us to look at before deciding whether there is a chapter in it.
