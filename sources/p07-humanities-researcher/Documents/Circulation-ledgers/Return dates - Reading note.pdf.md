# Return dates — Reading note

LHT-20240815-PAP-001 · 15 August 2024 · Judith Morris  
For Susan Atkinson · v1.0

## What the bureau recorded

Susan, I want to keep two dates on the worksheet when the bureau provides both. In *Retours au bureau*, number 31, *Le jardin des lectures*, has 9 March 1864 under « Attendu le ». The cell under « Repris le » is intact and empty. Enter 9 March as the expected date and leave the actual return date blank. We would need a dated return entry to place an actual return of 31 in the sequence.

Number 32, *Lectures du soir*, on the line below, shows how an actual return is entered. Its expected date is 10 March 1864 and its « Repris le » is 12 March 1864. Both dates belong to 32. Carry the number along with the dates when making a note from the copy, so that the dated entry stays with the right book.

Source note — Montclair Reading Archive, MRC/06, *Retours au bureau*, f.4v, upper two entries in consultation copy MRA-20240807-COP-005.

## Looking back from a new handover

1. In MRC/07, record the « N° de garde », the description as entered, the date in « Remis à nouveau le » and the destination. Keep the folio with the entry.
2. Look up that storage number in the bureau's returns and compare the object descriptions. Check that the numbering is comparable in the two books before treating a repeated number as a link. With only the title in common, or with a different description under the same number, keep the connection open.
3. Look for an entry with a « Repris le » date earlier than the new handover. If the cell is empty, or there are several possible entries and we cannot tell which belongs to the item, leave the antecedent return unresolved. Record the folio and date of any candidate return beside the handover reference.

The pairing to check first is 32, *Lectures du soir*. In MRC/07 it was handed out again on 15 March 1864 to Salle de Brévaux. The dated return of the same number and title on 12 March in MRC/06 is a candidate for the preceding entry. For that link, we still need to compare the numbering in the two books. For the handover itself, the next enquiry is the receiving record at Brévaux. The lender's entry gives us a date and item to look for there.

Source note — Montclair Reading Archive, MRC/07, *Nouvelles remises*, f.3v, n° 32 in consultation copy MRA-20240807-COP-006. Compare MRC/06, f.4v, n° 32.
