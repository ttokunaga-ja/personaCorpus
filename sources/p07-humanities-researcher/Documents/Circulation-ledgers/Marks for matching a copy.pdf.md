# Marks for matching a copy

LHT-20240816-PAP-001 · 16 August 2024 · Judith Morris  
For discussion with Roger Hewitt · v0.1

## Two entries under one title

In the dispatch book both entries read *Le foyer du dimanche*. On 26 March 1864 the lender entered Salle de Brévaux under « À l’attention de » and put an A in a circle in the mark column. On 29 March the addressee was Cabinet de lecture and the mark was a B in a square. A search run on the title alone will retrieve both. « Expédié le » dates the outgoing action. To date receipt I would need an entry made at the destination.

Source note — Montclair Reading Archive, MRC/09, *Envois*, f.4r, consultation copy MRA-20240807-COP-008. The marks are in the last column of the excerpt.

## Proposed order of comparison

I would copy each mark as a letter and a shape, then look for that mark or a corresponding identifying number on the receipt. If a number is used, check whose number it is and whether it refers to the same package. Then check that the receiving place agrees with the addressee and that the receipt date fits the dispatch date. Keep both dates and their original headings in the notes. A letter with the wrong enclosure shape, or a date earlier than dispatch, needs checking before the entries can be joined.

If a Brévaux receipt for *Le foyer du dimanche* turns up with no package mark, record it as a possible lead and leave the identity of the copy unresolved. Even an address and a plausible date would leave us without a way to distinguish this outgoing package from another parcel carrying that title. The question for the next visit is whether the receiving side's records can be searched by the marks on f.4r.
