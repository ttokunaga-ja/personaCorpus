# If we find the receiving book

LHT-20260629-PAP-001 | 29 June 2026 | Judith Morris | For discussion with Isabelle Chevalier and Susan Atkinson | v1.0

## The question to take back to the records

On 25 June we could identify destinations in 103 of the 117 entries examined across nine register books. Fourteen remain unidentified. The unit is an entry, not a person or a route. We agreed to leave the complete intercity movement diagram unpublished while we look for the receiving evidence. The working figure for *Les Veillées du jeudi* makes the problem visible without filling the gap.

## A destination and a connection are separate questions

On 5 May 1865 MRC/02 f.18r l.7 records a handover from Valperche to Salle de Brévaux. The next entry for the title, 12 May, is l.8: its destination is written « B. ». To return a proposed expansion of B. to the list of candidates, we need a contemporary form of the destination and a receiving entry corresponding to that 12 May handover. A modern resemblance in a catalogue alone would not establish either the reading or receipt.

MRC/03 f.9v l.3 records a receipt at Dépôt de Rochemont on 19 May. It has neither a copy number nor an identified sender. Even if a receiving book establishes where the 12 May entry went, connecting it to this independent receipt requires further evidence that the same copy was involved: a matching copy number or a continuous record of handovers across the interval. The common title and seven days between entries are insufficient.

## Next check

Isabelle will look for the receiving-side record; Susan will look for catalogue descriptions that may illuminate the ambiguous destinations. Please note the exact wording and shelfmark even if either search is negative. Until we have replies, I shall index « B. » under its original spelling only and leave the line broken. The 5 May handover and 19 May receipt can each be discussed on their own evidence; neither supplies the missing link.
