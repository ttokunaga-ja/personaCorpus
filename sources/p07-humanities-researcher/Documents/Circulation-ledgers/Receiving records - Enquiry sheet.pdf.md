# Receiving records

LHT-20240820-PAP-001 · v1.0  
20 August 2024 · Judith Morris  
Desk copy for the next Montclair enquiry

## Two starting points for one handover

The consultation copy puts an outgoing entry and an incoming entry on successive pages. They come from separate volumes and concern different items. Use each to test which record to call for on the other side.

| From the sending deposit · MRC/02, f. 3v | From the receiving deposit · MRC/03, f. 2v |
|---|---|
| **Entry in hand.** In *Sorties du dépôt*, the entry dated 11 March 1864 gives *Les saisons* and names Salle de Brévaux under « À remettre à ». That heading tells me where to ask about delivery. | **Entry in hand.** In *Entrées au dépôt*, the entry for *Le jardin familier* gives 22 March 1864 under « Reçu le ». « Provenance » is blank. I can start from the recorded receipt date, with the origin still unknown. |
| **Ask at the receiving end.** Where would a receipt for Salle de Brévaux be kept? Look for the date of receipt and a mark identifying the booklet. Check the mark against any corresponding outgoing record for *Les saisons*. Record the receiving date separately from the date on f. 3v. | **Work back to the sender.** Where might the sending side's dispatch copy be kept? Use the title and recorded receipt date to look for a candidate, then check who sent it and what on the dispatch copy identifies this particular item and its destination. |
| **Leave open if:** the receipt gives the title but there is no booklet mark that can be compared. | **Leave open if:** a dispatch names a sender but the details cannot tie that dispatch to this receipt. Keep « Provenance » blank in the transcription. |

## At the desk

For a candidate record, take down the shelfmark, leaf, date as written and any mark, together with the heading under which it appears. This will let me return to the particular entry when comparing a receipt with a dispatch copy.
