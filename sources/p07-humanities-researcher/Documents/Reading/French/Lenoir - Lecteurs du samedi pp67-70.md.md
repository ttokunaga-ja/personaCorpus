# Lenoir — names at the door

Camille Lenoir, *Les lecteurs du samedi*, pp. 67–70. 12 June 2025.

**p. 67 · Where would I see someone become a member?** Lenoir connects separate entry as a guest with subsequent admission. In the secretary’s circular for the Saturday sitting on 8 November 1862, one companion may come with each member. I can read the postscript on CR/13/A f.088r: the attendant is to keep a separate list, enter the presenting member beside each visitor and leave the visitors out of the membership count. It gives me the instruction but no names actually entered that Saturday. CR/13/A f.094 is a candidate in the missing range. I need a named entry in the visitor list and a dated admission for the same person in the membership register.

**p. 69 · The stone bridge.** In Lenoir the delivery associated with the meeting point becomes branch business. On f.089r, 4 November, the secretary asks members to gather at ten by the stone bridge at Clairpré and expects a courier from Bas-Maret there. On f.090r, 6 November, the same bridge appears at ‘Clerpré’. The correction says eleven instead of ten and leaves the rest of the notice in force. I can cite the intended meeting place and the change of hour. The name of the branch, the officer responsible for it and a record connecting it to the expected courier are still wanted. The proposed reference, CR/13/A f.098, is in the gap.

**p. 70 · Who was the evening for?** Lenoir reads an evening duty schedule as evidence of regular days open to the public. CR/13/B f.126 is within the surviving range and I have neither face in my copies. Check the dates and whose duty is entered, then look for several evenings actually opened on the same terms. Even a completed duty entry would still leave the question of who was admitted. Keep that question open until I have the terms of entry as well as the dates of use.
