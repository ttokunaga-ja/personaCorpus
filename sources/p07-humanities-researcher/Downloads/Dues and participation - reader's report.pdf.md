# Dues and participation

Revision and point-by-point response requested by 28 February 2025.

## Reader's report

The chapter makes good use of short runs of correspondence to show how the society kept track of its obligations and arranged access to reading. I would ask for revision. Some distinctions that are recoverable from the notes need to be visible in the argument, especially where the discussion moves from one kind of record to another. The last two points concern possible extensions. Please give the evidential limit in the response if the relevant records cannot support them.

### R01 · Unit of comparison (§1)

The opening moves between the member addressed, the occurrence in the correspondence and the condition described. Give the object of comparison in the opening paragraph. Does an example stand for a member's experience or for a condition that an officer recorded? This matters when drawing a comparison across the three boxes.

### R02 · Assessment and collection (§2)

The amount in the reminder at CR/12/A f. 023r belongs to a demand. Make the distinction between the obligation, the demand and an act of collection explicit at the start of the section. What would allow the reader to claim that this member actually paid the quarterly subscription?

### R03 · Invitation and voluntary gift (§3)

Quote or translate what immediately follows *offrande libre* in CR/13/A f. 085r. It would clarify whether the welcome extended to guests who gave nothing. Set this beside the allowance of one companion per member so that the terms of the invitation are visible in one place.

### R04 · Reading and borrowing (§4)

Give the preceding permission in CR/14/A f. 186r before discussing what the attendant is to collect. The reader should be able to see from the two parts of the instruction whether the arrears affected consultation in the room, removal of the book, or both.

### R05 · Deferment (§2)

Bring the operative dates in CR/14/A f. 173r into the explanation of *dispense*. Say when ordinary reminders are to resume and make plain how a time limit on collection differs from cancellation of the subscription.

### R06 · Missing leaves (§1)

Add a note specifying the extent of the missing material and the range the archive made available for copying. Distinguish availability from the author's selection of copies. This would give the reader a firmer basis for interpreting where a sequence of letters stops or fails to supply an outcome.

### R07 · Clairpré (§5)

In the paired example, show the words that connect the later notice to the earlier one. Cite both leaves and explain just what the second notice changes. The stone bridge and the resemblance of the names are weaker evidence than a reference from one instruction to the other.

### R08 · Unidentified names (§5)

Show the reader how to recover one or two of the eight independent occurrences from the index. Give the original spelling and leaf and line, and indicate what to do when there is no identified heading to follow.

### R09 · Conclusion (§6)

In the last paragraph, distinguish what would warrant a statement about a rule, what would establish a collection act and what would show actual use. The evidence for participation becomes harder to assess when these claims are brought together under a single conclusion.

### R10 · Attendance and payment (§6)

Can the author compare the visit rates of members whose subscriptions were paid and members in arrears? A comparison for a stated period, with the population eligible to visit specified for each payment group, would put the relationship between dues and attendance on a more precise footing.

### R11 · Val d’Erne (§5)

Please locate Val d’Erne geographically and show in a diagram the route by which the item addressed there was passed on. A distinction between its position and successive points of handover would help a reader assess the importance of the place to the society's arrangements.
