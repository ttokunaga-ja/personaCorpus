# Short intake annotation guide

Document ID: MDR-STI-20240513-GD-01-R02  
Issued: July 18, 2024  
Author: Vanessa Nguyen  
For: Fatima Rahman and intake text readers

## Purpose and reading scope

Assign one of the twelve intent categories only when the received body supports one intent. Read the whole body, including a request before a closing sentence. Mark the words identifying the requested action and its object, or the words that identify a supplied document or reported problem. The reading view contains the matching ID and received body; subject, channel, routing tag, outcome and later forwarding comments are not evidence. Do not infer an action from the destination or from what usually happens next.

## Categories and adjacent distinctions

| Category | Include when the body requests or supplies | Example | Distinguish from |
|---|---|---|---|
| New work request | New work or service is requested | “Please install a replacement unit.” | Quote request asks for a price, not the work itself. |
| Quote request | A price or estimate is requested | “Could you estimate the cost of replacing the unit?” | New work request asks for the work to be done. |
| Schedule check | An existing or proposed appointment time is sought or confirmed | “What time is the visit on Thursday?” | Reschedule asks to change the time. |
| Reschedule | A visit or appointment is to be moved | “Move the visit to Thursday.” | Schedule check asks about the time without requesting a change. |
| Cancel request | An existing visit or request is to be cancelled | “Cancel the Friday visit.” | Reschedule moves it to another time. |
| Job status | Progress or completion of work is requested | “Has the repair been completed?” | Schedule check asks when a visit is scheduled. |
| Send document | The sender provides a document | “Here is the specification you requested.” | Request document asks the recipient to send one. |
| Request document | A document is requested from the recipient | “Could you send the invoice again?” | Send document provides a document; Invoice question asks about its contents. |
| Correct record | A specified recorded item is to be corrected | “Please correct the address on my record to 18 Oak Street.” | Report defect concerns a faulty item rather than a record. |
| Access issue | Difficulty accessing an account or service is reported | “I cannot sign in to my account.” | Report defect concerns a fault in an item or service rather than access. |
| Report defect | A fault or malfunction is reported | “The replacement unit stops after a minute.” | Access issue concerns inability to get access. |
| Invoice question | A question about an invoice or its charges is raised | “Why is the inspection charge on this invoice twice?” | Request document asks for a copy; Correct record asks for a specified record correction. |

## Paired boundary examples

Each row contrasts the words that establish a single intent with a similar body that leaves the action or its object unresolved. The category applies only to the first example; do not infer a gold label for the held example.

| Boundary | Enough to label | Hold | Difference in evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schedule | “Move the visit to Thursday.” — Reschedule | “Thursday visit?” | “Move” specifies a change to the visit; the question names a visit and day but does not establish whether the sender wants to check or change it. |
| Cancellation | “Cancel the Friday visit.” — Cancel request | “Can you do something about the Friday visit?” | “Cancel” specifies the requested action; “do something” does not say whether to cancel, move or take another action. |
| Document / invoice | “Could you send the invoice again?” — Request document | “Could you take care of the invoice again?” | “Send the invoice” requests a document; “take care of” does not identify the action. |
| Correction | “Please correct the address on my record.” — Correct record | “Please correct this on my record.” | The first identifies the recorded item as the address; “this” does not identify the item in the body. |
| Progress | “Checking on the progress of the repair.” — Job status | “Checking on the repair.” | “Progress” asks about the work's status; the second names the same repair but does not state what is being checked. |
| Two requests | “Please cancel the Friday visit. Thanks for looking into this.” — Cancel request | “Please cancel the Friday visit and send the invoice.” | The first has one request and a closing; the second contains two distinct requests with no stated priority, so one intent cannot be selected. |

## Recording and adjudication

Each reader independently records a first candidate and the supporting words for each ID. Compare both candidates and both marked phrases with the body. Agreement on a candidate is not enough: the phrases must support the same single intent. If candidates differ, or if matching candidates lack sufficient support, bring the body and both readings to adjudication. Record the evidence and decision without using information outside the reading view. A closing sentence does not erase an earlier request, and two equally expressed requests do not become one intent by choosing whichever appears last.

Use the disposition field to record Hold when the text does not establish one intent, including an unspecified action or object, or equally ranked distinct requests. Leave the gold-label field empty for Hold; retain the first-candidate fields as reading history. Hold is a disposition, not a thirteenth category. An empty gold-label field without a disposition does not itself document a Hold decision.

## July 18 reread

Vanessa and Fatima brought together their independent rereads and comparison records for all 4,800 bodies, including the May pilot, and checked the remaining boundary cases against the received text. Of these, 126 have disposition Hold and an empty gold-label field: 32 came from matching first candidates and 94 from differing first candidates. The remaining 4,674 have text-supported gold labels and are candidates for a later evaluation partition. They are not themselves a fixed evaluation partition. Keep the 126 in the accounting but outside the scoring population; do not fill their labels by inference.
