# December review workload findings

Document ID: MDR-CA-20241219-RM-01-R01  
Issued: December 19, 2024  
Author: Vanessa Nguyen  
To: Michael Serrano and Brandon Romero

## Comparison

Brandon and I replayed 600 lab intake items in the same arrival order, 200 on each of December 4–6. The same 90 items required review in both runs. Each day started with an empty queue at 09:00 PST. We assigned one reviewer, served items in arrival order and allowed three minutes from start to completion. The first run allowed continuous review through 17:00. The second paused review from 12:00 to 13:00 while intake continued. The three minutes per item were fixed for the exercise.

Wait to start is start minus arrival. Time to completion is completion minus arrival. Outstanding at a given minute is the number of review items that have arrived through that minute less the number completed through that minute. An item in review stays in the count until completion.

## Results by replay date

The review count and work minutes apply to both runs. The last two columns describe the lunch-pause run. All clock times are PST.

| Replay date | Review items | Work (min) | Open at 13:00 | Last lunch completion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| December 4 | 28 | 84 | 10 | 13:30 |
| December 5 | 32 | 96 | 18 | 13:54 |
| December 6 | 30 | 90 | 14 | 13:42 |

All three days ended with zero outstanding at 17:00 under both conditions. With continuous review, the maximum outstanding count was two on each day and the longest wait to start was two minutes. With the lunch pause, December 5 reached 18 at 12:58 and held there at 13:00. At restart, 25 review items had arrived and seven had finished. The first completion after restart was QD-287 at 13:03, when the count fell to 17.

Across the three replay days, the longest wait to start was 55 minutes and the longest arrival-to-completion time was 58 minutes, both in the lunch-pause run. QD-287 illustrates both values. It arrived at 12:05, started at 13:00 and completed at 13:03.

## Staffing decision

At the December 18 lab meeting we withdrew the proposal to set staffing from the daily average alone. The average of 30 reviews and 90 work minutes describes the volume. The 13:00 balances show the concentration of unfinished work even on days that clear by closing. We agreed to keep the current intake process and reviewer assignments. A later staffing comparison needs an acceptable wait to start, clear responsibility for work during cover and at handback, and a replay with a different arrival pattern.
