# Intake reply paired readings

Document ID: MDR-STI-20241114-RM-01-R01  
Issued: November 14, 2024  
Author: Vanessa Nguyen  
Prepared for: Fatima Rahman

Fatima, for each pair please read the action and its target from the original on the left, then locate the words that would support the same reading in the excerpt on the right. For the excerpt Emily and I removed the first candidate quote line and everything below it. We chose a provisional start line by hand where the boundary was unclear. Any current text after that line is absent from the excerpt.

## Q27 — Which appointment is moving?

| Original | After cut |
|---|---|
| Could we move that one to Apr 26?<br>\> My appointment is on Apr 23. | Could we move that one to Apr 26? |

The line-initial marker gives a clear boundary. In the original, “move” is the current action, and “that one” picks out the appointment on Apr 23 from the quoted line. “To Apr 26” gives the requested destination date. The excerpt still supports a move to Apr 26. Its only reference to the appointment being moved is “that one,” whose antecedent was cut. Record this as loss of the source date only.

## Q91 — Where does the current reply resume?

| Original | After cut |
|---|---|
| Ignore my cancellation below.<br>As I wrote<br>earlier, please cancel my appointment.<br>Please send me a receipt. | Ignore my cancellation below. |

The attribution “As I wrote earlier” wraps across lines, so the trial uses the beginning of “As I wrote” as a provisional cut. In the original, the first sentence withdraws the cancellation quoted below it. The final sentence returns to the current reply. “Please send me” supplies the current action and “a receipt” is its object. The excerpt preserves the withdrawal and loses the instruction to send the receipt. The word “cancellation” identifies the instruction being withdrawn. The requested action and its object are indeterminate in the excerpt.

## Q54 — Cancellation with its target intact

| Original | After cut |
|---|---|
| Please cancel my Apr 29 appointment.<br>\> I booked an appointment for Apr 29. | Please cancel my Apr 29 appointment. |

The quote marker gives a clear start. Both sides contain “Please cancel” and “my Apr 29 appointment” in the current reply, which supports the same cancellation and identifies the same appointment after the cut.

## Points to take into the date check

Q27 and Q54 have the same kind of visible boundary. In Q27 the older line supplies the date needed to identify the target, whereas in Q54 the current sentence already supplies it. On the rescheduling sheet, check the expression that points back to the source date separately from the words that give the requested date. Keep the cut readings with the comparison and retain the received bodies for reference.
