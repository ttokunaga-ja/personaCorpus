# Quote stripping: reading risk

Document ID: MDR-STI-20241119-RM-01-R01  
Issued: November 19, 2024  
Author: Vanessa Nguyen  
To: Emily Chen  
Status: Discussion draft

## Question

Can we shorten a received reply by removing its earlier wording and still tell what the sender is asking us to do and what the request refers to? I want to resolve that question before we consider a shorter body for any comparison.

## What we read

We took 120 replies from the 934-document development partition, once per reading ID, where the received body showed both a present reply and earlier wording. Of the 120, 86 had clear quote boundaries and 34 unclear boundaries. We read the full body alongside an excerpt ending just before the first candidate quote line. For unclear boundaries we chose the most plausible starting line by hand. Words that returned to the current reply below that line were still cut. We kept the existing labels and predictions out of the reading.

## What the shorter body loses

Twelve replies kept the request to reschedule and the desired date but lost the date of the appointment being moved. On November 18, Fatima and I checked the words that tie the current reply to the source date row by row. In Q27, “Could we move that one to Apr 26?” still sounds complete on a quick read. Only the quoted line tells us that “that one” is the Apr 23 appointment. Leaving a recognizable action in the excerpt can hide the loss of its target.

Q91 gives us a different reason to drop the rule. The sender withdraws an older cancellation and asks for a receipt after the older wording. The cut removes that receipt request, leaving only the withdrawal. Reading the word “cancellation” in the excerpt cannot recover what the sender wants now.

## Working recommendation

Take uniform quote stripping out of the candidate conditions and keep the full received body available. Keep these excerpts as records of what the trial removed. The existing labels remain the reference.
