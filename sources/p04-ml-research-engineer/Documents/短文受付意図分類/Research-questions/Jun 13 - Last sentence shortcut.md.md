# Last sentence shortcut — June 13

Emily and I read 18 boundary cases at the desk today, selected from the 33 disagreements in the May pilot. They are cases for examining the procedure, not a new sample from which to estimate a rate.

We tried picking the request from the final sentence alone. In one case the body starts with a request to cancel a visit and ends “Thanks for looking into this.” The last sentence supplies a courtesy, not the requested action. Cutting to it drops the cancellation altogether.

Decision: leave the last-sentence shortcut out of the reading rules. Read the whole received body and identify the words that express the action and its object. A fixed position in the text is not a substitute for that evidence. The 18 cases show why we should not adopt the shortcut; they do not establish its error rate across the collection.
