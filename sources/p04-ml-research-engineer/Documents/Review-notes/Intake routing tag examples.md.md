# What a routing tag actually tells us

April 25, 2024 — for Fatima

The tag records a handling route, not necessarily the sender's requested action. Three ways the two can come apart:

1. An attachment specification arriving at Accounts can say “Here is the specification you requested.” The supplied document is evidence for Send document; Accounts tells us where it was routed, not that the intent is Invoice question.
2. “Could you move the visit to Thursday?” might be routed to a scheduling queue. The verb “move” supports Reschedule; the queue alone would also accommodate a simple Schedule check.
3. “Please send the invoice again” may reach Accounts. The request is for a document, so Request document is a plausible reading from the words; the department name does not establish an Invoice question.

For a reading exercise, hide the routing tag and queue name from both readers and retain them in the inventory for later reconciliation. Ask each reader to point to the words that support the action and object. Then compare what the text supplies with what the tag would have suggested. If the action cannot be recovered from the text, the tag should not silently become the answer.
