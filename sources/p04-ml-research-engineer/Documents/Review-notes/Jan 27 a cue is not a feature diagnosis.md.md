# January 27 — a cue is not a feature diagnosis

For the Discussion: Nicole and I can point to “don't cancel” and “move it to Tuesday” as evidence a reader used to distinguish a rejected action from a request. We also have the saved prediction. Those observations do not tell us which part of the input caused the linear classifier to select its label. A reader's supporting phrase is not automatically the feature responsible for a model decision.

The January exercise selected 160 development misses and compared readings of the bodies as they stood. We did not intervene on those inputs. The 41 scope readings describe a subset of that exercise; neither their count nor the readers' initial agreement is a measurement of the classifier's ability to detect negation. The other 119 need their own textual evidence.

A future controlled comparison could hold a short appointment request and its object constant while changing only whether the cancellation is rejected or requested, then record how the prediction and score change for each member of the pair. Check that each resulting body still has a defensible single reference intent before interpreting the contrast. Even then, the comparison would answer a question about those controlled inputs, not establish a general mechanism for all errors.

Keep the fixed evaluation membership, reference labels, features and April scoring untouched. This proposed comparison is separate from that result.
