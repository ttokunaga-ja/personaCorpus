# CE-Reader-2 service interval — question for Mason
BCE-CE-2026-REV-001-R01 | Jan 20, 2026 | Melissa Watkins | Internal discussion revision for Robert Nguyen and Anthony Mendoza

## Question
Robert asked whether we could remove one planned stop from the calendar each year. The routine service schedule is April and October. This revision incorporates Anthony's comments on the January 14 draft and sets out the question for Mason. Neither option has been selected.

## Evidence to date
Anthony's range check, BCE-CE-2026-LAB-002-R00, confirms that all four recent as-found and as-left deviations met the inclusive ±0.05 ng/mL band at the 5.00 ng/mL reference. Two of the four visits were supplemental; the other two were routine. Those supplemental checks narrow the intervals observed between calibrations. The results describe the reader at the visits and do not cover a year without calibration.

Separately, BCE-CE-2026-TBL-001-R00 compares active use in two equal 92-day periods: 240.0 hours from July 1 through September 30 and 292.8 hours from October 1 through December 31, 2025. The increase is 52.8 hours, or 22.0% relative to July–September. Setup, idle reservations and Mason service are excluded. The hours comparison does not measure drift and cannot establish drift at twelve months.

## Options for review
| Option | Planned routine stops per year | What four in-band visits support | What increased use leaves unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep six months | 2 | All four recorded checks were within band before and after calibration. Two supplemental visits shortened the observed gaps; the routine April and October checks remain opportunities to assess the reader. | The later 92-day period had more active hours. The next check is still needed to assess the reader. |
| Move to twelve months? | 1 | The four results are reassuring at the times checked, but the supplemental visits mean the record does not cover twelve months without calibration. | The 240.0 and 292.8 hours quantify a rise in use, not drift over a year. One fewer planned stop would leave a longer gap before the next check. |

## Question for Mason
What would we be assuming about performance between checks if we moved to twelve months, given the supplemental visits and the higher recent active use? What evidence would Mason need to advise on that interval? Please ask Mason to keep the April slot available while we review both options. We should retain the current schedule until the question is resolved.
