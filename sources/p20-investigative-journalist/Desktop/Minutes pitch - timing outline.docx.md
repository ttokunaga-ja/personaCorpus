# The clock can't tell us this

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260915-OUT-001  
Date: September 15, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford

## Opening tried

Put the fourteen 2025 requests and the two September requests in one ordered list, with the longest waits at the end. The list looks like a clean way into the question of when the public can get minutes.

## Why I stopped

The fourteen entries are completed intervals from receipt to a first substantive response; two of those central responses were notices without minutes. The two new requests have receipt times but no substantive response as of Keith's September 14, 3 p.m. check. Leave their response dates and completed durations blank. A blank is not zero, and it is not a finished wait.

Even if I calculate elapsed time from September 9 to a named cutoff, that is time still running, not the same quantity as a completed response interval. Ranking the two beside completed intervals would imply a comparison we cannot yet make. It would make a poor opening for a piece about obtaining minutes: a response may be a status notice rather than a copy.

## Better question

Separate the historical response calculation from the records needed to establish access. The Finance approval and posting trail and the work session Clerk index and circulation trail may tell us more. Even knowing what version is posted today does not tell us when or how someone could first obtain it. What record, if any, preserves that earlier route and date? Keep the proposed ranking here; do not use it as the lead.
