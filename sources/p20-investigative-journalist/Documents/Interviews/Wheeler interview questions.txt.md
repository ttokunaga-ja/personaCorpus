Wheeler — questions for January 27

Start with her role: which desk, what kinds of copies passed through it, and what she could see herself. Ask her to stop me if I mix a contract year or a road segment. Don't supply a date before she has described the document or event.

For every example, in this order:
1. Where were you, and what were you doing there?
2. What did you personally see or handle? Describe the object, marks or people without filling in what another desk did.
3. Did you see a date on a paper? Which copy, and when did you read it? Was it contemporaneous or later?
4. What are you remembering without a document in front of you? What are you inferring? What might disprove the inference?
5. What record and which office might let us check the same change, decision and work? Do you know, or is that a question for the records office?

Alder Road, FY2022, PW-22-01, Depot Gate–Linden Avenue: ask about the copy and any gate entry separately from a change-file receipt. Did she see a delivery, or did she read about one? When?

Mill Road, FY2022, PW-22-02, Stone Culvert–Pump House Lane: ask what she saw on the road and whether she could identify the work from those marks. The engineer's recommendation is conditional; who could tell us if its condition was met?

Alder Road, FY2023, PW-23-01, Depot Gate–Linden Avenue: say the fiscal year aloud. Where was she when she saw a vehicle, and what could she tell about its contents and destination? What could she not tell? Keep the CO-02 signature distinct from any earlier activity.

Quarry Road, FY2023, PW-23-03, Weighhouse Gate–Briar Street: restrict questions to the Weighhouse Gate side. Ask what she saw there and whether she saw the work itself or only traffic preparations. Keep CO-01 distinct from the view at the gate.

Mill FY2023 is a different entry from Mill FY2022, even though the endpoints match. Check which year is being discussed before using the quantity schedule.

[Crossed out] “The order was signed September 12, after the truck arrived, wasn't it?” — supplies a date and an interpretation; ask for her own account first.

Finish: read back the boundaries of what she knows, not a conclusion about approval. Ask how best to check the document trail and whether she wants to clarify a quotation's context later.
