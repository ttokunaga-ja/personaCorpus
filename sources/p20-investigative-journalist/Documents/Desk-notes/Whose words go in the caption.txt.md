Nov. 17 — after quote training with Danielle and Alicia

We played ten candidate spots. Two excerpts didn't carry enough of the exchange to tell what the answer applied to. I took both out of the draft and went back to the transcript. Keep the two out of the caption list as well unless I can recover the question and hear the passage through.

Next time I mark a quote: open the full transcript, go back to the question that prompted the answer, and start playback there. Listen through the next exchange for a correction, a qualification or a change of speaker. Mark the question and the proposed in and out points in the transcript. In the caption check the subject of the sentence against the person doing the action in the audio. Send Alicia the time range with the question still attached. Ask Danielle to read the caption against the whole exchange if a short cut changes who appears to be making the claim.

An example from my Keller notes: “The field crew judged the gate-approach subbase weak and gave it priority over the ditch-edge repair.” If I cut the sentence down to “gave it priority over the ditch-edge repair” and write “the borough” as the subject, I've shifted the decision from the field crew to the borough. The attribution needs to carry both levels: Salazar says the crew made that judgment. The planned transfer in the change order doesn't establish who made the diagnosis.
