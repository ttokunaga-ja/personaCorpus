# The road records after the change

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260901-DRF-001  
Date: September 1, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v0.8  
For: Leslie Crawford and Richard Espinoza

The dates suggest that work at Quarry began before the change order was signed, while records from two other roads show how the borough recorded decisions and quantities.

By Leslie Crawford

At the Stone Culvert end of Mill Road, where a seam bends around the drainage outlet, a borough schedule called for 840 square yards of milling. The completed measurement was 812. Both numbers describe the revised milling area under Mapleton Falls Borough's FY2023 contract for Mill Road between Stone Culvert and Pump House Lane. One number guided the layout. The other came from the book where Public Works recorded what it measured after completion.

The August 29, 2023 revised schedule called for a cut that would taper down from the full roadway width, clear the outlet and its supporting edge, then return to the scheduled width. The 840 square yards on page 63 of the initial release includes the transitions. The schedule directs the field crew to measure completed work separately. There is no area listed there for each individual part.

The completed-measurement entry at S092 is dated September 5, 2023. It lists 186 square yards at the approach, 477 around the outlet and 149 at the return. Public Works confirmed the meaning of the measurement field on August 31, 2026. The figures show a difference of 28 square yards between plan and measurement. The records give no location for that difference on the road. A photograph of the seam shows the shape at the outlet today.

## From the awards to the changes

The borough's award archive lists 18 resurfacing contracts, six in each of fiscal years 2021, 2022 and 2023. An award entry identifies the contract and the limits advertised for the road. To find out how work within those limits changed, we sought records for five combinations of year and road section. The same road name and even the same endpoints can recur in successive years. We used the contract reference as well as the endpoints to match the papers to the work.

The initial release contained 124 pages, including the signed agreements, changes and revised schedules. A later release of four paper sides included an entry slip and a traffic preparation sheet. This August the borough supplied another 93 pages. The decision register and completed-measurement book in that supplemental release answered questions the signatures and planned quantities in the initial release had left open.

## What the signature dates record

Near Depot Gate, the Alder Road change order, PW-23-01 CO-02, moves surface-course quantity toward a soft area within the contract limits. The decision register records delegated approval on September 11, 2023. The parties executed the change order on September 12, 2023. The first date identifies the approval entry at S036. The second comes from the signed order at page 46 of the initial release.

Suzanne Wheeler, a former borough procurement staff member, described being at Depot Gate on September 7, 2023. “I saw a materials truck at the Depot Gate booth. I didn't know what the truck was there for.” The entry slip from that day lists a Keller Municipal Paving Inc materials truck as received. The slip gives the vehicle category and records its entry. It leaves the use of the truck and any connection to CO-02 unestablished.

## What Keller says about the work

In a July 15, 2025 response, Raymond Salazar of Keller Municipal Paving Inc said the field crew reported pavement moving under load on Alder and gave priority to the Depot Gate end. He said Keller used partial-width milling on Mill to protect the existing drainage outlet and its supporting edge. Keller supplied a schedule for Mill with an area of 840 square yards. That schedule restates the planned area and includes both transitions.

Salazar maintained the explanations on August 28, 2026. In that follow-up he said the company could not independently verify the borough's completed measurement. For Quarry he said the field crew judged the gate-approach subbase weak and gave it priority over the ditch edge. In PW-23-03 CO-01, the changed work schedule reduces the estimated area of ditch-edge pavement repair and adds the same amount, 400 square yards, to planned localized subbase repair at the Weighhouse Gate approach. Both estimates concern the section from Weighhouse Gate to Briar Street.

Barricades at Quarry on September 28, five days before the October 3 signature, show that crews had begun the subbase repair before the change order was executed. The traffic preparation sheet places the work on the ground before the signed order.

The September 28 sheet records traffic preparation and leaves the construction-performed field blank. The October 3 signature records execution of the order. The search for a contemporaneous signed or verified daily report or start ticket connecting the changed subbase work to a day remains open.

## Which prices can be compared

A Public Works price extract covers five comparable work items in the three FY2023 sections. The original estimates sum to $500,000 before tax. The revised amounts for those same items total $582,000 before tax, a difference of $82,000, or 16.4 percent. The Alder surface line and the Quarry repair transfer are unchanged in the extract. The increases appear in the three Mill lines for the approach, the outlet area and the return.

The outlet line rises from $98,000 to $148,000. Page 4 of the extract includes a $28,000 outlet protection detail within that $148,000. Counting the detail again would overstate the revised estimate. The comparison is between priced work items on a common basis. It provides no payment total and no percentage change across the 18 awards.

The paper trail starts with those award notices. The later records make it possible to follow specific changes, provided each entry stays attached to the decision, estimate or measurement it records. On Mill the revised schedule tells the crew how to go around the outlet. The completion entry tells us what area Public Works actually measured.
