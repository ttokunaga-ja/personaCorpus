# Three changes, three explanations

Document no.: ACN-INV-20250721-DRF-001  
Date: July 21, 2025  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v0.2  
For: Leslie Crawford; reviewed by Veronica Huang, July 22

Keller Municipal Paving says its crews changed the emphasis of work on three Mapleton Falls road segments for different reasons: a soft area near a gate, an existing drainage outlet and a weak approach beneath another gate. The records available so far describe the altered work, but leave important questions about how the decisions were made and what was ultimately measured.

## Alder Road

For the Depot Gate end of Alder Road, Keller spokesman Raymond Salazar said the field crew reported that the pavement moved under load. The company said that report led it to prioritize the soft area on the gate side.

The September 12, 2023 change order for PW-23-01 redirects surface-course quantity toward that area and calls for the treatment limits to be confirmed with Public Works. A materials-truck entry at the gate five days earlier records a vehicle's receipt, not what its load was used for or when this change began. On June 12, 2025, a rectangular patch was visible from the public sidewalk; the surface alone does not reveal the old condition underneath.

## Mill Road

Salazar said Keller used a partial-width cut around the Mill Road outlet to protect the existing opening and its supporting edge. Its July 15 schedule describes an approach taper, the outlet section and a return as parts of one planned 840-square-yard area.

The Borough's August 29, 2023 revised PW-23-02 schedule also lists 840 square yards as planned and directs separate measurement of completed work. A June 12, 2025 view from the public shoulder shows the outlet and a seam curving around it, but cannot measure the cut. Keller's July 15 schedule draws on the Borough's August 29 revised schedule; both give the same planned 840 square yards, not a completed measurement or independent confirmation of the reason for the cut.

## Quarry Road

Salazar said the field crew judged the gate approach's subbase weak and decided it needed attention before the ditch-edge work. That is Keller's explanation of the priority, not an independent diagnosis in the records we have.

The signed October 3, 2023 PW-23-03 change order transfers 400 square yards in the estimates from ditch-edge pavement repair to localized subbase repair before Weighhouse Gate. A traffic staging sheet records barricades on September 28 but leaves construction work blank. On July 2, 2025, photographs from the public road showed the approach and ditch in relation to one another; they do not show the subbase or the area accepted in 2023.
