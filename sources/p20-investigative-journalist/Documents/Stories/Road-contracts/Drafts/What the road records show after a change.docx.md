# What the road records show after a change

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260901-DRF-001  
Date: September 8, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v1.0  
For: Richard Espinoza

Delegated approval, measured area and the price of changed work appear in different fields of the borough’s records. Reading those fields together shows what can be compared — and what the records do not establish.

By Leslie Crawford

[Opening photograph: Pictures/Reporting/Mill outlet from the shoulder.jpg. Use the horizontal view, keeping the shoulder, the drainage outlet and the turning seam in frame.]

Photo caption: A pavement seam curves around the drainage outlet on Mill Road near Stone Culvert on August 26, 2026. Photo by Leslie Crawford.

## A bend in the cut

At the Stone Culvert end of Mill Road, a seam in the pavement bends away from a round drainage outlet and then turns back toward the wider roadway. The outlet sits low at the shoulder, with a supporting edge that projects into the space a paving crew has to work around. The bend is visible from the public shoulder. Its dimensions in the borough's resurfacing records depend on which page you open.

A revised schedule called for 840 square yards of milling around the outlet and through the transitions on either side. Mapleton Falls Borough's completed measurement was 812 square yards. Both figures describe the milling area affected by the revision on Mill Road between Stone Culvert and Pump House Lane. The larger number belongs to the instructions used to lay out the cut. The smaller one comes from the Public Works book where the department recorded the area it measured after completion.

The photograph shows the seam as it appeared in August 2026. To find out what the borough counted in 2023, we had to follow the work from the award notice to the revised schedule and into a separate record of measurement. The award notice was easy to find. The measurement came in a supplemental release almost three years after the schedule was issued.

The Mill records are one part of a broader look at resurfacing in Mapleton Falls. On Alder Road we traced the decision to redirect surface work toward Depot Gate. On Quarry Road a change order provides a different kind of detail, showing the amount of repair the borough planned to move from one part of a job to another. Public Works also provided comparable price estimates for selected work on the three roads. The results depend on keeping the limits of the roads and the purposes of the records in view at the same time.

## From the awards to the work

The reporting began with 18 resurfacing awards in the borough's public archive, six apiece in fiscal years 2021, 2022 and 2023. The notices identify roads, section endpoints and contract references. Those details provided a way to ask for the agreements and follow what happened to particular pieces of work after award. The notices themselves give no account of how much pavement a crew eventually milled or how the borough approved a change to the work.

Matching the endpoints mattered because the same road can appear repeatedly. The borough awarded work on Alder Road between Depot Gate and Linden Avenue in both fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2023. Mill Road between Stone Culvert and Pump House Lane also appears in both years. A route name and two endpoints would match either year's award in each case. The contract reference tells the reader which set of papers belongs together. On Quarry, a bid addendum specifies Weighhouse Gate to Briar Street for the fiscal 2023 job. The stretch from Briar Street to Hill Street appears in earlier notices as a separate section.

We checked the locations from public roads, where the endpoints and features such as the Mill outlet helped orient us. The field visits also set a limit on what we could establish with our own eyes. A seam in the pavement can help place the area a schedule describes. It carries no label identifying the contract that paid for it, and the present surface gives little view of what lies underneath. We used the contract files to follow the particular changes.

Our first request sought agreements, signed change orders, requests for changes with written justification, engineering reviews, approval records and revised schedules for all 18 awards. Dorothy Hensley, the borough's public records officer, asked for priorities by fiscal year and road endpoints. Some records could be located by contract reference. Others, including revised schedules and engineering papers, might be filed under the road and its limits. Approval records could require a search by agenda subject.

We narrowed the request to five award entries, the two years on Alder, the two on Mill and the fiscal 2023 section on Quarry. The borough released 124 pages in August 2024. They included executed agreements, incorporated schedules, changes, and supporting records. An order could be read alongside the agreement whose terms it revised. A separate set of papers at the back contained approval material for multiple contracts, and there was no basis to treat it as the complete set of decisions for the roads we were following.

In March 2025, the borough supplied four more sides of paper, including a gate entry slip for Alder and an older receiving register. The distinction between receipt and decision matters in that register. For the fiscal 2022 Alder job, the entry marks the arrival of a change file at the borough. For the fiscal 2022 Mill job, it marks receipt of a set of papers for technical review. In the first release, the Mill technical recommendation has an empty approval field. Those records describe steps in handling the papers for the earlier contract, which shares the same endpoints as the Mill job at the outlet.

The first two releases gave us enough to locate the changes and to see why the dates needed more work. A date on a record has to be read beside the action recorded there. The Alder truck slip, for instance, records the booth's receipt of a vehicle. The earlier Alder receiving register records the borough's receipt of paperwork. Both are useful for finding the next record. In neither case does the date say that the borough decided to alter a specification or pay for different work.

The later request for the three fiscal 2023 jobs asked for the decision and field records alongside the orders and schedules already released. On August 22, 2026, the borough provided 93 supplemental pages, including a decision register made available electronically and a group of completed measurements collected on paper. Two entries could be checked against records from the first release. The register entry answered a question about authority on Alder. The measurement book supplied an area for Mill that the plan could only estimate.

## Who approved the change on Alder

Change Order 02 for Alder Road, under contract PW-23-01, reassigns surface-course quantity toward a soft area on the Depot Gate side. The section still runs to Linden Avenue. The order directs the contractor to use the allocation sheet to identify the area taking more surface work and the sections giving up quantity. It calls for the limits of the treatment to be confirmed with Public Works before the surface course is placed. The existing pay items and their unit prices still govern the accepted quantities.

The May 2023 agreement shows how the pieces fit. The drawings establish where the contractor is to work, and the quantity schedule establishes the items, units and prices. A written change has to identify the affected sections and pay items. For a progress payment, the contractor is to report measured quantities by item, and Public Works is to verify the measurements before payment. An order can therefore move work within the limits of the drawings while leaving the amount to be paid dependent on a later field measurement.

The signed order gives September 12, 2023, as the execution date. Page 46 of the first release shows the signatures of Eleanor Briggs, the borough's public works director, and Thomas Keller for the contractor, Keller Municipal Paving Inc. The date identifies the parties' execution of the order. We looked for the decision in the borough's register because approval and execution are separately recorded actions.

The supplemental release identifies September 11, 2023, as the date Briggs gave delegated approval for CO-02. The entry is at S036. On August 31, 2026, Briggs checked it against the decision register for Public Works. Hensley's August 31 letter confirmed the September 11, 2023, register entry and identified the September 12 signatures as a separate action under CO-02. Both dates can now be assigned to the entries that support them, with the contract and change number linking the papers.

Another record, from the booth at Depot Gate, describes what came through the gate on September 7, 2023. The slip lists a Keller materials truck and records it as received. Suzanne Wheeler, a former borough procurement staff member, recalled the same type of vehicle at that location in an August 25, 2026, interview. “I saw a materials truck at the Depot Gate booth. I didn't know what the truck was there for.”

The slip supplies the carrier and vehicle category. There is no entry for the purpose of the truck or for a delivery to CO-02. Wheeler's account reaches as far as the booth. Together, those sources describe the truck's presence; determining what it supplied would take a record that ties the materials to particular work. The decision register supplies the date of the borough's approval and leaves the truck unexplained.

Raymond Salazar, speaking for Keller on July 15, 2025, said the field crew reported that the pavement moved under load and that Keller gave the Depot Gate side priority for that reason. He stood by the explanation on August 28, 2026. The clause in the change order about a soft pavement area accounts for where the surface quantity went. For the observation about movement under load, the source is Keller.

We asked Keller how the crew compared giving that area priority with keeping the original allocation, who confirmed the limits and whether the work was described in a daily report or acceptance record. Salazar supplied no comparison or such field record and gave no name for the person who confirmed the limits. So the borough's decision and the company's account of what led the crew to favor that area remain distinct parts of the story. The order gives instructions for how to record accepted work, leaving the accepted quantity to a separate record.

## Measuring the Mill cut

The schedule for Mill Road, under PW-23-02, explains why the bend has to be read as part of a larger cut. Dated August 29, 2023, the revised schedule directs the crew to taper down from full-width milling at the approach, clear the drainage outlet and its supporting edge, and return to the scheduled roadway width once past it. Page 63 of the first release assigns 840 square yards to the whole revised area, including both transitions. The schedule gives directions for each part and a single estimate for all three.

In the same schedule, the field representative and the contractor are directed to confirm the marks before milling. If an obstruction prevents the marked cut, the schedule directs them to refer it to Public Works before moving the line. That is a practical reason to distinguish the layout from the area later entered in the book. The layout tells the crew how to preserve the outlet while following the contract. The measurement records the total after the cut has been made.

Salazar said in his July 15, 2025, reply that keeping a full-width cut around the outlet risked damage to the structure and its supporting edge. The company supplied a schedule that shows the three parts used to arrive at its 840 square yards. The area on that schedule is the same planned total as on the borough's schedule. The two documents made the layout easier to follow. The borough's instructions direct the field crew to measure the completed area separately.

Salazar reaffirmed Keller's explanation on August 28, 2026, and confirmed that 840 was a plan figure including the transitions. Asked about the completed figure, he attributed it to Public Works and said he could not independently confirm it for Keller. The company supplied no separate completed measurement. The source for the area actually measured is the borough's completed-measurement book.

The entry at S092 is dated September 5, 2023. It records 186 square yards at the approach, 477 around the outlet and 149 at the return, for a total of 812. Briggs checked the entry against the book on August 31, 2026, and Hensley's letter confirms that it is the department's completed measurement for the Stone Culvert to Pump House Lane section. That check attaches to the measurement in the book, three years after the entry was made.

Subtracting the measured total from the plan leaves 28 square yards. The book divides the measured area into three parts, but page 63 gives no planned area for any one part. There is no corresponding planned figure to subtract from the measured outlet area. We can describe the difference for the revised area as a whole. Assigning it to the ground beside the outlet, or to either transition, would require detail the schedule does not give.

That is the limit of the photograph. The outlet and the seam show why it is possible to describe three parts of a milling layout in such a short stretch. The photograph has no measurement scale, and the surface it shows is the surface we could see on August 26, 2026. The numbers come from the documents. The comparison uses square yards on both sides, with the same endpoints and the same revised area as its reference.

## Moving repair within Quarry Road

The fiscal 2023 Quarry contract runs from Weighhouse Gate to Briar Street. Under PW-23-03 CO-01, the borough planned to reduce ditch-edge pavement repair by 400 square yards and add 400 square yards of localized subbase repair at the approach to the gate. The order's schedule puts the ditch-edge estimate at 800 square yards, down from 1,200. The approach estimate goes from 600 to 1,000. Those are the allocations in the signed change order, with accepted areas to be measured separately for payment.

Salazar said on July 15, 2025, that Keller's crew judged the subbase at the approach weak and considered it a higher priority than the repair along the ditch. He maintained that account on August 28, 2026. Salazar did not supply a diagnostic record with the first reply or an additional field record with the second. The order itself shows what area the borough and contractor agreed to reassign. Its two items carry the same contract unit price, so the reduction and the addition offset each other in the order's work subtotal.

The distinction between changing an allocation and changing its price matters here. A reader tracing only the price would see that the two movements offset. A reader tracing the schedule would see a different distribution of work between the gate approach and the ditch edge. The order specifies that the original bid limits continue to apply, so this is a redistribution inside the advertised section. Establishing accepted areas would require the quantities recorded under the two pay items in the field.

## What the price lines add up to

Our first comparison of prices for Alder and Quarry in 2024 paired two original work subtotals with two revised totals. We set it aside after going back to the column labels. The original subtotals excluded materials tax. The revised totals included estimated tax of $142,000 on Alder and $168,000 on Quarry. Together those two entries accounted for the entire apparent difference of $310,000. Put the original and revised work subtotals side by side and the pair totals $5.2 million in each column. The estimated tax figures give no measure of tax actually paid.

On Alder, the underlying work subtotal was $2.4 million in both the agreement and CO-02. On Quarry it was $2.8 million in both the agreement and CO-01. The agreements and the orders use those subtotals to describe the work before tax. The orders show the tax estimate on its own line and carry it into the final column total. That is why the bottom figure on the order has to be read with the lines above it, even when the order is signed and belongs to the same contract.

For the more limited comparison here, Briggs provided an April 15, 2026, Public Works extract of five work items that could be compared on the same basis across the three fiscal 2023 road sections. For those items, before tax, the estimate rose from $500,000 to $582,000. The difference was $82,000, or 16.4 percent of the original estimate. The extract gives the two prices for each of the selected items and identifies which of the entries are included in a larger line.

On page 2, the Alder surface item shows $120,000 in both columns. The estimate for the Mill entrance on page 3 rose from $64,000 to $82,000. The outlet area on page 4 rose from $98,000 to $148,000. The return on page 5 rose from $38,000 to $52,000. On page 6 the Quarry repair reallocation remains $180,000 on both sides. The three Mill differences, $18,000, $50,000 and $14,000, account for the $82,000 increase in the five lines.

Page 4 includes an outlet protection detail of $28,000 within the revised $148,000 for work around the outlet. That detail describes part of the larger price. It has no original comparison amount of its own in the extract. Adding it again to the five independent lines would count it twice and give a revised sum of $610,000. Reading the parent line and its detail together leaves the revised sum at $582,000.

At Mill, entrance, outlet and return are useful names in both conversations, but the price extract cannot tell us why the completed area came in below the plan. It compares original and revised estimates and contains no completed quantities. Likewise, the completed measurement has no corresponding set of prices for the original and revised work. The 28 square yards and the $82,000 can each be reproduced from their respective pages. A reader would need additional records linking field quantities to accepted pay items to calculate what was payable for the work as measured.

The price extract covers selected work within the contracts. It has no spending totals for the three roads, and its five lines cannot be used to construct a rate of change for all 18 awards. The Mill outlet also appears in the area comparison, but there the two values come from the quantity schedule and the completed measurement. Applying the price percentage to the finished area would mix two different comparisons. The estimate gives dollars for selected work, and the measurement gives square yards of completed milling.

## Following the entries

A resident looking up this work can begin where we did, with the award reference and the section endpoints. Those identify the file to ask for. To see the different actions on Alder, read the September 11 approval entry against the September 12 signature page for the same order. To see the difference between an instruction to the crew and a measurement by the borough, read the planned area on page 63 beside S092. For a price comparison, the original and revised amounts have to cover the same work and carry the same tax treatment.

At Stone Culvert, that trail of records returns to the turn in the pavement. The schedule tells the crew how to approach it and how to come back to full width. The completion book tells how much milling Public Works measured across the approach, the outlet and the return. In the price extract the three portions appear as estimates with increases of their own. Keeping those documents alongside one another gives a specific account of the change at this outlet, from the layout the borough ordered to the area it recorded at completion.
