# Start with the truck — maybe not

Document no. ACN-INV-20250220-OUT-001  
Version v0.1  
Date February 20, 2025  
Prepared by Leslie Crawford

## Opening options

**Truck at the gate.** Suzanne Wheeler remembers seeing a materials truck at the Depot Gate post on September 7, 2023. The Alder FY2023 order was signed September 12. Tempting scene: a truck appears before the signature and the reader wants to know why. But she cannot identify its purpose. A vehicle at a gate is not proof that CO-02 work started that day, much less that it started without authority. Do not use the juxtaposition to imply either.

**Two signed orders.** Show readers what Alder CO-02 and Quarry CO-01 actually changed. Alder shifts surface-course quantities toward a soft area by Depot Gate; Quarry shifts work from the ditch edge to subbase repair on the approach to Weighhouse Gate. These are concrete documents, but signatures date execution and leave the decision and work trail to be established. This is stronger than a scene, though two orders alone do not answer who authorized the changes or when.

**Follow the check.** Begin with who recorded what and why the column matters. The 18 public notices identify the procurement entries, not decisions on later changes. The signed orders describe two changes. Ask what the signature, a routing receipt and a daily work record each can establish. Then bring in Wheeler's observations with the limitations beside them and show the missing decisions and logs.

## Working choice

Use the third approach. It gives the reader a way to follow the evidence rather than promising a finding the records cannot yet support. Use the two orders as examples after establishing the distinction between an executed document and the separate approval question. The earlier $310,000 comparison is out: Miguel and I found the old subtotals excluded estimated materials tax while the revised totals included it.

## Set aside / reporting still needed

Possible scene, not for the opening: “At the Depot Gate post, Wheeler saw a materials truck.” If it returns, the next sentence must say she did not know its purpose. Do not call it a truck for the changed work on this evidence.

The February 6 request asks Dorothy for decisions and work records on four entries. A date on a copy and a person's recollection are leads; we still need records tying the decision and the work to the same change. The two-year Alder and Mill entries share endpoints, so retain the fiscal year and contract reference each time.
