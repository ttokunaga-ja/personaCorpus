# The dates on the road records

Document no.: ACN-INV-20250409-DRF-001  
Version: v0.6  
Date: April 17, 2025  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
For: Richard Espinoza

Hold for reporting — evidence review May 1, 2025

## Article

**[P1]** Mapleton Falls Borough's public award archive lists 18 resurfacing contracts across fiscal years 2021 through 2023. The notices identify the award references and road limits; they do not say who approved a later change or why a change was made. That distinction matters when the same road segment appears in more than one year's files.

**[P2]** The borough's first release in August supplied 124 pages for five combinations of fiscal year and road segment. It included signed contracts, change orders and other records, but the approval-related pages did not give the desk a complete decision and field-work sequence. A request in February sought decision records and daily logs for four of those contracts. In March the borough offered three further paper records, four pages in all, while its search continued.

**[P3]** The records do show changes in what was planned. For Alder Road's FY2023 contract, PW-23-01, a change order signed September 12, 2023, reallocated surface-course quantity toward a soft area at the Depot Gate end. For Quarry Road's PW-23-03, an order signed October 3 shifted an estimated 400 square yards from ditch-edge pavement repair to localized subbase repair on the approach to Weighhouse Gate. Those signatures record execution of the orders; the documents alone do not establish when the decisions became effective or when the changed work began.

**[P4]** Suzanne Wheeler, a former borough procurement staff member, spoke on the record about what she remembered seeing and what she later read. For Alder's FY2022 file, PW-22-01, she said she had read July 11, 2022, as a gate delivery date on a copy; she did not witness that delivery. The March register records receipt of a change file on July 14. We have not obtained the July 11 copy or a log connecting it to the changed work.

**[P5]** For Mill Road's FY2022 file, PW-22-02, Wheeler recalled road markings at the Stone Culvert end on August 8, 2022. The March register records a technical review packet received for routing on August 11. An engineer's review in the first release recommends a narrower cut only if measurements protect the outlet and allow sound transitions; its approval line is blank. Neither the markings nor that recommendation establishes that the conditions were met or that changed work started on August 8.

**[P6]** For Alder FY2023, Wheeler recalled seeing a materials truck at the Depot Gate booth on September 7, 2023. She did not know its purpose. A March entry slip records a Keller Municipal Paving Inc materials truck received at the gate that day. It does not identify the load's use or connect it to CO-02. The September 12 signed order and the truck receipt are different records of different acts.

**[P7]** At Quarry's Weighhouse Gate side, Wheeler saw barricades on September 28, 2023, but did not see subbase work or paving. A traffic preparation sheet from that date records barricade placement and leaves construction work performed blank. The sheet calls this traffic preparation; it does not identify it as the subbase work reallocated in the October 3 order.

**[P8]** The distinction extends beyond the four dates. A revised Mill FY2023 schedule carries 840 square yards as a planned milling quantity; it calls for completed work to be measured separately. The schedule is not a measurement of work done; the measured completed quantity remains to be obtained. And an earlier desk comparison that suggested a $310,000 increase in the two Alder and Quarry FY2023 contracts compared pretax work subtotals with totals including estimated materials tax. On the same pretax basis, the old and revised subtotals for those two contracts were each $5.2 million combined. The tax estimates are not evidence of tax paid.

**[P9]** The remaining question is not answered by putting an earlier date to the left of a later one. The desk is seeking the decisions and logs that could identify who approved each change, when it took effect, and whether the work recorded on the ground was the work described in the order. The borough has said it is continuing the search.
