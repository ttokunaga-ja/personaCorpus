# What the pictures have to show

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260210-OUT-001  
Date: February 10, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v0.1  
For: Francesca Johnson

## Opening possibilities

For tomorrow's weekly edit, let's look again at the 18 frames with the housing repairs story in mind. One possible opening is a full view of a building, giving the reader a sense of the setting before moving to the work. That is only an option for discussion: a broad view could disclose more about where a resident lives than the story needs. We should not choose an opening until we have considered that risk together.

The other approach is to open close on a repaired part and use the caption to explain the kind of work visible. A detail can give the reader something concrete without asking the setting to carry the story. It will need a careful caption; a close-up alone does not tell us the history of the repair.

## What the images can and cannot carry

The reporting question is the wait between a request and completion. A photograph cannot show that interval. The dates and the meaning of each date have to come from the records, not from the apparent condition of a surface or fitting. We should describe only what a picture actually shows and keep the time claim in the reporting.

## For the edit

Francesca, could we compare the two approaches with Marianne and review all 18 frames for details that could narrow down a location? If a frame is too revealing, let's leave it out and consider whether a tighter view of the work would explain the relevant part without exposing the setting. The meeting is for selection and discussion, not a final publication approval.
