# What the library file actually supports

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260114-DRF-001  
Date: January 20, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v0.3  
Desk read: Richard Espinoza

Mapleton Falls Borough Library staff recommended Lanton Counter Systems’ checkout equipment because Lanton put in writing how checkout could continue while the terminals were replaced. Under its proposal, the installer would replace and test the equipment in stages, leaving at least one terminal available to patrons throughout the work.

Library head Gerald Scott gave that reason in the March 13, 2025 staff evaluation of the nine offers. A lower-priced offer from Mercerwick Library Supply included installation and testing. Its response said the installation would be arranged with library staff. The material staff reviewed did not spell out a sequence that would keep a terminal available throughout the changeover. Purchasing logged a separate sequence sheet from Lanton on February 19 under the same receipt number as Lanton’s February 7 offer.

The staff recommendation was for Lanton’s Q01 at $63,400 before tax. Mercerwick’s Q03 was $62,900, $500 less. Alderfen Access Equipment’s Q04 was $64,200. All three amounts cover four self-checkout terminals and two card printers, installation and a working demonstration, and 36 months of on-site maintenance with parts and labor. Alderfen quoted one price for the package. Its service sheet confirms coverage for parts and labor without breaking out a separate charge.

Six other offers could not be put on that price basis. One included six terminals. One provided free remote help for a year, and another priced on-site labor with parts billed separately. The remaining offers left a maintenance price, installed scope or tax basis unconfirmed. In May, Scott explained the $18,600 difference that first prompted our questions. Lanton’s two offers showed the same $44,800 equipment price. Q01 also quoted $18,600 for on-site maintenance. Q02 left maintenance unpriced.

On January 12 the Borough released 76 numbered pages, from the original specification through the Clerk’s routing jacket. The evaluation gives the staff’s reasoning. The routing slips and public agendas place the procurement on two meeting schedules. The March 18 Library Advisory Committee notice lists checkout counter equipment as item 4. The April 8 Borough Council agenda lists the staff recommendation as purchasing item 7B. The release includes the staff paper and both routing slips. It contains neither set of meeting minutes.
