# When can someone get the minutes?

Document no.: ACN-INV-20260917-PIT-001  
Date: September 17, 2026  
Prepared by: Leslie Crawford  
Version: v0.3

## Question

When can a person actually see the minutes of a public meeting, and what can the borough's records tell us about the interval between a request and access?

## The story

The clock on a request is not necessarily the clock on access. In our 2025 set of 14 Mapleton Falls Borough requests, the median time from receipt to first substantive response was 26 calendar days. This is not a median wait for minutes: ten responses supplied copies, while four gave only a status. An acknowledgment of receipt is not counted as a substantive response.

Keith and I matched the intake book MFB-PRO-20250804-001 to dispatch extract MFB-PRO-20250804-002 by request reference. The seventh and eighth elapsed times were 25 and 27 days: MIN-25-107, received May 16 and answered June 10, and MIN-25-110, received May 21 and answered June 17. Their average is 26. The June 10 Finance notice MFB-PRO-20250610-002 described an unapproved draft and enclosed no minutes; the June 17 work session notice MFB-PRO-20250617-001 referred the location question to the Clerk. Neither tells us when a member of the public could see those minutes. ACN-INV-20250806-LOG-001 contains the dates and calculation.

## What remains open

Both follow-up requests were received September 9, 2026 and are awaiting substantive responses as of this draft. Keith's ACN-INV-20260914-LOG-001 records the acknowledgments and prior notices through September 14 at 3:00 p.m. For this update I checked the received correspondence for both request references through September 17 before preparing this draft and checked the returned counter papers for 042: the only 2026 responses on hand are the 041 acknowledgment and the 042 intake slip. Neither reports a search result or supplies minutes. This is a check of our records, not proof that no record exists at the borough.

## Decision and reporting

I propose holding publication while we seek the underlying records and ask the borough how approval, posting and responses work. The 14 responses describe this set, not the entire system. The elapsed response time alone cannot establish how accessible the minutes were or whether any deadline or law was violated. We need applicable requirements, approval and availability evidence, and the borough's account before assessing either claim. For September 21, the decision is whether the reporting question merits further work, not whether the story is ready to publish.
