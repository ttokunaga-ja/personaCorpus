## Decision

Richard will carry the minutes-access pitch into the October planning slot as a proposal. He has not approved it for publication. Keith, Richard and I reviewed the September 17 pitch and the response chart against the records we have. The reporting question remains worthwhile, but we cannot yet say when a reader could actually obtain the minutes in the two cases we are following.

## What the 14 responses show

The 2025 set records the calendar time from receipt to the first substantive response for 14 requests. Keith and I matched the intake and dispatch entries by request reference on August 6. In ascending order the seventh response took 25 days and the eighth took 27, so their midpoint is 26 days. Ten responses supplied minutes; four were status notices without minutes. Both middle responses were notices, not copies. The 26 days therefore describes this set of first responses, not the time until the minutes were available to a member of the public. It does not establish how accessible the system was generally or whether any delay was unlawful.

## Two requests remain open

Both follow-up requests were received September 9. At the chart's 11:00 a.m. cutoff today neither had a substantive response. The intake confirmations establish receipt, not the outcome of a search or a date of access.

| Reference | Request and present evidence | Record still needed |
|---|---|---|
| MIN-26-041 | Finance Committee, February 13, 2025. The earlier notice said the minutes were an unapproved draft; it supplied no copy. | Approval and posting records, including evidence of when an approved copy first became available. |
| MIN-26-042 | Borough Council work session, March 4, 2025. The earlier notice said the Clerk had been asked about the location. | Clerk's index and circulation records, and the minutes or draft if held. |

## Next reporting and limits

Keith will pursue the Finance approval and posting records. I will follow the Clerk's index and circulation for the work session. We will bring our progress together on October 5. A current posting or an intake acknowledgment alone cannot date the first opportunity to read a record. We need the underlying records before deciding how to describe the relevant periods or assessing legality; we should not fill the gaps with the elapsed time on the chart. Richard can reconsider the pitch when that evidence is in hand.
