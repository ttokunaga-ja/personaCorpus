# Theresa Coleman interview — August 6, 2024

Recorded from 2:00 p.m.; duration 08:24. Leslie Crawford and Theresa Coleman speak in English. Elapsed times mark the start of each passage. Pauses and page handling are audible as indicated.

[00:00] Leslie: This is Leslie Crawford, August sixth, just after two. Could you say your name and your role for the recording?

Theresa: I'm Theresa Coleman. I'm the representative of the Parkview Court Tenants Association.

Leslie: On Thursday you said I could name you in that capacity. Is that still all right?

Theresa: Yes, you can use my name and say I'm speaking for the association.

Leslie: And separately, may I record this for my reporting and to check quotations accurately?

Theresa: Yes, for your reporting and checking the quotes. Please don't put residents' names or addresses on the recording.

Leslie: Agreed. No names, addresses or unit numbers. Thank you.

[00:41] Leslie: Your notice says a resident should contact management first. What happens if they also want to tell the association?

Theresa: They can bring the concern to us. They don't have to. We make a note of what they tell us, and sometimes they show us a paper they've kept. We don't arrange the repair by making that note.

Leslie: So your entry isn't an instruction to a worker?

Theresa: No. It's our record that a concern came to us, or that we copied information into the book. It's not a management work order.

Leslie: And someone could have spoken to management before you heard anything?

Theresa: Yes. The notice asks them to go there first. Our date won't necessarily tell you when that happened.

[01:27] Leslie: You brought a list of twenty-two. What exactly are those twenty-two?

Theresa: They're the repair concerns that reached the association and that I'm discussing with you today. I've given each request a code so we can talk about the papers without naming a household. The codes are for the requests, not for homes.

Leslie: Are you saying that's every repair request from every resident at Parkview?

Theresa: No. Reporting to us is voluntary. These are the twenty-two that came to the association and are in the material I've brought for this conversation. I can't tell you from our book how many other requests went only to management.

Leslie: So we can't use twenty-two as a count of all requests at the property.

Theresa: That's right. It's the scope of what I can explain here, not a property-wide count. [Pause.]

[02:14] Leslie: When I look at the register, what is the date beside an entry?

Theresa: It's the date of the association's entry. Usually that's when we note the concern, but a record can also be copied in later. You need the kind of entry as well as the date.

Leslie: Does the book have a separate field for the date management first received a request?

Theresa: Not in the copy I'm describing. We may be told that a resident had already called, but that doesn't make our entry date the date of that call.

Leslie: And the resident papers are separate from your book?

Theresa: Yes. These are work slips residents showed us. Not everyone has given us a slip. I can show you what is written in the fields of the ones we have, without their identifying details.

[03:16] Leslie: How many have a date in a completion field?

Theresa: Fourteen of the twenty-two have a completion date on a signed resident work slip I've seen. The signature field was filled in; I don't need to show you a person's signature to tell you that.

Leslie: You've read the field, rather than just heard that the work was done?

Theresa: Yes. For those fourteen I read the completion field on the slip. That's different from someone saying a worker came.

Leslie: And the other eight?

Theresa: I can't give you a completion date from the papers I have. Two have slips with the completion field empty. For the other six I don't have a slip in this set.

Leslie: Does that mean eight jobs were not completed?

Theresa: No. It means I can't establish a completion date from these papers. A blank field and a missing slip aren't the same thing, and neither tells us by itself whether the work was done.

[04:20] Leslie: Can we talk through the fields before we talk about individual dates? If I see a date on a slip, what should I look for?

Theresa: Look at the label beside it. One might say a visit or a measurement, another might record an appointment. The completion field has its own place. A date elsewhere on the page doesn't fill that field.

Leslie: If the signature field is filled in, does that turn a measurement date into a completion date?

Theresa: No. You still need to read what was signed and which field the date belongs to. I wouldn't carry a date over just because it appears on the same paper.

Leslie: Let me look at the two with blank completion fields. [Paper movement.] I'll use your request codes.

[05:02] Theresa: R-17 is the door brush. The slip has a visit measurement dated July twenty-ninth. The completion field is blank.

Leslie: You read “measurement” on the slip?

Theresa: Yes. It records a visit to measure. It doesn't say that the replacement was completed on that date.

Leslie: And R-20?

Theresa: That's the washbasin drain. The slip says a return visit was booked on August second. That's the date the appointment was made, not a date of completion. The completion field there is blank too.

Leslie: Do we know from that field whether the return visit happened?

Theresa: No. It records the booking. You'd need another record to say what happened afterward.

Leslie: There's a later date in the register for R-22. Is that a completion?

Theresa: No. August fifth is when the communal lighting ticket was transcribed into the association register. The original lighting ticket isn't among these resident slips. The register entry doesn't give a completion date.

Leslie: So, for R-22, you read the association's transcription entry, not a completion field on the original ticket?

Theresa: Exactly. I can't tell you the completion date from that entry.

[06:46] Leslie: For the fourteen, could I compare the date in your register with the completion field and call the difference the time management took to respond?

Theresa: I wouldn't call it that. Our book tells you when the association made its entry, not necessarily when management first got the request. You would have to find the earlier contact separately. The slips tell you what their own fields say.

Leslie: And for the eight we leave the completion date open, even though some other field has a date.

Theresa: Yes. A date in the wrong field doesn't answer that question. It may help you know what to ask for next.

[07:31] Leslie: Could you send copies of the association register and the resident slips, with names, addresses and unit numbers removed? I'd like to keep them as separate sets and use the same request codes to compare them.

Theresa: Yes. I'll prepare copies without those details. I'll keep the codes with the entries so you can see which concern corresponds to which slip. The register and the slips will be separate.

Leslie: And there are sixteen slips in what you've shown me, including the two without a completion date?

Theresa: Sixteen, yes. Fourteen with completion dates and those two with a blank completion field. The other six don't have slips in this bundle.

Leslie: Thank you. I'll come back to you if I need to check a field or a quotation.

Theresa: Please do.

[08:24] End of recording.
