## Slide 2 — Table

**Who does the reading before anyone speaks?**

| Approach | Preparation | Evidence we would hear or see |
|---|---|---|
| Instructor paraphrases first | Have a short explanation ready. The pair can start talking as soon as it is given. | We hear whether the pair can repeat the explanation. Both may use the clauses the instructor has already selected. |
| Take a show of hands for or against an extension | Put the two choices up and take the vote immediately. | We see the split in decisions. There's no record of whether a voter read a deadline, saw the blank field or simply followed someone else. |
| Pair compares separately chosen evidence | Have the memo and register open. Each person brings marked evidence from the reading and has two minutes to select and locate the entry and clause they will share. | We can hear which clause and entry each person used, what they think the evidence settles and what their partner understood. |

Speaker notes: The hands might tell us where to ask a follow-up, but a vote gives us no reason to work with. The third approach takes more time and needs a prompt both partners can read. It gives us something to respond to when the two readings differ.

## Slide 3 — Two columns

**Q5 — Seven minutes for separate readings**

| First reader | Second reader |
|---|---|
| Point to the document, clause and entry you marked. Explain what that evidence lets you decide. | Say the first reader's reason back in your own words. Point to the evidence you chose and explain what it adds or leaves open. |
| When you swap roles, restate your partner's reason before you add anything to it. | When you swap roles, check whether your partner's restatement matches what you meant. |

**Together:** Write down one thing reception can establish and one thing it still needs to check.
