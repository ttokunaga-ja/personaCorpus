# Building Proposals from Evidence — review of May practice
Fletcher Course Review Ltd
FCR-REV-2025-001 | Issued 13 May 2025 | Reviewer: Sean Desjardins | To: Heather Callahan, Elmwood Adult Learning Cooperative

## Scope and judgement
I reviewed ELM-BPE-2025-HO-003 v0.9, the 16-question practice booklet and its kit preparation notes, against the four stated aims. Q04, Q08, Q11 and Q15 are assigned to comparison under constraints, but each can be answered by locating one entry. A correct short answer to any of these does not require the learner to weigh the two routes. The observations below concern alignment of the questions with their aim; they are not findings about learner attainment.

## Questions to reconsider
**Q04 — quantity.** “What is the maximum number of kits that can be prepared in-house?” The answer is 20. Ask the learner to consider two quantities, one within and one above that limit, and explain whether each route is feasible. The print-room quantity range and other conditions must also be considered.

**Q08 — staffing.** “How many staff members are needed for in-house preparation?” The answer is two. Hold the quantity and timing constant and change the number of staff available. Ask which routes remain feasible with one person and with two, and why.

**Q11 — receipt time.** “What is the print-room order deadline for Thursday 09:00 delivery?” Naming Wednesday noon is sufficient. Compare an order received before noon with one received after it, for a quantity above the in-house limit. Require an explanation of both routes at each time, including the possibility that neither is currently available.

**Q15 — unconfirmed information.** “What number is in the count field of the Wednesday 09:00 attendance sheet?” The answer is 18. Keep that count but leave staff availability unconfirmed. Ask what can be concluded about each route, what needs checking and what action is possible before the print-room deadline. Unconfirmed availability is not the same as a confirmed shortage.

## Suggested response
Keep the numbered items and make each require a comparison supported by the preparation notes. Separate the feasibility judgement from the reason and the next action so the reader can show where a condition matters. The remaining twelve questions can continue to address their existing aims; this review does not claim an effect from a revision that has not yet been used.
