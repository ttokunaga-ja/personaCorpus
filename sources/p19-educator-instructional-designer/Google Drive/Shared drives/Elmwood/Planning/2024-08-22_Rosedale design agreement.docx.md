# What staff should bring to the decision
ELM-CWM-2024-DD-001 | v1.0 | 22 Aug 2024 | Heather Callahan

To: David Ashcroft

## Context
At 10:00 today Glenn Fletcher and I agreed the shape of Communicating in Workplace Meetings for 24 Rosedale staff. There will be six 75-minute meetings, 10:00–11:15, with six groups of four. Glenn's maintenance agenda gives us a useful distinction between an update and an item on which the chair needs a decision.

## Change and rationale
| Sequence | Practice | What learners bring forward |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the agenda | Identify what needs a decision rather than retell the update. |
| 2 | Make a 30-second contribution | State the request first, then give a relevant fact and a question the chair can put. |
| 3 | Develop a conditional proposal | State the proposed action and scope, identify the condition and change the action when it is not met. |

The four-person groups give each participant a turn to speak and listen. The final individual exercise will let us count submissions separately from proposals that meet the learning criteria. Attendance alone will not establish either count.

The Saturday appointment scenario is material for practice. Whether Rosedale actually proceeds with Saturday slots remains its own operational decision and is outside this course. We are not treating this course as a measure of customer-service performance.

## Next review
I will circulate the first lesson sequence for comment and check whether learners can distinguish the decision from the preceding repair update. We will confirm the final exercise criteria before marking and report submissions and attainment separately to Glenn.
