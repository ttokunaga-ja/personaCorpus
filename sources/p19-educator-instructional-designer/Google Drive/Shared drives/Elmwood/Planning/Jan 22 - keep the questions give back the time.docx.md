# Keep the questions, give back the time

Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-DD-001 · v1.0 · 22 Jan 2026 · Heather Callahan  
To David Ashcroft

## Context

David Ashcroft and I met from 14:00 to 14:30 on 22 January and agreed to use an 18-page equipment-loan packet in place of the 30-page autumn reading. We kept the five course aims and the four questions from the 20 January circulation draft. The shorter reading gives people time to show one another the words and entries they used to make a decision.

## Change and rationale

| Course aim | What the January packet retains |
|---|---|
| 1. Find the ordinary rule | Q2 asks for the ordinary same-day return and the words giving 16:30 in clause 1 of the preparation room notice. |
| 2. Identify when the rule applies | Q2 gives a day loan with no request for an extension. In Q5 readers use the receipt at 15:32 against the 15:45 limit to decide whether the application condition applies. |
| 3. Check what an exception requires | Q5 keeps the receipt and approval fields separate. Receipt at 15:32 meets memo clause 1. The approval field is still blank at 16:05. Under clauses 2 and 4, reception checks with the duty lead and holds the promise while checking. The later return by 09:00 on the next business day requires both conditions to be met. |
| 4. Compare instructions by issuer and scope | Q8 keeps the preparation room’s notice to borrowers and the duty lead’s memo to reception in full. The issuer, intended reader and scope need to be visible in each source so readers can explain which instruction applies to an ordinary return and which governs an extension. |
| 5. Explain a decision from a clause and a case fact | Q10 moves the clock past 16:15 with approval still unrecorded. The learner writes one sentence to the borrower giving the ordinary return and the reason, then names the supporting clauses. |

Remove the following 12 pages from the autumn booklet. Keep the two source documents, the case records, and the space for the Q8 comparison and the final explanation.

| Autumn pages | Pages removed | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| 3–4 | 2 | The standalone vocabulary repeats terms readers meet in the sources. Explain essential terms briefly where they are used. |
| 9–11 | 3 | Replace transcription with marking the clause used alongside the case fact. |
| 16–18 | 3 | The worked explanations lead to the same conclusion. The retained cases give readers space to arrive at and explain that decision. |
| 22–25 | 4 | Remove extra independent practice. The four retained questions still cover the ordinary rule, both conditions for an exception, the two sources and the reasoning at the desk. |

The lesson still takes 70 minutes. Keep the 5-minute introduction and 10-minute exit task. Independent reading moves from 40 to 20 minutes. Discussion goes from 15 to 35 minutes, including the 15 minutes of whole-group discussion we already had.

| Added time | What we agreed to do with it |
|---|---|
| 7 min · pair evidence check | Each reader brings a clause or register entry they have marked for Q5. Partners compare the entries and say each other’s reason back before deciding what the record establishes and what still needs checking. |
| 8 min · compare instructions as a group | Use Q8 to put the issuer, intended reader and scope of the notice and memo side by side. Ask which instruction governs the case and have someone show the words that support the choice. |
| 5 min · rewrite and check the reason | Return to Q10. Have readers revise the sentence to the borrower and check both the advice and the reason against the relevant clauses and the blank approval field after the deadline. |

## Next review

I’ll update the booklet and the instructor sequence together, checking the new references against the pages readers will actually use. Tristan will check the instructor materials against the sequence. Arjun will replace the 30-page copies on the Room 2 shelf with the 18-page packet by noon on 29 January. Keep one complete autumn edition with its original pagination for the December review notes.

When we next use the packet, keep an exit response with the source and clause attached to the reader’s advice. Look for a case fact that explains why that clause applies.
