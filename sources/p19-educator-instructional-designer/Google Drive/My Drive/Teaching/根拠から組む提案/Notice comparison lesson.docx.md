# Notice comparison lesson
Building Proposals from Evidence · ELM-BPE-2024-LP-002 v1.0 · 26 Jun 2024 · Heather Callahan

For Erin MacDonald · Wednesday, 10 July 2024 · 10:00–10:40 EDT · 40 minutes

## Learning aims

By the end of the session, learners should be able to support an observation sentence with one line of the doorway record, mark the cabinet explanation as a possibility and propose a comparison the person responsible for the break room could consider before deciding whether to move the notice.

On 14 June we found that five of the 14 anonymous paragraphs used the stop count as a reading count. Giving each step its own space should let us see where a learner makes that jump and ask about it before they revise.

## Teaching sequence

| Time | Lead | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00–10:05 (5 min) | Heather | Put up the 22 May midday row from the doorway briefing. Give everyone a moment to rewrite “Nine people read the notice at lunch.” Ask a volunteer to point to the time and the counted action that support the new wording. The source records nine entries coming to a complete stop at the entrance line from 12:10 to 12:30. |
| 10:05–10:13 (8 min) | Heather | Locate the present notice and the east-wall candidate on the room sketch. Put 12:10 beside the north wall and 14:40 beside the east wall. Ask what could explain different counts. Once learners name both time and position, put the same 20-minute window beside each wall. Use the option of one notice at a time on two nearby weekdays. Ask what else they would keep the same and what might still vary between days. |
| 10:13–10:25 (12 min) | Heather | Hand out the comparison worksheet. Learners write an observation, a possible effect of the cabinet and one comparison for the person responsible for the break room. Circulate. If someone proposes an immediate move, ask what they could observe first and how they would record it at both locations. |
| 10:25–10:35 (10 min) | Erin | Give out the peer check and have partners swap plans. Ask them to circle the change, underline the shared conditions and flag anything that varies on one side only. Ask, “If this plan counts stops, how would it establish whether anyone read the notice?” Each partner returns one question for the writer to answer in revision. |
| 10:35–10:40 (5 min) | Heather and Erin | Return plans to their writers. Give them time to revise a condition and finish the sentence about what their measure can establish. If a pair raises a question about remembering the notice, Erin can open the closing discussion by naming the separate measure that would require a voluntary answer. |

## Check for understanding

Read the revised observation back against the dated row. Look for the time window, the entrance line and a count of events. In the comparison, look for the north and east walls, the same window, the same notice and observation line, and a defined action. Ask the writer to point to the line that puts a limit on the conclusion. If the plan shows a count for the east wall, ask what record it came from and have the learner leave that result open.

Preparation for Erin and me: have the doorway briefing and floor sketch ready, and bring the comparison worksheet and peer check as separate sheets. Our planning options are in Notice trial options, ELM-BPE-2024-MAP-003 v1.0.
