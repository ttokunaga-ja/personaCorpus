# Break room notice: making a proposal

Building Proposals from Evidence | ELM-BPE-2024-LP-001 | v0.1 | 09 May 2024 | Heather Callahan

Preparation draft for Erin MacDonald | 45 minutes

## Learning aims

Use a count from the break room entrance to support a proposal about where to put the notice. Explain what the change would do for someone entering the room and give the person responsible for the room a clear request.

## Teaching sequence

| Elapsed time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Ask for a show of hands: should the notice be moved? Take a reason from each side before we look at any figures. Keep the two reasons on the board so we can come back to them. |
| 5–12 min | Describe where the notice is now. Ask what the person responsible for the room would want to know before agreeing to move it. |
| 12–20 min | Put up the entrance counts. Ask pairs to pick one line and say what the number in each column is meant to represent. Which number would they use in a proposal? |
| 20–28 min | Look at the route from the entrance to the notice. Ask where else the notice might go and what makes that position worth suggesting. |
| 28–40 min | Have each learner write a short paragraph to the person responsible for the break room. It should name a position, use a count from the table and explain why the suggested position might help. |
| 40–45 min | Swap paragraphs. Underline the count and circle the request. Give the writer a minute to clarify anything the reader had to ask about, then collect the paragraphs. |

## Check for understanding

When the paragraphs come back, ask a writer to point to the row they used and explain the connection between that count and the position they've suggested. If the connection is hard to follow, ask what they expect someone coming through the doorway to be able to do once the notice has moved.
