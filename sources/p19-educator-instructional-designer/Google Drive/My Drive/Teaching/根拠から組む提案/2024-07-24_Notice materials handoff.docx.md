# Notice materials handoff
Building Proposals from Evidence · ELM-BPE-2024-DD-002 v1.0 · 24 Jul 2024 · Heather Callahan

To Erin MacDonald and the next instructor

## Context

Erin and I kept the 14 anonymous paragraphs from 12 June to see how the original single-paragraph prompt was working. In our 14 June read, five drafts used the count of stops as if it counted people who read the notice. Six reported the stops and said reading was unknown. In three the observation was missing or the distinction remained unclear without an explicit reading claim. We used the tally to design July's prompts and selected two contrasting passages for feedback. The paragraphs carry no grades.

## Change and rationale

On 10 July we taught as far as planning one comparison before seeking a permanent move. Learners put the observation, the possible effect of the cabinet and the plan in separate spaces. They checked with a partner that the two conditions changed only the intended feature and that the proposed measure answered the question they were asking.

The observed counts are four, nine and three stops in three 20-minute windows on 22 May at the current north-wall position. The proposed comparison uses the same noon window on two nearby weekdays. As of this handoff, the east-wall location has neither been fitted with the notice nor observed. The notice is still on the north wall.

## Next review

Adjust the worksheet prompts, peer questions or writing time for the next group. Keep the observation first and the plan separate from its results. When a pair has matched the times, ask them to check the paper size and layout too. Then ask what they could tell the break room contact if one position produced more stops. Any decision to pursue an actual comparison goes to the person responsible for the room first.
