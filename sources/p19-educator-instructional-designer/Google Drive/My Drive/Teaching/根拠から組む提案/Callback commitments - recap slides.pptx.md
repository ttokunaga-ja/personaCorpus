## Slide 1

Layout: Cover

Title: Can we hold a slot without promising it?

Subtitle: Building Proposals from Evidence · ELM-BPE-2025-SLD-007 · v0.1 · 31 Oct 2025 · Heather Callahan

Speaker notes: Opening activity, 3 minutes. Ask for a quick vote before discussing the distinction.

## Slide 2

Layout: Heading and body

Title: What do we know?

Body:
- 12 callbacks; each slot can take up to 16.
- Wednesday 14:00–15:00 or Thursday 09:00–10:00.
- 10 callbacks are due by Wednesday 16:00; the other 2 can wait until Thursday.
- Wednesday duty cover has not yet been confirmed.

Speaker notes: Allow about 30 seconds. Both slots fit; the deadline makes Wednesday the preferred option for the ten. The missing cover confirmation affects the promise, not the deadline.

## Slide 3

Layout: Two columns

Title: Which statement needs duty cover confirmed?

Left column:
A. “Wednesday is our first choice. Can we hold that slot while we check cover?”

Right column:
B. “The callbacks will take place Wednesday from 14:00 to 15:00.”

Bottom prompt: Vote A, B, or both. What would you check before saying it to the duty lead?

Speaker notes: Give about 45 seconds for the vote and a reason. A asks to hold a preferred option subject to confirmation; B promises the time and requires the cover check. Do not treat a request to hold as a confirmed booking.

## Slide 4

Layout: Heading and body

Title: What remains open?

Body:
- Check Wednesday duty cover before promising the callback time.
- The 10 Wednesday 16:00 deadlines still apply while cover is unconfirmed.
- Ask the duty lead about an alternative Wednesday response for those 10; the other 2 can wait until Thursday.

Prompt: What would you tell the duty lead now, and what would you leave conditional?

Speaker notes: Allow about 1 minute 45 seconds to hear a response and connect it to the vote. Listen for both the unconfirmed promise and the deadline that remains. Close by asking for a conditional request, not a definite Wednesday commitment.
