## Slide 1 — Cover

Title: Comparing two notice locations

Subtitle: Building Proposals from Evidence · ELM-BPE-2024-SLD-003 v1.0 · 04 Jul 2024 · Heather Callahan

Speaker notes: Use on 10 July during the eight-minute comparison discussion. Ask learners to hold off on choosing a wall until they have checked the two observation conditions.

## Slide 2 — Two columns

Title: Two walls, two times

Diagram: Two room outlines side by side, each with north at the top and a doorway at the bottom. Inside the left outline, put a notice shape against the top wall. Inside the right outline, put a notice shape against the right wall.

| North wall · Current position | East wall · Candidate |
|---|---|
| 22 May, 12:10–12:30 | Proposed window, 14:40–15:00 |
| 9 stops recorded | Not observed |

Question below the pair: If the stop counts differed, could you tell whether position or time of day made the difference?

Speaker notes: Point out that the afternoon window here is a proposal for the east wall. The May afternoon record was taken with the north-wall notice still in place. Ask a learner to identify how a change in the flow of people at the doorway could affect the count.

## Slide 3 — Diagram

Title: Give both positions the same window

Diagram: Place two position boxes above a shared conditions box. Connect each position box to the shared box with a line.

- Left position box: North wall · One nearby weekday · Result: not observed for this trial
- Right position box: East wall · Another nearby weekday · Result: not observed
- Shared conditions box: 12:10–12:30 both days · Same notice, one on display at a time · Same south entrance observation line

Speaker notes: The aim is to change the position while keeping the comparison as similar as possible. Even at the same time of day, a different day can bring different traffic. Ask why putting both notices up at once would make it hard to connect a stop with either position.

## Slide 4 — Heading and body

Title: What will you put in your plan?

Body:

- Name the two positions and the conditions you would keep the same.
- Define the action the observer will record.
- Tell the break room contact what would still be unknown.

Speaker notes: Give out the worksheet. If a learner chooses stops, ask them to specify a complete stop at the line. A change in that count would describe what happened at the entrance during the two windows. Ask what evidence they would need to make a claim about reading.
