# Let people commit to a reason first
ELM-BPE-2025-DD-005 | 10 Sep 2025 | Heather Callahan  
Building Proposals from Evidence | To: Erin MacDonald

## Context

Erin and I tried the three-stage callback sequence at the table today. I showed the Wednesday heading, the added capacity fact and the deadline explanation in succession. Erin took the learner's role. We could talk about the final explanation, but we had no record of the point at which her judgment changed: the three screens had gone by before she had committed a response.

## Change and rationale

After each piece of information, pause and ask each person to write one line: what can you say now, and what supports it? Only then show the next piece. Keep the lines so that a change in judgment can be located rather than reconstructed after seeing the answer. A capacity fact that applies to both slots may show that a choice fits without explaining which should be preferred; the deadline can change that comparison.

For the closing question, try withholding one piece of information instead of adding another. Ask what can still be said from the remaining records and what cannot yet be promised. This is a direction to test in the next design, not an account of a completed classroom exercise.

## Next review

Erin and I will look at the order of the three callback records and the question attached to each one. Check whether the pause gives us a usable line of reasoning at each stage and whether the final question leaves enough information for a meaningful answer.
