# Break room observation lesson

Building Proposals from Evidence | ELM-BPE-2024-LP-001 | v1.0 | 30 May 2024 | Heather Callahan

12 Jun 2024 · 45 minutes · Heather Callahan and Erin MacDonald

## Learning aims

Write a sentence that another reader can check against one row of the doorway tally. Explain what the break room sketch suggests about the view from the entrance. Use the count and that possible explanation in a short request to the person responsible for the room.

## Teaching sequence

### 0–7 min · Write an observation on your own

Open Doorway observation briefing with the observation line and the 22 May tally. Keep the room sketch aside and drop the opening vote on the move. The notice was on the north wall throughout the three 20-minute windows. The record shows 4 stops at 10:00–10:20, 9 at 12:10–12:30 and 3 at 14:40–15:00. Ask everyone to choose one row and write a sentence naming the window, the count and the action recorded. Give them time to finish a sentence before taking answers.

### 7–17 min · Check the wording against the record

Ask pairs to point to the row behind each sentence. Erin, take us back to the rule on the Doorway observation sheet if anyone turns the tally into a headcount. One mark means an entry came to a complete stop at the line across the south doorway. An entry that slowed down and kept moving added no mark. Repeat entries were counted by the same rule and were never matched to individuals.

Keep the lunch row on screen. If we hear “nine people” or “nine readers,” ask “What did the observer have to see to make one mark?” Give the writer a moment to change the subject or verb. The usable sentence is “From 12:10 to 12:30 on 22 May, nine entries came to a complete stop at the entrance line.” We have no total for all entries and no count of who could read the notice or who read it.

### 17–25 min · Read the floor sketch and look at the sightline

Hand out Break room floor sketch (ELM-BPE-2024-HO-001 v1.0) on paper. Have learners put a finger on the dotted observation line at the south entrance, then find the current notice on the north wall, the cabinet toward the east and the dashed candidate position on the east wall. Use the paper to establish where everything is.

Then project Cabinet sightline from doorway separately. Trace the dashed ray from the marked viewpoint to the cabinet edge. Ask what might change if we took one step inside and turned. Label the board “What we counted” and “What the sketch suggests.” Keep the possible obstruction under the second heading. The diagram shows a blocked sightline from one point. We have no measured share of the notice visible from the doorway and no observation at the east-wall position.

### 25–40 min · Write to the person responsible for the room

Hand out Break room proposal prompt (ELM-BPE-2024-HO-003 v1.0). Ask for one paragraph of 80–120 words that uses the nine stops in the lunch window, gives a reason suggested by the sketch and asks for a specific change to the notice’s location. As we circulate, ask the writer to point to the words that name what was counted and then to the words that explain why the proposed position might help.

### 40–45 min · Collect the paragraphs without names

Ask learners to reread the sentence with the count, leave their names off and hand in the page. Erin and I will read the papers together after class.

## Check for understanding

A reader should be able to find the lunch row from the paragraph and see that nine describes complete stops at the entrance line. The cabinet may explain why part of the north-wall notice is hard to see from that point. Look for wording that keeps this as a possibility. Check that the person responsible for the room can tell where the writer wants the notice moved and why. A clear request still needs a count that matches the tally and a reason the sketch can support.
