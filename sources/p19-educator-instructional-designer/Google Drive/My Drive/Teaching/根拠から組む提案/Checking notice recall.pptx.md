## Slide 1 — Cover

Title: Checking notice recall

Subtitle: Building Proposals from Evidence · ELM-BPE-2024-SLD-004 v1.0 · 09 Jul 2024 · Erin MacDonald

Speaker notes: Use after the peer check on 10 July. Take a plan that measures stops and ask what it would take to find out whether an entrant could state the purpose of the notice.

## Slide 2 — Diagram

Title: Two ways to gather evidence

Diagram: Show two separate horizontal paths of three boxes, with arrows pointing from left to right within each path.

- Top row: At the entrance line → Observe complete stops → Tally of stops
- Bottom row: After people pass through → Invite a voluntary answer → What respondents can say about the notice

Speaker notes: The recording card covers the upper path. On the lower path we would invite answers after passage, including from people who kept walking. This is an additional method to discuss. There are no recall results in the doorway record.

## Slide 3 — Table

Title: Ask the same question at each position

Question to ask after passage: “If you'd like to answer, what is the notice asking people to do?”

| Position | Number who answer | Number of answers that state the notice's purpose |
|---|---|---|
| North wall | Not yet collected | Not yet collected |
| East wall | Not yet collected | Not yet collected |

Speaker notes: Use the same notice and invitation in both conditions. Include a response such as “I can't remember” in the number who answer, while counting answers that state the purpose in the separate column. A person who declines has supplied no answer. Keep these totals separate from the number of stops.

## Slide 4 — Heading and body

Title: Whose answers would we have?

Body:

- People who are in a hurry may be less likely to answer.
- A person can have read the notice and still be unable to recall its purpose when asked.
- What could you say about the people who answered? What would you still need to learn about everyone else?

Speaker notes: Ask what we know about someone who declines. We cannot classify that person or someone unable to recall the purpose as having left the notice unread. Invite one learner to say how they would word a conclusion limited to those who volunteered an answer.
