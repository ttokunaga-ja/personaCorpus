# Slide 1
Layout: Cover
Title: Make the second action visible
Subtitle: Building Proposals from Evidence | ELM-BPE-2025-SLD-002 | 28 Apr 2025 | Heather Callahan
Speaker notes: After returning the proposals, use a new workplace situation rather than displaying anyone's answer. Today we will make the action after a condition visible.

# Slide 2
Layout: Heading and body
Title: A request to the lead
Body: Our training is currently booked for 14:00. I recommend moving it to 10:00 so the team can attend before the afternoon shift. The lead must secure the 10:00 room by noon.
Question: What does the lead know to do if the room is not secured by noon?
Speaker notes: Ask learners to name the recommendation and the check. Do not add an action that is not written.

# Slide 3
Layout: Two columns
Title: Same recommendation, different next action
Left heading: Still to discuss
Left body: “Move training to 10:00 if the room is secured by noon. We can discuss another time separately.”
Right heading: Action stated
Right body: “Move training to 10:00 if the room is secured by noon. If it cannot be secured by noon, keep the current 14:00 slot.”
Speaker notes: In both cases the lead knows the preferred time and the deadline. On the left another time still needs discussion; the lead cannot tell what to retain. On the right the lead can retain the current slot when the condition fails. Ask what remains to be communicated.

# Slide 4
Layout: Heading and body
Title: Return to your own proposal
Body: Underline the action you propose if your condition is not met. Circle the person or team who can carry it out. If you have only named a check, add a sentence that tells the reader what to do when the answer is no.
Speaker notes: Give learners time to reread their own work. Invite them to check with a partner whether the underlined words actually name an action. A check is not itself the fallback.
