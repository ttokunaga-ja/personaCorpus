# Friday pickup — teaching copy
Reading Conditions at Work | ELM-CRW-2024-LP-002 | v1.0 | 25 Jun 2024 | Heather Callahan

For Erin and the covering instructor | Duration: 45 minutes

## Learning aims

Learners identify the usual rule, find the conditions that apply, check the requirements for an exception against the facts, compare instructions by issuer and scope, and explain a decision using a clause and a case fact. Timing supports the lesson; speed is not the achievement condition.

## Teaching sequence

Prepare the two-pickup slides, *When Friday pickup can go ahead*, and the visitor notice and reception instruction for the comparison. Keep the later conditions and the two confirmation times covered at the start. Use the board space for a conclusion under each time.

| Time | Activity and instructor prompts |
|---|---|
| 0–7 min | Give learners the usual Friday rule: collection by 16:00. Show the two arrivals at 16:20 without the exception heading or later conditions. Ask each person to write the usual deadline and an initial answer to “Would you release it at 4:20?” Keep the first answer for comparison. |
| 7–19 min | In pairs, reveal the shared card: both collections are booked and arrive at 16:20. Ask “Is each collection booked? What does that tell us?” Then show the later window, up to 17:30, and ask what else must be checked. Reveal the separate requirement for supervisor confirmation before 16:00. Ask “When did the supervisor confirm?” Show 15:40 first and ask pairs to decide with a reason; then show 16:05 and ask them to decide again. Have pairs mark the clause and the fact used for each. Do not treat booking as confirmation. |
| 19–29 min | Give pairs the visitor notice and reception instruction. For each, identify the issuer, intended reader and scope. Ask: “Does the public notice need to be rewritten to describe every reception exception? Can reception release the 15:40 case under its instruction?” Compare the usual public deadline with the conditional instruction for reception. |
| 29–35 min | Individual exit writing, six minutes: each learner chooses one of the two cases and writes a decision, the relevant section and the case fact that makes it apply. Collect a one-sentence reason from every learner before opening the comparison; invite anyone who has not spoken to write or hand in their sentence without requiring a public answer. |
| 35–45 min | Comparison, ten minutes: place the two decisions side by side. Invite different explanations, including reasons collected from learners who had not spoken, and check them against the notice. Ask what changed between the two cases and whether the arrival time or confirmation time explains the different outcomes. |

## Check for understanding

For the 15:40 case, look for a booked collection, supervisor confirmation before 16:00 and arrival at 16:20 within the 17:30 limit. Section 2 permits release. For the 16:05 case, the booking and arrival are the same but confirmation is after the required time; sections 2 and 3 lead to collection on the next business day.

If a pair gives the same decision for both, point to each confirmation time and ask them to compare it with “before 16:00” in section 2. If they cite only the booking or the 17:30 limit, ask separately what the confirmation record shows. In the exit sentences, check for a decision supported by both the relevant clause and a case fact; return to the notice with anyone whose reason does not yet connect them.

Source: Janice Mitchell, *Friday collection: reception instructions*, BSC-OPS-2024-014, 02 May 2024, sections 1–3.
