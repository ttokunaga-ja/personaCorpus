# Why we dropped the timing target
Reading Conditions at Work | ELM-CRW-2024-DD-002 | v1.0 | 18 Apr 2024 | Heather Callahan
To: David Ashcroft

## Context
At our meeting on 18 April, 13:30–14:00, David and I reviewed the five proposed aims. The 4 April plan records how quickly a learner finds three facts. At the 9 April desk check Erin found the usual deadline promptly but applied it to an exception case as well. The time and the located words alone did not tell us whether she had checked the conditions governing the decision.

## Change and rationale
We agreed to use these five aims for Reading Conditions at Work:

1. Find the usual rule in a workplace document.
2. Identify the conditions that determine when the rule applies.
3. Check each requirement of an exception against the facts of a case.
4. Compare competing instructions by their issuer and scope of application.
5. Explain a decision by connecting the relevant clause to the case facts.

| Earlier approach | Agreed approach | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Count facts found and record elapsed time as evidence of achievement. | Use timing to manage the instructor's lesson only; judge the learner's decision and its support. | A quickly located deadline can still be applied to the wrong case. |
| Select the more restrictive instruction. | Compare who issued each instruction and the cases and readers it covers. | Different instructions can address different audiences or situations; strictness alone does not settle their application. |
| Exit by locating three facts. | Give two cases under the same usual rule whose facts lead to different decisions; ask for the clause and facts behind each. | The contrast makes the condition that changes the decision visible. |

## Next review
Heather will develop a workplace reading and the paired exit cases for the next materials review with David. The examples need enough information to check each exception requirement, without making the answer depend on speed. Check that the two cases share the usual rule and that their different outcomes follow from a stated fact.
