# Slide 1 — Cover
Who can clear the dispatch hold?
Reading Conditions at Work | ELM-CRW-2024-SLD-002 | 04 Jun 2024 | Heather Callahan

Speaker notes: Ask learners to distinguish the person who supplies the remeasurement record from the desk person who decides whether to clear the hold.

# Slide 2 — Heading and body
## A sheet arrives at 13:50
Usual handover: sheets completed by 14:00.
Temperature-check field: blank.
What happens before the sheet can go forward?

Speaker notes: A blank temperature field triggers a hold, even though the sheet arrived before 14:00.

# Slide 3 — Diagram
## Hold, record, decision
Sheet received before 14:00 → temperature field blank → desk puts sheet on hold.
Duty person remeasures and records the result → desk checks that record → desk may clear the hold up to and including 14:30.
No recorded remeasurement → hold continues.

Speaker notes: In this handover example the desk person checks the duty person's recorded remeasurement and decides and carries out clearance. The duty person provides the measurement and record; a spoken report alone does not give the desk evidence to clear it. The instruction permits clearance only up to 14:30.

# Slide 4 — Two columns
## “I measured it again”
**Spoken report**: The original field is blank; someone says a repeat measurement was made.
**Record**: Where would you look for the duty person's recorded remeasurement? What must the desk person verify before clearing the hold?

Speaker notes: Invite pairs to point to the remeasurement record on the sheet. Do not treat the report as a record. Ask what happens if no record is found and how the 14:30 limit affects the decision.
