# What the next clerk needs from your memo
Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-HO-014 · v1.0 · 21 Sep 2026 · Heather Callahan

## A handover that someone can use

Read this instructor example alongside your own memo and the feedback you received. It uses the counter case, not a new set of facts.

> Please return at 13:00. The counter record shows the passage was checked at 12:10. At 12:20 the approval field is blank; the record does not tell us whether approval was given elsewhere. Section 2 allows 12:30 only if both passage check and responsible person's approval were recorded by 12:15. Ask the responsible person: “Was approval recorded by 12:15, and where can I verify it?” Until both conditions are established, keep the 13:00 advice, as section 3 directs.

## Compare it with your own sentence

Find the sentence in your memo that a visitor or the next clerk would hear first. Does it give an action that can be used now? Find your reference to the passage entry and its time, and the sentence that explains why that entry matters under section 2. Then find what you wrote about the blank approval field. Does it distinguish an unrecorded answer from a refusal?

If you suggested 12:30, underline where you established *both* required entries by 12:15. If that support is missing, revise the advice and write the question you would put to the responsible person. Compare your revision with the feedback on your own work.

Source: Counter reopening — documents for the exercise, visitor notice, staff instruction sections 2–3 and counter confirmation record viewed at 12:20.
