# One arrival time — two instructions

Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-LP-005 · v1.0  
11 Aug 2026 · Heather Callahan · For Erin MacDonald  
Session: 17 Aug 2026 · 70 minutes · Brookdale reading course, 36 places

## Learning aims

Learners will identify who each instruction is for before choosing a destination. They will use the applicable instruction and the reception record to explain separately where a late visitor should check in and which entrance receiving staff should use for a scheduled delivery. The shared arrival time is not sufficient evidence on its own.

Materials: Two people at reception at 10:20 (ELM-CRW-2026-HO-011 v1.0), available in the local packet and on paper; copies of the comparison table below and blank slips for the final rewrite.

## Teaching sequence

| Minutes | Activity | Teacher action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Access check | Check that each learner can open the local handout or has the same paper copy. Offer a paper copy if needed. Keep this check separate from the reading work. |
| 5–20 | Read independently | Ask learners to circle the intended reader or people covered by each instruction, then mark the relevant time and condition. Do not ask for a door yet. Ask: “Who is this instruction written for? Who does it apply to?” |
| 20–40 | Two individual decisions | Each learner completes the table below, starting with the reader and scope. Ask separately where the visitor should check in and which entrance receiving staff should use for the delivery. Each answer needs the document and the record, not just a place. |
| 40–60 | Exchange evidence in pairs | Partners compare their two decisions and point to the words in each instruction and the corresponding record. If both arrivals have been sent to the east door, ask the pair to find “scheduled deliveries only” and identify the visitor notice before reconsidering. Invite a pair to explain how the same arrival time can lead to two destinations. |
| 60–70 | Individual rewrite and collection | Give each learner the sentence below. Ask them to rewrite it as two instructions, one for the visitor and one for receiving staff, using the evidence they have just compared. On the slip, identify the source and record behind each sentence. Collect the slips. |

## Check for understanding

In the visitor notice, the instruction is for visitors who arrive at least 15 minutes after their appointment. The record shows an appointment at 10:00 and arrival at 10:20: 20 minutes late. The visitor should check in again at front reception. The receiving instruction is addressed to receiving staff and limits the east door to scheduled deliveries from 10:00 to 11:00. The record identifies a scheduled delivery at 10:20, within that interval, so receiving staff should use the east door for it.

Read the individual slips for both destinations and for the link from each destination to the right instruction and record. A correct door alone does not show that the learner distinguished the scope. If a learner sends both arrivals east, ask which words make the delivery instruction limited and which document addresses the visitor; let the learner reconsider before supplying the answer. Keep any access observation separate from these anonymous reading responses.
