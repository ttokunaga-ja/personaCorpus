# When to ask for the addressee
Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-IR-003 · v1.0 · 18 Aug 2026 · Heather Callahan

For Erin MacDonald · Review of 17 August discussion

## What we heard

Erin and I listened to the reasons given by eight pairs as they worked with the two 10:20 arrivals. Three pairs initially sent both the visitor and the scheduled delivery to the east door. After they restated who the instructions addressed, those pairs moved the visitor back to front reception. This was a small set of conversations at one point in the lesson, not a count of the whole class or a measure of individual attainment.

## Where the distinction appeared

The visitor notice addresses visitors arriving at least 15 minutes after an appointment. The record shows a 10:00 appointment and arrival at 10:20. The receiving staff instruction uses the east door from 10:00 to 11:00 for scheduled deliveries only; the other arrival is recorded as a scheduled delivery at 10:20. The time is shared, but the scope of each instruction is different.

The marked instructor copy, *Circling the reader on the reception sheet*, preserves the two extracts and the record, with circles around the visitor condition and “scheduled deliveries only”, and corrected arrows. It helps us revisit the distinction, but it does not by itself establish what every learner understood.

## Next practice

Do not judge the explanation by destination alone. Ask the writer to give the next person one usable sentence that makes clear who is covered and names the supporting document. Have a partner check whether that sentence could accidentally send the visitor along the delivery route. Listen for the limiting words before offering a correction; compare the statement with the record as well as the instruction.
