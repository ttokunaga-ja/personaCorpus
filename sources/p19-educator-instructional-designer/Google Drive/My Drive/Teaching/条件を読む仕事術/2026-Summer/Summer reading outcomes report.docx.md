# Reading Conditions at Work — summer outcomes
ELM-CRW-2026-RPT-001 · v1.0 · 28 Sep 2026 · Heather Callahan

## Training scope

To: David Ashcroft, Education Lead

The July–September course asked learners to read a workplace notice alongside the staff instruction and a check record. The final assessment was a one-page counter decision memo, linking a visitor-facing instruction to the applicable clause and recorded facts, and identifying what still needed to be checked. This report concerns the reading course only.

## Submission and assessment results

The denominator is the 36 learners enrolled at 16:00 on 11 September 2026, the submission deadline.

| Measure | Learners | Of 36 enrolled |
|---|---:|---:|
| Valid memo submitted | 34 | 94.4% |
| At least 14 of 20 points | 27 | 75.0% |

Of the 34 submissions, seven scored below 14. Two learners had no valid submission and no score; they are not part of those seven. The accompanying count sheet shows the marked score bands. A percentage of the 34 submissions alone answers a different question and is not the agreed reporting rate.

## Assessment and interpretation

Erin and I moderated eight anonymised memos on 14 September, retaining our independent marks and recording the decisions where our readings differed. We agreed a 20-point scale: Evidence 8, Organisation 6 and Understanding conditions 6. We then reread the remaining valid submissions using those criteria. For a learner with more than one valid submission by the deadline, we used the last valid version. A missing or unreadable submission was not scored as zero.

The task can show how a learner supports a counter decision in this case; it does not establish performance across every workplace situation. The ordinary 13:00 advice and a question about the blank approval record are separate steps. In the next explanation I want to check whether learners can choose the ordinary advice while still identifying what must be confirmed with the person responsible, rather than treating a blank as a refusal.

## Follow-up

Kathleen and I have prepared an opportunity next term for the two learners without a valid submission. We plan to contact them individually with paper and optional consultation routes; the follow-up has not yet taken place. The September figures above describe the deadline close, not the outcome of future work.
