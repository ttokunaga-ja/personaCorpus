# What the first exit slips actually showed

Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-IR-002 · v1.0  
14 Jul 2026 · Heather Callahan · For Erin MacDonald

## What we read

Erin and I reviewed 28 anonymous exit slips from the 13 July counter exercise. Each slip is counted once by the response pattern visible on the paper; this is a picture of the exercise, not a course result.

| Pattern in the response | Slips | What we could see |
|---|---:|---|
| Reason and enquiry together | 16 | The answer connected the normal 13:00 notice or the early-opening condition to the record, and identified approval as something to check. |
| Blank read as refusal | 7 | The approval field was treated as a refusal; a 13:00 answer alone did not resolve that interpretation. |
| Time only | 5 | A time was given without enough explanation to identify the reason or an enquiry. |
| Total | 28 | Anonymous slips collected at the end. |

Separately, Arjun recorded 24 people opening the local PDF and 10 reading the paper packet at entry, with two absent and access unobserved. The 34 who could read the packet are an access observation, not the denominator for these 28 slips. We cannot link a route to an answer or use either count as a final score.

## Where the question led

I asked aloud, “Did they approve it?” That invites a yes-or-no claim where the record has only an empty approval field. One slip gave the ordinary 13:00 time yet said “They refused approval.” The record shows the passage check at 12:10 and no approval entry as of 12:20; it does not establish refusal. Section 3 directs staff to check a blank before announcing early opening.

Another slip named the normal time, connected the early option to both required entries and proposed asking the responsible person. It shows the distinction we want to hear, without implying an unrecorded outcome of that enquiry.

## Change for the next discussion

Start with “What is recorded as of 12:20, and what do you still need to check?” Ask for the visitor-facing sentence only after the record and outstanding enquiry have been named. Listen for the notice, the condition in section 2 and the check in section 3; do not infer approval or refusal from silence in the record. Erin and I will use the next exercise to listen for the link between a condition and the fact that makes it applicable.
