Who was that instruction written for?

Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-SLD-007 · v1.0 · 12 Aug 2026 · Heather Callahan

Presenter notes: Ask everyone to have the two extracts and the reception record available on paper or screen.

# Slide 2 — Two arrivals
Layout: Table

| Arrival | Appointment or delivery | Arrival time |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor | Appointment 10:00 | 10:20 |
| Scheduled delivery | Delivery | 10:20 |

What do you know from the record? What does it not tell you about the destination?

Presenter notes: Let learners name the shared time before turning to the instructions. Do not use the shared arrival time as the reason for a shared destination.

# Slide 3 — Read across, then compare
Layout: Two columns

Left — Visitor notice
- Issuer: Brookdale Service Cooperative
- Reader: visitors
- Instruction: 15 minutes or more after appointment time → check in again at front reception.

Right — Receiving staff instruction
- Issuer: Brookdale Service Cooperative
- Reader: receiving staff
- Instruction: 10:00–11:00 → east door for scheduled deliveries only.

With a partner: if you send the visitor to the east door, which instruction's scope have you mixed up? Point to the limiting words.

Presenter notes: Ask partners to name the intended reader and the people covered before naming the destination. The delivery instruction is limited to scheduled deliveries; the visitor's 20-minute delay meets the visitor notice.

# Slide 4 — Two routes
Layout: Diagram

Diagram: Two branches from “Arrivals at 10:20”. Upper branch: “Visitor, appointment 10:00” → “Front reception” → “Evidence: ______________________________”. Lower branch: “Scheduled delivery” → “East door” → “Evidence: ______________________________”.

Write the document and the relevant words beside each destination. Tell your partner how the record connects to those words.

Presenter notes: Reveal the routes after pairs have chosen. Ask for both document and record; a location alone is not the explanation.
