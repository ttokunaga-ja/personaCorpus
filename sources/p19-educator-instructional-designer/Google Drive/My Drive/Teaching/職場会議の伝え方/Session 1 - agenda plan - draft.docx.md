# From agenda to decision — first pass
ELM-CWM-2024-LP-001 | v0.1 | 27 Aug 2024 | Heather Callahan
Communicating in Workplace Meetings | Session 1 of 6 | 12 Sep 2024, 10:00–11:15
Circulation copy for comment

## Learning aims
Participants identify the item requiring a decision and begin a short contribution that asks the chair for a decision. Use Glenn's maintenance agenda as the common text. Twenty-four participants work in six groups of four.

## Teaching sequence
| Time | Activity | Facilitator action |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00–10:15 | Completed example | Before distributing the agenda, model: “If the key rota is confirmed by Thursday noon, could we try Saturday mornings for two weeks, up to six visits each Saturday?” Ask what the chair is being asked to decide. |
| 10:15–10:25 | Read | Give out the agenda extract. Participants underline the update and circle the item for decision. Ask them to find where it says to confirm the key rota. |
| 10:25–10:45 | Speak in fours | Each person gives a short request to the chair; listeners identify the requested decision. Rotate the speaker. |
| 10:45–11:05 | Share | Invite one request from each group. Compare the request with the information reported under item 3. |
| 11:05–11:15 | Exit | Individually point to the agenda item that asks the meeting to decide and write what the decision is about. Collect the responses. |

## Check for understanding
Look for item 4 and a decision about trying Saturday mornings for two weeks. If a participant points to item 3, ask what the meeting is being told there and return to the wording of item 4.

## Connection to the remaining sessions
Session 2 develops a 30-second contribution with a reason and a question. Session 3 distinguishes a preference from evidence. Session 4 considers a condition that changes the action; session 5 practises responding to an objection; session 6 brings the elements together in a meeting and individual exercise. The agenda is a practice scenario, not a record of an approved trial.
