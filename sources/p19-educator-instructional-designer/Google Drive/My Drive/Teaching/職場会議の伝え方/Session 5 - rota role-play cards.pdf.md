# An objection that changes the action
Communicating in Workplace Meetings | ELM-CWM-2024-HO-004 | 31 Oct 2024 | Heather Callahan

## The item
The meeting is deciding whether to try Saturday morning maintenance visits for two weeks, up to six visits each Saturday. In the last four weeks, 8 of 30 weekday visits were rescheduled. The scheduling lead must confirm the key rota by Thursday noon before each Saturday; if it is not confirmed, the weekday slots stay in place.

Several people support a Saturday trial. The scheduling lead now says that a change in the rota means they cannot guarantee that the key rota will be confirmed by Thursday noon. Support for the trial has not itself confirmed the rota.

## Roles
- **Chair:** Invite the proposal and the scheduling lead’s information. Repeat the confirmation point before asking the group what action can be recorded.
- **Proposer:** Give the action and reason. When you hear the new information, revise what you ask the meeting to do. Say what happens if confirmation is still missing at noon.
- **Scheduling lead:** Explain that the change in the rota prevents a guarantee of confirmation by the deadline. Do not claim the rota is confirmed. Identify what information you would need before confirming it.
- **Recorder:** Note separately what the group decides now and what must be carried forward. Do not record support as approval.

## Discussion and rotation
Spend six minutes on the conversation, then change roles so that everyone tries each role. After each round, ask:

1. What information changed the proposal? Point to it.
2. What would have to change before Saturday could be proposed again? Who would confirm it, and by when?
3. If the rota is still unconfirmed at Thursday noon, who will tell the others that the weekday slots are being kept? Agree a person in your group and record that responsibility.
4. What was decided now, and what remains for a later decision?

Source: RWHC-AGD-2024-021, items 3 and 4; role scenario for classroom discussion.
