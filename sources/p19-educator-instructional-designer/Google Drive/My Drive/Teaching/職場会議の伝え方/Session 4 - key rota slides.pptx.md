# Slide 1 — Cover
Title: When the key rota is still open
Subtitle: Communicating in Workplace Meetings | ELM-CWM-2024-SLD-004 | 23 Oct 2024 | Heather Callahan
Speaker notes: Today we will put both possible actions into words before asking the listener to judge.

# Slide 2 — Two columns
Title: Same proposal, different information
Left: Thursday 11:40 — the scheduling lead confirms the key rota. Propose the two-Saturday trial, up to six visits each Saturday.
Right: Thursday noon — the key rota is still unconfirmed. Hold off confirming Saturday bookings; keep the weekday slots.
Speaker notes: Ask the group to name the information that changed, not the number of people who prefer Saturday.

# Slide 3 — Diagram
Title: At the Thursday noon decision point
Diagram: “Key rota confirmed by the scheduling lead by Thursday noon?” branches to “Yes → propose Saturday morning for two weeks, maximum six visits each Saturday” and “No → hold Saturday booking confirmation; keep weekday slots”. The 11:40 confirmation enters the Yes branch; no confirmation at noon enters the No branch.
Speaker notes: The 11:40 report is within the deadline. A missing report at noon is not a confirmation.

# Slide 4 — Heading and body
Title: The chair interrupts
Chair: “There is still no reply.”
Speaker: What will you say about confirming Saturday bookings? Which slots remain available?
Listener: Point to the information and action in the speaker’s answer.
Speaker notes: If a group extends the deadline, ask: “What does item 4 say to do when the rota is still unconfirmed at noon? What can you keep available now?” Invite the speaker to say the weekday action, not simply wait longer.

# Slide 5 — Heading and body
Title: Write both branches before you decide
1. State the action and scope when confirmed.
2. State the action when unconfirmed at noon.
3. Point to the change in information that explains the different action.
Speaker notes: Give each person a turn. Listen for the distinction between a proposed trial and an approved decision.
