# Workplace meetings — outcomes
ELM-CWM-2024-RPT-001 | v1.0 | 02 Dec 2024 | Heather Callahan

## Training scope

For Glenn Fletcher, Rosedale Workers Housing Cooperative.

**22 of 24 submitted; 17 of those 22 met the conditional proposal criteria.**

Communicating in Workplace Meetings was designed for 24 participants in six 75-minute sessions from 12 September to 21 November. Using the maintenance handover agenda in six groups of four, participants practised identifying the decision in an agenda, making a 30-second contribution with a reason, distinguishing opinion from evidence, changing an action when a condition changes, responding to an objection, and recording a decision in a simulated meeting. The final session moved from role practice to an individual written exercise.

## Submission and assessment results

| Measure | People | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Target group | 24 | Participants in the course group |
| Submitted final exercise | 22 | One effective submission per learner by 26 November, 4:00 p.m. |
| Met conditional proposal criteria | 17 | Among the 22 submissions |
| Submitted, criteria not yet met | 5 | Among the 22 submissions |
| No submission | 2 | No assessment result |

The individual exercise asked participants to propose an action and its scope, support it with a fact from the agenda, identify the staffing coordinator's confirmation by Thursday noon, and state what to do if the key rota remained unconfirmed. Erin and I assessed each effective submission against all four elements. Attendance and particular wording were not conditions for the assessment. The five submitted but not yet meeting the criteria are distinct from the two people whose work was not submitted and therefore not assessed.

## Interpretation and follow-up

The figures describe the submitted written exercise; they do not count course completion or establish a change in workplace behaviour. The agenda was a practice scenario and the exercise does not establish whether the Saturday trial was implemented.

For a future group, I suggest a five-minute practice beginning with the unconfirmed branch: first say which weekday arrangement will remain, then state the confirmation condition and proposed Saturday trial. This gives participants a way to make the alternative explicit before returning to the complete proposal.
