# Slide 1 — Cover
Title: Update or decision?
Subtitle: Communicating in Workplace Meetings | ELM-CWM-2024-SLD-001 | 10 Sep 2024 | Heather Callahan
Notes: Session 1, 12 September. Give out the agenda extract before the comparison.

# Slide 2 — Two columns
Title: What are we listening for?
Left: Item 3 — update
- In the last four weeks, 8 of 30 weekday repair visits were rescheduled.
- Listen for what has happened and what needs clarification.
- “How many visits were rearranged?”
Right: Item 4 — decision
- Try Saturday mornings for two weeks, up to 6 visits each Saturday?
- Listen for the action and its scope.
- “Are we willing to try this under the stated condition?”
Notes: Ask pairs to point to the relevant item before explaining. Do not describe the Saturday trial as approved.

# Slide 3 — Diagram
Title: Follow the agenda item
Diagram: “What is this item asking the meeting to do?” branches to “Receive an update → item 3 → ask what happened” and “Make a decision → item 4 → ask whether to try”. Under item 4: “2 Saturdays; up to 6 visits each; key rota confirmed by the preceding Thursday noon; otherwise keep weekday slots”.
Notes: The staffing person checks confirmation of the key rota. Invite learners to say which branch they chose and point to the words that support it.

# Slide 4 — Heading and body
Title: Put the decision into one sentence
Body: Write a question the chair can put to the meeting. Include the length of the trial and the daily maximum. Compare it with a partner's sentence: can the chair hear what is being decided?
Notes: If someone reads six visits as the total for the two weeks, ask: “Where does the agenda say how many on each Saturday? How many Saturdays are proposed?” Return to “up to six visits on each Saturday”; do not turn the limit into a promised total.
