# Slide 1 — Cover
Title: Which fact belongs in the minute?
Subtitle: Communicating in Workplace Meetings | ELM-CWM-2024-SLD-003 | 09 Oct 2024 | Heather Callahan
Notes: Session 3, 10 October. Use the maintenance agenda as a proposed decision, not a record of an outcome.

# Slide 2 — Table
Title: A wish, a reason, a limit
| Statement | What does it do? |
|---|---|
| “I would like Saturday appointments.” | Expresses a wish. |
| “Eight of 30 weekday visits were rescheduled in the last four weeks.” | Gives an agenda fact that can support a reason for trying a different arrangement. |
| “Up to six visits each Saturday for two weeks.” | Defines the scope of the proposed trial; it is not evidence that the trial has happened. |
Notes: Ask each group which statement would help explain the proposal and which tells the meeting its boundaries. A preference alone is not evidence of a decision.

# Slide 3 — Table
Title: The recorder's three spaces
| Wish | Fact | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday appointments are wanted. | 8 of 30 weekday visits were rescheduled in four weeks. | Trial approved. |
Notes: This is a draft made before agreement. Ask what is wrong with the third cell. Cross out “Trial approved” and replace it with “Decision not yet made”. The meeting has not agreed simply because someone wants a trial.

# Slide 4 — Diagram
Title: From contribution to record
Diagram: “Wish: try Saturday” → “Reason: 8 of 30 rescheduled” → “Proposed scope: 2 weeks, up to 6 each Saturday” → “Chair asks for a decision” → “Record the decision actually made”. Below: “Before agreement: decision not yet made.”
Notes: In fours, one person proposes, one is chair, one listens and one records. Pause before the chair asks for a decision. What belongs in each space now? Discuss before moving on; no actual Rosedale outcome is implied.
