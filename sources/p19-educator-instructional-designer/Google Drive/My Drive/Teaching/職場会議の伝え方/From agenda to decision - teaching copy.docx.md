# From agenda to decision — teaching copy
ELM-CWM-2024-LP-001 | v1.0 | 04 Sep 2024 | Heather Callahan
Communicating in Workplace Meetings | Session 1 of 6 | 12 Sep 2024, 10:00–11:15
For the teaching team

## Learning aims

By the end of this session participants should be able to distinguish an update from a decision on an agenda, identify the proposed action and its limits, and ask the chair for a decision using a relevant fact. They should also identify who checks the condition and what to do if it is not met. Use the Rosedale maintenance agenda as a practice scenario: the trial has not yet been approved.

Twenty-four participants work in six groups of four. Have the agenda handout ready for each participant. Keep the completed speaking example out of sight until after the individual reading and pair comparison.

## Teaching sequence

| Time | Activity | Facilitator action |
|---|---|---|
| 10:00–10:10 | Individual reading | Give out the agenda extract. Ask each person to mark what the meeting is told in item 3 and what it is asked to decide in item 4. Have them underline the proposed duration, limit and condition. Do not show the completed example yet. |
| 10:10–10:20 | Pair comparison | Partners compare their marks and explain which words signal an update and which ask for a decision. Ask them to find the 30 visits and eight rescheduled in the last four weeks, and to distinguish six visits on each Saturday from six across both weeks. |
| 10:20–10:30 | Demonstration | Now model a short request: “Could we decide whether to try Saturday mornings for two weeks, up to six visits each Saturday? Eight of 30 weekday visits needed rescheduling. For each Saturday, can the person responsible for staffing confirm the key rota by noon on the preceding Thursday? If it is not confirmed by then, we should retain the weekday slots.” Ask listeners to identify the request, supporting fact and condition. |
| 10:30–10:55 | Speaking in fours | In each group, rotate speaker, chair, listener and recorder so each participant speaks. The speaker asks the chair to decide on the two-week trial and gives one fact from item 3. The chair asks who will confirm the rota and when; the speaker identifies the person responsible for staffing and noon on the Thursday preceding each proposed Saturday. If the rota is unconfirmed at that deadline, the speaker states that weekday slots remain. The recorder notes the proposed decision and condition without recording approval before the group has agreed. |
| 10:55–11:05 | Share | Invite a request from each group. Ask the room to identify the decision first, then the fact supporting it. If a group treats the six-visit limit as a two-week total, return to the words “each Saturday”. Separate what the agenda proposes from a decision the meeting has actually made. |
| 11:05–11:15 | Exit | Individually, point to the item requiring a decision and write what the meeting is asked to decide. Then answer: who checks the key rota, by when for each proposed Saturday, and what should happen if it is still unconfirmed at that time? Collect the responses. |

## Check for understanding

Look for item 4 and a decision on whether to try Saturday mornings for two weeks, with a maximum of six visits each Saturday. Item 3 is an update: eight of 30 weekday visits were rescheduled over the most recent four weeks. It supplies a reason to consider the trial; it is not the decision.

The person responsible for staffing confirms that the key rota is settled by noon on the Thursday preceding each Saturday in the proposed trial. If it is not confirmed by that deadline, retain the weekday appointment slots. A response that says only “confirm the keys” leaves the person and action unclear. Ask the participant to return to the agenda and name both. If someone proposes waiting beyond noon, ask what the agenda says to retain when the deadline arrives without confirmation. Do not treat a proposed trial as an approved one.

## Connection to the remaining sessions

On 26 September, build a 30-second contribution around a decision, a supporting fact and a question to the chair. On 10 October, distinguish a preference from the evidence and the decision. On 24 October, practise how a condition changes the action. On 7 November, respond to an objection and revisit the proposal. On 21 November, bring the elements into a meeting practice and an individual exercise. Carry forward the distinction between the agenda's proposed action and a decision actually recorded by the meeting.
