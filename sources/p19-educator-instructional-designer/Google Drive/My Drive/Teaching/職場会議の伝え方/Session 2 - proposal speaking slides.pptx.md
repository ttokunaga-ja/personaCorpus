# Slide 2 — Two columns
Title: When can the listener say yes or no?
Left: “We have had repair appointments moved around, and staff have been looking at the calendar. I'd like to talk about Saturdays.”
Listener: I know the topic, but what am I being asked to decide?
Right: “Could we decide whether to try Saturday mornings for two weeks, up to six visits each Saturday? Eight of the last 30 weekday visits were rescheduled.”
Listener: I can identify the proposed decision at the first question; the next sentence gives a reason.
Notes: Ask learners to point to the first words at which each listener can identify an action to accept or reject. The left column does not yet offer one.

# Slide 3 — Heading and body
Title: The chair interrupts
Body: Speaker: “There have been several changes to repair appointments and…”
Chair: “What is it you want us to decide now?”
Speaker: Finish the opening in one sentence. Then give one fact and ask the chair a question.
Notes: Work in fours; rotate speaker, chair and listeners. The chair interrupts without supplying the answer. Listeners mark the question that can be put to the meeting.

# Slide 4 — Heading and body
Title: Try the opening again
Body: Say your request, your reason and your question in 30 seconds. Is the decision audible before the history? What can you leave out?
Notes: If a group races through the whole contribution to fit 30 seconds, stop the clock and have the speaker redo only the opening sentence at a conversational pace. Then add the one supporting fact and the question.
