10 July 2024 — after the comparison lesson with Erin

We asked the pairs to check the time window first. One plan got both windows to 12:10, then put the east-wall notice on a larger sheet. We read the two set-ups aloud and pulled apart what belonged to the location and what belonged to the notice itself. If the stops differed, the larger sheet could matter just as much as the wall. Keeping the heading, paper size and layout the same gave the plan one intended change again.

Keep Erin's “What changed on just one side?” question when we next hand out the peer check. Ask the next one aloud before the papers go back: “If you count stops, how far can you go with that result?” I want to hear a claim about the doorway action and what the count leaves open about reading. Easy to miss that second part when both columns look tidy.
