# What to listen for in the four new prompts
Reading Conditions at Work | ELM-CRW-2024-IR-002 | v0.1 | 24 Jul 2024 | Erin MacDonald

For Heather — a guide for the next conversation around ELM-CRW-2024-HO-005.

## Evidence from the trial

The July selection result did not show whether a correct response to Q07 came from comparing both exception requirements. The four prompts invite the learner to make the reasoning visible. They have not yet been trialled, so the points below are listening prompts, not results.

## Item changes

| Prompt | Listen for | Follow-up if the reasoning is incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | The item is booked and the supervisor confirmed at 3:40, before 4:00. Arrival at 4:20 is within the 5:30 limit, so section 2 permits release. | “What else, besides the booking, needs checking? Where is the time limit?” |
| R2 | The 4:05 confirmation is after the required 4:00 cutoff. The fact that arrival at 4:20 is before 5:30 does not repair that missed condition; carry over under section 3. | “Which time belongs to confirmation, and which to collection?” |
| R3 | The desk checks the supervisor's confirmation log and does not release while the record is unknown. A missing record at the desk is not itself proof of a late confirmation. | “What are you waiting to learn? What would change once you find it?” |
| R4 | The visitor notice gives the public usual deadline; the desk notice addresses staff and section 2 sets the conditional exception. Section 3 covers cases where conditions are not met. The two documents have different readers and scope. | “Who is each document for? Which section answers the desk's question?” |

## Next trial

Listen for a coherent connection between the facts, section and decision, rather than a repeated phrase. Ask learners to point to the relevant part if their explanation is unclear. We can decide during instruction how much quotation to ask for; a long quotation alone does not demonstrate the connection. Keep a note of uncertainty separately from a wrong decision so we can revise the prompts after hearing them.
