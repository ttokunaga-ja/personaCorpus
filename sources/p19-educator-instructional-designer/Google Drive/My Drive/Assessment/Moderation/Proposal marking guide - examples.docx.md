# Proposal marking guide — annotated examples
ELM-BPE-2025-IR-001 | v1.0 | 25 Apr 2025 | Heather Callahan
Building Proposals from Evidence — instructor guide for Heather Callahan and Erin MacDonald

## Independent reading
Read each proposal independently before comparing marks. Award 0–4 for each criterion; total out of 12. Use the submitted text and the task materials. Note the words supporting each mark. A source title need not match verbatim when the answer identifies the place and fact; do not supply a missing fact or alternative. A check and the action if it is unconfirmed are separate.

## Criteria
| Score | Recommendation | Evidence | Conditional comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | A clear, workable recommendation addresses the decision and its intended recipients. | Relevant support is identified and connected clearly to the recommendation. | Compares the options and considers an alternative in relation to the condition. |
| 3 | A clear recommendation addresses the decision with a minor omission. | Relevant support is identifiable and mostly connected to the recommendation. | Considers an alternative with a minor omission in the comparison. |
| 2 | A recommendation is present but its scope or practicality is unclear. | Some relevant support is present, but its connection to the recommendation is incomplete. | Some comparison or conditional thinking is present, but it is incomplete. |
| 1 | An intended choice can be inferred but is difficult to use. | A limited or weakly relevant supporting detail is present. | A condition or an alternative is mentioned with little development. |
| 0 | No recommendation can be identified. | No relevant support is identifiable. | No condition or alternative can be identified. |

## Annotated examples
These are judgments on the quoted passages for the next read, not replacements for either reader's independent marks on the complete April proposals.

**P02**: “The Room B line in the capacity table gives 24 places, so the expected 18 people can attend together.” The proposal recommends Room B at 09:00 and asks for the assistant to be checked by Monday 15:00; if unsettled, it says to reconsider.
- Recommendation 4: the room and time provide a clear, workable choice for the group.
- Evidence 4: the capacity table and Room B line identify the source; 24 places and 18 expected are explicitly connected to attending together. The source title need not be copied exactly.
- Conditional comparison 2: the check and reconsideration recognise uncertainty, but no specific alternative is named or compared.

**P04**: “I recommend Room B at 09:00. Everyone should fit in Room B.”
- Recommendation 4: the room and time give a clear choice for the group.
- Evidence 0: the assertion that everyone should fit gives neither a capacity figure nor a source. Do not infer a comparison from the room name.
- Conditional comparison 0: the passage states neither a condition nor an alternative.

**P06**: “The capacity table gives it 24 places for the 18 expected. Please confirm the assistant instructor by Monday at 15:00.” The proposed choice is Room B at 09:00.
- Recommendation 3: the choice and time are clear, but its practical delivery depends on the assistant who is not yet confirmed.
- Evidence 3: the table and relevant numbers are identifiable and mostly support the choice, though the passage leaves their connection implicit.
- Conditional comparison 1: the confirmation and deadline are stated, but no action if unconfirmed is given. Do not read two sittings into the answer.

**P15**: “I recommend Room B at 09:00 because the capacity table gives it 24 places for the 18 expected.” “Without that confirmation at 15:00, run two sittings in Room A, nine people at 09:00 and nine at 11:00.” The answer asks for the assistant to be confirmed by Monday 15:00.
- Recommendation 4: the room and time make a clear, workable choice for the group.
- Evidence 4: as in P02, the table and its capacity for the named room are identifiable, and “because” explicitly connects 24 places for 18 expected to the recommendation. The cited passage meets the same Evidence criterion; its brevity alone does not reduce the mark.
- Conditional comparison 3: it gives a specific alternative for the unconfirmed condition and its times and group sizes, but the comparison between the options is brief. The alternative is present even without the exact phrase “if not confirmed”.
