# Reading memo marking guide
Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-IR-004 · v1.0 · 15 Sep 2026 · Heather Callahan

For Heather Callahan and Erin MacDonald · Remaining valid counter decision memos

## Using the scale

Award each criterion independently. Read what the writer actually put on the page; do not supply a missing connection or treat an absent approval entry as a refusal. Intermediate points can reflect work between the descriptions. Total the three marks out of 20. Use the last valid version received by the deadline; an absent or unreadable memo has no mark.

| Criterion | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence / 8 | 0–2: no relevant source or recorded fact linked to the advice. | 3–5: identifies a relevant source or fact but the link to the decision is partial or difficult to trace. Naming documents alone does not establish the link. | 6–8: identifies the applicable source and recorded facts and connects them to the advice; the reader can trace why the decision follows. |
| Organisation / 6 | 0–1: advice or next action cannot be found. | 2–4: some advice or action is present but the route between them and the supporting record is incomplete or hard to follow. | 5–6: the next clerk can find the visitor advice and follow the reason and next action. A separate paragraph is not required. |
| Understanding conditions / 6 | 0–1: does not distinguish the ordinary time from a conditional early time. | 2–4: recognises part of the conditional rule or ordinary advice but misreads, omits or fails to check a necessary condition. An assertion that a blank means refusal belongs here. | 5–6: distinguishes 13:00 from 12:30, accounts for both conditions and treats the 12:20 blank as unconfirmed, identifying the enquiry needed before changing advice. |

## Where to look

| Passage or feature | Evidence | Organisation | Understanding conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 2 and the 12:10 passage check | Is the recorded fact connected to the advice rather than merely listed? | Can the next clerk follow where the reason comes from? | Does the writer recognise the passage condition and that approval is also required by 12:15? |
| “Approval was refused” beside a blank | Is the record identified and related to the decision? | Can the advice and action be followed? | The 12:20 blank confirms no recorded approval there, not refusal. Ask whether and when approval was recorded. |
| Advice first, then a question under section 3 | Is the question grounded in the relevant record? | Can a clerk act on the 13:00 advice and find the next step? Paragraph count alone is not the criterion. | Does the question address the unconfirmed approval before the early advice is offered? |
| A list of document names | Does the memo identify an entry and connect its fact to the decision? A list alone does not. | Is the route from advice to action usable? | Does the memo distinguish the conditions from the normal advice? |

## Agreed examples for the next read

CRW-06: 6 / 5 / 2 = 13. The section and passage entry are present, but the claim of refusal goes beyond the record. CRW-17: 5 / 5 / 5 = 15. The compact handover starts with advice and gives the next clerk an enquiry. CRW-29: 3 / 5 / 4 = 12. It names the sources without connecting section 2 or the 12:10 entry to its advice. These examples guide the remaining reads; the independent marks and decisions are retained in the moderation record.
