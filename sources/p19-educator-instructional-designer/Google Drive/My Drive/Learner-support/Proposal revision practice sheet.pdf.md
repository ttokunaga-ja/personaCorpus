# Two sentences to reopen a proposal
Building Proposals from Evidence | ELM-BPE-2025-HO-002 | 29 Apr 2025 | Heather Callahan

## Start with one fact
Choose one fact from the information you used in your proposal. Write where the reader can find it. Then write one sentence explaining what that fact supports in your recommendation. A fact about a room, time or number is useful only if the reader can see how it bears on your choice.

Fact and source: ______________________________________________

Sentence 1 — what the fact supports: ____________________________

## Make the other action visible
Write a second sentence saying what you propose to do if the condition for your preferred action is not met. Name the action, not just the check.

Sentence 2 — if the condition is not met: ________________________

## Check an example
A reception desk needs an extra shift. The rota says the desk is open until 17:00; the support worker's availability for 15:00–17:00 must be confirmed by noon.

1. “The rota shows that reception stays open until 17:00, so the requested 15:00–17:00 support shift covers the late opening.”
2. “If the support worker is not confirmed by noon, keep the existing reception rota and tell the operations team that the extra shift is not yet covered.”

In the first sentence, underline the fact and circle the part of the recommendation it supports. In the second, underline the action and circle the condition. Does the sentence say what happens if confirmation is absent, or only ask someone to check? Who needs to hear about the action?

## Check your two sentences
- Can a reader find the fact in the source you named?
- Have you explained what the fact supports, rather than only naming it?
- Have you written an action for when the condition is not met?
- Is the action something the reader can carry out or communicate?
