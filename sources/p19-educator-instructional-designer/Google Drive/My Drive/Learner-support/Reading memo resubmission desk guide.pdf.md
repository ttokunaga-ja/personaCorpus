# What to do with a second copy
Reading Conditions at Work · ELM-CRW-2026-HO-013 · v1.0 · 08 Sep 2026 · Heather Callahan

For Arjun Deshmukh and the consultation and reception team

## At the desk

The decision memo is due on Friday 11 September at 16:00 Toronto time. Record when each copy is received, including a replacement. Keep the earlier receipt time in the reception record; do not overwrite it when a later copy arrives.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| A readable first copy arrives by the deadline | Record the receipt time and identify the copy as the person's current valid memo. |
| The same person sends a readable replacement by 16:00 | Record its receipt time separately. Keep the earlier time and identify the last valid copy received by the deadline as the one memo for that person. |
| A file cannot be opened | Record the attempted receipt time and that the contents could not be checked. Ask the person to confirm what they sent and provide a readable copy; record the time of any replacement. Do not mark the unreadable file as a confirmed valid submission. |
| A later copy arrives after 16:00 | Record its time separately. Do not substitute it for the last valid copy received by the deadline. Refer any question about the late copy to Heather. |

If the unreadable file was intended as a replacement, retain the earlier readable copy and its time while checking with the person. A later unreadable file does not establish a new valid version. Pass unresolved receipt questions to Heather with the recorded times; do not infer an answer from silence.

## Keep access and receipt separate

Reading the case on paper does not tell us how the memo was submitted or whether it was submitted. Use the receipt record and the readable memo to establish the submission, not the route used to read the case.
