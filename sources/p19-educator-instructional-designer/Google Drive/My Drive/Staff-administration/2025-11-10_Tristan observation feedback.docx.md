# Where to pause the explanation
Reading Conditions at Work | ELM-CRW-2025-DD-001 | v1.0 | 10 Nov 2025 | Heather Callahan  
To: Tristan McLeod

## Observed sequence

I sat in on the 10:00–10:50 lesson on 5 and 10 November. On the fifth, you put up the loan notice at 10:00 and read the reception memo aloud at 10:18. The first time everyone wrote a loan decision was 10:38. My 10:18 note marks the change of topic. I didn’t time the explanation as a continuous block that morning.

On the tenth, I timed 35 minutes of continuous explanation, from 10:00 to 10:35, covering the notice, the reception exception and how to read the register. At 10:26 you asked, “Any questions before we look at the register?” That gave people a chance to ask you something. There was no instruction for each person to choose a condition and write it down. Individual writing and the whole-group check took the remaining 15 minutes together. I didn’t time them separately.

## Agreement on 10 November

When we talked from 11:15 to 11:40, we agreed to pause the next lesson at 15 and 30 minutes from the start. Give each check three minutes and have everyone write before opening it up to the room.

- **15 minutes.** Ask for the usual return time and the line on the borrower notice that gives it. A time on its own leaves us guessing where the reader found it.
- **30 minutes.** Put the reception memo beside the register. Ask what the register establishes about the application and what entry would allow reception to promise a next-business-day return.

We can keep 35 minutes for explanation in three stretches. The two checks take six minutes, which leaves nine for drawing the decision together at the end.

## What to look for next

Ask the two source questions separately. “Where does the borrower notice give the usual return time?” Then, with the extension case, “Where would you find the record of the duty lead’s approval?” The first asks for the wording of the rule. The second asks for the record that would let staff use the exception.

At the next visit I’d note the passages or entries learners actually use in a few written examples, alongside the reasons they give. Keep those notes without names. These two visits tell us when people first had a chance to write and what we agreed to try. We’ll need to see the new sequence in use to comment on the resulting explanations.
