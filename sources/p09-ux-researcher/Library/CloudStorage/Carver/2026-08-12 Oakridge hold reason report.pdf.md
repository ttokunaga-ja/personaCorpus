# Carrying a hold reason across the handoff

Document: CWR-DR-20260812-RPT-01 v1.0  
Date: August 12, 2026  
Prepared by: Jennifer Salazar, Carver Workplace Research LLC  
Prepared for: Christine Wright, Oakridge Dispatch Cooperative

## Recommendation

In the next prototype, place a route-level Hold reason beside Held in the route list. Keep the source and the time checked in the selected route's detail. Where the reason for the relevant route and time has not been established, show Unknown rather than carrying forward an unsupported explanation. If a place to ask is known, make it available in the detail. Keep a stop instruction with its stop row.

At our August 12 discussion, you selected the position beside Held for the next prototype. This is a choice of what to try, not an approval to change the live screen or a release date. Ready and Held remain the status values in the proposal.

## What we heard and observed

The July follow-up included 24 individual interviews, with 12 people in each of two recent handoff-frequency groups. Jennifer and Brian reviewed the interview records together. Each person is counted once, even where the record includes more than one scene. Some accounts describe earlier work; others include a walkthrough during the interview. The counts describe these interviews, not a sample designed to estimate prevalence.

Fifteen people contributed observations supporting the recommendation through three distinct paths:

- For eight, the reason for the particular held route remained unconfirmed in the recorded scene. A vehicle type, a stop instruction, a status change or a contact for follow-up was not itself a reason.
- One learned the reason only after passing the held route to the next person. The later answer could not have been included in that handoff as it happened.
- Six first learned what was pending through an additional question and response. The route needed to be connected to the answer; an instruction to wait alone did not identify what was pending.

The other nine should not be treated as a single group with no difficulty. Four found a reason in material already available, such as a current handoff entry or a full message. Two already had an explanation and sought a missing detail or checked whether it was still current. One heard the reason orally before opening the list. Two described routes that stayed Ready during the handoff and did not encounter a hold in the scene discussed. These are different limits on what we can say about a reason display. In one known-reason example, the reason was available but the person who could release the hold was not established.

## From situation to display decision

| Situation at the handoff | Display decision to explore |
|---|---|
| No reason is available for the held route | Show Unknown beside Held. If a follow-up contact is known, identify it in route detail without presenting it as the reason. |
| A reason is available through another route, such as a handoff note or oral message | Carry the route-level reason beside Held when it can be associated with this route and time. Show its source and checked time in detail so the receiver can judge what was passed on. Keep stop-specific directions on the stop. |
| A reason may be old or its current applicability is unknown | Do not present an earlier day's explanation as the current reason merely because the route number matches. Use Unknown until the relevant reason is confirmed; in detail show a known place to check and, when available, the source and time of the confirmation. |

A reason describes what is pending. A source says where that information came from, and a contact may be a place to check it. None of those establishes who can release the hold. Unknown is not permission to release, and this recommendation does not add a release control or change authority.

## Next check and limits

Jennifer will take three conditions to Jeremy for the next prototype discussion: a confirmed reason with its source, a matching number with only a previous day's information, and an unknown reason with a known place to ask. Ask what the incoming dispatcher can pass on and where they would check; also listen for whether they confuse a stop instruction with the route reason or Unknown with permission to release. The display and update rules remain to be worked out through that work.

The interviews were allocated by recent handoff frequency, not sampled to represent all dispatchers. The 15 of 24 is a count of people in this study, not the share of all users who need a reason. Participants were not using this proposed display in the July interviews, and we did not measure its effect on calls or handoff outcomes. We therefore cannot estimate a reduction in calls or establish that this placement will improve performance. Those are questions for subsequent research.
