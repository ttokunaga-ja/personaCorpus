# Finding the place again

CWR-NS-20240926-RPT-01 · v1.0  
September 26, 2024  
Jennifer Salazar, Carver Workplace Research LLC  
Prepared for Ronald Perez, Kenton Contract Warehousing Inc

## Purpose and scope

Ronald asked us to follow six night-shift workers through an interruption and identify where they resumed the count. We visited the Chicago warehouse on September 4 and September 11, 2024, from 8:30 to 10:15 p.m. CDT on each date. We followed three people per visit, with their individual agreement, and recorded one interruption-to-resumption scene for each. The visit window is not six continuous individual observation periods. Jennifer recorded actions; Ayesha Siddiqui recorded consent and the observation boundaries. We did not record audio.

The day procedure provides a useful comparison, not a night-shift instruction. In the day process a fixed reconciliation clerk receives the paper circulation sheet, opens the bin in Count List, saves the reconciliation and marks the returned sheet. At night the worker reconciles and saves the count personally; there is no fixed clerk to receive the sheet when the worker is called away. A personal paper check at night is a cue used by the worker, not the day desk's handoff.

## What we observed

All six people had an interruption in the scenes followed. The table identifies the first cue used to find the place again, not every thing a person looked at. Times are local and recorded to the minute. The first cue and the first resumed reconciliation are separate events.

| Scene | Last confirmed → resumed bin | What happened after return | First cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| N01, Sep 4 | B-14 → B-15 | Returned at 20:53; found the B-14 mark on personal paper at 20:54 and resumed at 20:55. | Paper |
| N02, Sep 4 | C-07 → C-08 | Returned at 21:20, asked a colleague at 21:21 and resumed at 21:22. | Colleague |
| N03, Sep 4 | D-03 → D-04 | Returned at 21:52, checked the bin label at 21:53 and resumed at 21:54. D-04 had an entered quantity but had not been saved; the worker recounted and saved it. | Bin label |
| N04, Sep 11 | B-21 → B-22 | Returned at 20:46; used the fold to find the B-21 mark on paper at 20:47 and resumed at 20:48. | Paper |
| N05, Sep 11 | C-11 → C-12 | Returned at 21:14; a colleague said C-12 and the worker resumed in the same recorded minute. No conspicuous searching was observed. | Colleague |
| N06, Sep 11 | D-09 → D-10 | Returned at 21:55; followed the paper from the top to the D-09 mark at 21:56 and resumed at 21:58. | Paper |

Across these six scenes, the first cue was paper in three, a colleague in two and a bin label in one. Brian Chen and I compared independent first classifications: four of six initially matched. We resolved N03 as a bin label because that was used to identify D-04, even though the terminal also figured in the scene. We resolved N05 as a colleague because the spoken bin number was the cue, even though there was no conspicuous search.

## Interpretation

A return to the work area is not necessarily a return to reconciliation. N03 shows why an entered quantity cannot stand for a confirmed bin: D-03 remained the last confirmed bin while D-04 was still unsaved. The label helped locate D-04; it did not establish the quantity. N05 is an important counterexample to a claim that everyone searched on return: the colleague supplied C-12 and reconciliation began in the same recorded minute. Minute-level notes do not establish that the interval was zero seconds.

The observed cues suggest that a persistent reference to the last confirmed bin could help a worker orient after an interruption. They do not establish that the display will be noticed, used, or reduce time or errors.

## Recommendation

Consider one read-only line above Count List: **Last confirmed bin**. It would show the last bin whose reconciliation was saved. In the N03 sequence it would remain D-03 while a quantity was merely entered for D-04; after D-04 was reconciled and saved it would advance to D-04. The line is a reference, not a confirmation of an unsaved quantity or an instruction to skip a check.

We recommend this display alone for further consideration. An interrupting warning adds an action without identifying the line to resume; it could also interrupt a person who, like N05, can continue on a colleague's cue. We do not recommend automatic selection or confirmation, or transferring the day desk's circulation and stamp process to the night shift. This report does not decide implementation or a schedule.

## Limits and next observation

These six selected scenes describe what happened in the two visit windows, not the frequency of interruption across the night shift. We did not observe day shift, evening shift, or night work outside those windows. We cannot estimate occurrence rates or the effect of a display, including time saved, for those periods from these observations. If Kenton considers a wider deployment, observe additional work outside these windows and record return, first cue and resumed reconciliation separately before judging the proposal's reach or effect.
