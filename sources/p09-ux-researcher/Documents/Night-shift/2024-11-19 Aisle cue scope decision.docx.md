# Aisle cue scope decision

CWR-NS-20241119-DEC-01 · v1.0 · November 19, 2024 · Jennifer Salazar  
For Patrick Delgado and Jeremy Sutton

## Decision

Keep the last confirmed bin as a read-only reference in the paper proposal. Put the aisle of that Save next to the bin only when the aisle can be verified from the confirmation. If the aisle cannot be verified, use “Aisle unknown”. The aisle the worker is standing in does not establish the origin of the Save. Do not use a quantity entry as confirmation or let the cue select a row or save a count.

## Evidence and limits

| Record | What it supports |
|---|---|
| November 7 and 14 walks and Brian's return coding, CWR-NS-20241115-DEC-01 v1.0 | In three of four return scenes the worker used a confirmed bin from the aisle they came back to. Each of those calls has an observed Save in the return aisle and evidence the worker used that bin. In T3 the worker saved F-12 in F at 20:02 after checking the stock, then returned to E at 20:05. They used the E shelf label, stock and tally to resume checking E-22 at 20:08. A correct Save can be a poor guide to the next shelf in a different aisle. |
| T4, November 14 walk notes and event log | At 20:13 the worker entered a quantity at F-19. At 20:17 the worker returned to F using the confirmed F-18 as the cue. The F-19 Save came at 20:22 after a recount. Follow confirmation, not entry, when identifying the last confirmed bin. |
| November 18 paper walkthrough with Jeremy | The bin-only line did not tell me whether to continue in E. “Aisle at Save: F” let me locate the earlier Save, but it did not identify a row in E. On the unknown card we walked through the check at the shelf. Only Jeremy and I read the slips. This gives us a wording issue and a procedure to discuss, with no evidence of how workers would use the cards on shift. |

## What to walk through with Ronald

If the Save belongs to a different aisle or its aisle is unknown, start in the aisle the worker has returned to. Read the current shelf label, check the stock there and match both to the worker's own tally. Use the matched shelf to find the row and reconcile the quantity. The person doing the count presses Save after that check. If the label, stock and tally do not match, hold the Save and ask Ronald to clarify the shelf and row before continuing.

When the confirmed bin is from the same aisle, it can help the worker get oriented. The worker still checks the shelf and quantity before saving. Keep the proposed display distinct from the current Count List and use the current shelf check in the discussion with the floor team.

## Next check

On November 21 I'll walk Ronald through the T3 return. I want to settle what we should ask workers to check when the Save's aisle is unknown, including where to stop and whom to ask if the records disagree. I'll bring the verified and unknown paper slips to show the two conditions. The proposal leaves the display change and its timing open.
