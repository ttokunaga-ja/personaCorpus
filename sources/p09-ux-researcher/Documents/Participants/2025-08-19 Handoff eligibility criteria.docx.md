# What counts as receiving a shift

CWR-HO-20250819-DEC-01 · v1.0  
August 19, 2025  
From: Carolyn Davis  
For: Mihoko Kaneko and Jennifer Salazar

## Decision

Check each candidate in this order for the September comparison:

1. Has the person actually taken part in an operational shift handover in the last 30 days? Training alone does not count.
2. In that work, did the person receive a shift and take responsibility for work still needing action? Someone who only passes information on is not a receiving participant for this comparison.
3. Does the person normally use a shared screen to check that work? A receiver who relies only on paper or oral communication does not meet this screen-use condition.

Record the answer and the evidence for each condition. Stop the eligibility decision at the first unmet condition and count that person once under that reason. Later answers can be left unassessed; do not turn them into a second exclusion. Where an answer is unclear, check it before assigning a result.

## Why this boundary

Both prototypes ask people to receive work using a screen. Keeping ordinary screen use common to both makes the comparison more interpretable; it does not imply that other receiving practices are unimportant. The April handoff read used shift frequency to organize observations, not to assess skill. Neither job title nor handover frequency is a pass/fail or allocation rule here.

Carolyn will bring the coded eligibility check to the weekly meeting. Keep contact and scheduling records separate from the analysis codes.
