# What the two training rounds allowed
CWR-TC-20260925-DEC-01 · v1.0  
September 25, 2026 · Ayesha Siddiqui  
To: Mihoko Kaneko and Jennifer Salazar

## Decision
Keep the two sets of eight training slots as separate consent checks. Neither round involved audio or screen recording. All sixteen slots are outside the audio-expiry calculation; leave the audio expiry field blank, rather than assigning a date from another study.

## Scope of each check
| Round | Slots | Permitted material | Conditions and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| February 2025 first sessions | TC-01–TC-08; four on February 5 and four on February 6 | Coded operation notes, answers and short original-language excerpts | Ask each person at the start; retain only with that person's permission. Internal research use; Nakayama receives counts and a summary, not individual notes or quotations. No audio or screen recording. |
| June 2026 retest | TCR-01–TCR-08; four on June 16 and four on June 17 | Answer summaries and operation notes | Ask each new participant for this round; retain only with that person's permission. Internal use; Nakayama receives counts and summaries, not individual records. No audio or screen recording. |

## Basis and handling
The February permission sheet covers the first sessions; the June check explicitly calls for permission again for the retest. A client arrangement is not an individual consent, and neither sheet grants permission to record. The two rounds involved different new hires, so a permission from one cannot be carried into the other. The note categories above describe what could be retained with permission; they do not establish that an audio file exists.

For the September review, count these as sixteen consent-scope entries, not sixteen recordings to delete. The July dispatch register is a different study and does not confer audio permission here. If a slot's note permission needs further examination, return to that slot's own consent record; do not infer a yes from its inclusion in this review.
