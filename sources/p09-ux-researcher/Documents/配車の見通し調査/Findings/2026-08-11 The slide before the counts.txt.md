August 11 — readout rehearsal

I started with the seven counts. That sent the discussion straight to how big each group was, before I had explained which handoff needed the information. The numbers alone did not make the operational situation clear.

Start instead with a recipient passing a route on while the reason is still Unknown. A status can be passed on, but the reason cannot be supplied from the available information. Then contrast the one person who learned the reason after the handoff with the six who first learned what was outstanding through another question and answer. Neither sequence proves that a display would eliminate a call.

Only then put up the seven-category count. Ask Christine how she reads the nine comparison cases: four used existing material, two supplemented or checked freshness, one had been told before opening the list, and two did not encounter a hold. These are different kinds of comparison, not nine people with no problem.

If asked for the route or the participant behind an example: “For this discussion I can give you the anonymous behavior and the aggregate count. The individual records stay with the research team.” Use only the anonymous aggregate numbers in the readout.
