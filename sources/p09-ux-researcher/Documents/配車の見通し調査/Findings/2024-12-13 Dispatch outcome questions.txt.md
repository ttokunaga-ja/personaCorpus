Questions for Patrick — December 16

Christine's six routes give scheduled local windows, not actual arrivals. Can we answer “how many were late” from these materials? I do not see an actual arrival to pair with any of the six.

What operating period and routes belong in the count? For each movement, where would we obtain the corresponding scheduled window and actual arrival, including local date and time zone? What rule makes an arrival late: strictly after the window end, or another agreed threshold? How should missing or changed schedules be treated before counting?

R-615 shows why the date belongs in the comparison. The scheduled Indianapolis end is December 13, 00:30 EST. As a hypothetical comparison only, an arrival at December 13, 00:15 EST is before that end. If the end were incorrectly read as December 12, 00:30, the same arrival would appear after it. We do not have that actual arrival; this is not an observed delay.

The readback tells us what was or was not said in our internal exercise. It cannot supply an actual arrival or a count of late deliveries. Bring the schedule and readback record; keep an outcome claim separate from the handoff example.
