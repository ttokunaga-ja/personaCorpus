# Where the novice explanation breaks

Document CWR-DR-20250124-RPT-01 · Version v1.0  
January 24, 2025 · Jennifer Salazar  
Prepared for Patrick Delgado, Research Director

## Decision for the research review

Withdraw the October sketch, CWR-DR-20241031-DEC-01, as a novice-only explanation for returning to corroborate information at the dispatch desk. Brian and I found four people who handled ordinary and exception handoffs independently and still went back to compare the display with a source outside it. Keep the October reasoning visible in the withdrawn copy. The question for another review is what the dispatcher had in hand and what still needed corroboration before they passed the work on.

## The four counterexamples

Experience here comes from the work participants said they had done independently. For each of these four, their account covers both ordinary and exception handoffs. We used that scope of responsibility alongside what they said they compared in the particular handoff.

| Case and basis for experience | Comparison in the interview |
|---|---|
| UX-033 — described independently handing over routine routes and dealing with exceptions at handoff. | Checked the earlier time written on paper against the current Window. The departure from the earlier plan could be read by bringing the paper value and the current display together. |
| UX-037 — described taking responsibility for ordinary and exception handoffs independently. | Compared the message from the preceding dispatcher with Held. The participant had the message from the preceding dispatcher. Held supplied the visible state. |
| UX-042 — described managing ordinary and exception handoffs on their own, including when a separate answer was still due. | Began with the route and planned window. Later compared the number the vehicle coordinator gave orally with Vehicle and passed the replacement number on as confirmed. The coordinator's answer comes from the participant's account. |
| UX-043 — described receiving and passing on routine routes and exception work independently (28:26). | Compared a paper exception memo from the preceding dispatcher with the stop Note (20:16). The participant said the paper had an additional condition missing from the Note and that they conveyed it verbally. We have their description of the difference. The paper was never retained. |

The counterexamples cover time, status, vehicle and a stop condition. The source of corroboration varied across those tasks. Each case puts the work inside an experienced person's handoff, so the novice-only explanation cannot account for the records we have.

## How we read the interviews

On January 22, Brian Chen and I read ten interviews from October 2024 and two from January 2025 as twelve cases. The first passes matched on ten. A case qualified as Cross-check when the participant explained at least one comparison pair. The final counts were six Cross-check, three Other action and three Unresolved.

UX-034 came back to the same row. There was too little in the record to determine either a comparison or another task, so we agreed to leave it Unresolved. UX-041 is a Cross-check interview on the strength of four explained comparisons. Two of Adam's six returns remain unexplained individually: we saw the same row at 22 minutes and the stop 04 Note open at 27, with no comparison target established in either case.

## What to carry forward

The twelve interviews support an internal review of the sketch. The set was assembled to read handoff scenes and cannot provide job-title rates. It also leaves open how experience changes the kind or amount of corroboration people do. A screen return can serve other work: UX-044 returned to the list to select a different route after finishing the first handoff. Keep the action and its evidence together in the next read, leaving unexplained returns open. We have yet to choose a replacement grouping.
