# One stop condition that traveled by voice
CWR-DR-20260204-RPT-01 · v1.0 · February 4, 2026 · Jennifer Salazar
To: Christine Wright

## What we saw and heard
In one interview the participant opened the relevant stop row and its Note. The participant described an earlier handoff in which they compared a condition on paper with that Note and passed the additional condition verbally to the next dispatcher. The comparison and handoff are the participant's account, not actions we observed during the interview. We did not obtain the paper or its wording.

## The participant's account
| Moment | Anonymous participant's words |
|---|---|
| Comparison | “The paper had an additional condition. That part was missing from the stop Note on screen.” |
| Handoff | “When I handed over to the next person, I told them the additional condition verbally.” |
| Limit | “They heard it from me verbally; that is as far as I know. I did not see how they handled it afterward.” |

## What this example supports
This is one instance of a stop condition being carried by voice when an additional part was absent from the Note. It does not establish how often this happens or describe a Hold reason. The participant did not see what the next person did afterward, so the outcome of the handoff remains unconfirmed.
