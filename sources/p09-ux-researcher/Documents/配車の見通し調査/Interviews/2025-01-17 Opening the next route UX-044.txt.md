UX-044 — Opening the next route
January 17, 2025, 13:00–13:45 CST
Interviewer: Jennifer Salazar. Times are elapsed from the start.

00:00 Jennifer: I'd like to walk through a recent handoff with you. I can record the conversation for the research team if that's all right. You can ask me to pause or stop.
00:34 Participant: Yes, you can record.
00:49 Jennifer: We won't record the screen. Let's leave out any shipper names or actual addresses. If one comes up on screen, we'll stop sharing and pause the recording.
01:02 Jennifer: What part of the shift were you covering?
01:10 Participant: I was the one leaving the desk. We overlapped a bit, so the next dispatcher and I could sit down together. There were two routes I wanted to go through before I left.
02:03 Jennifer: Out of the last five shifts, on how many did you give or receive a handoff?
02:17 Participant: Two. This was the one where we had time to go through it at the desk.
02:44 Jennifer: Start where you started with her.
03:22 Participant: I picked the first route. I had a short list I'd made for myself of what to tell her. The route on screen was the one for the first item.
04:20 Jennifer: Take me through that item.
04:27 Participant: The delivery window had moved. I showed her the current Window. I said that's the time we're working to, but there was a call outstanding.
05:38 Jennifer: What was the call about?
05:43 Participant: The receiving side was going to call us back about whether they could take it then. We hadn't had that call. I could read her the time, but I couldn't say they'd agreed to it.
06:32 Jennifer: Was anything written beside the time that told her that?
06:49 Participant: She got the part about the call from me. My list was there to remind me what I still had to say.
07:04 Jennifer: How did you put it when you handed it over?
07:11 Participant: “That's the window on the plan. We're still waiting to hear from receiving. If they don't call back, follow up before you tell anyone they've agreed.” She wrote it down.
08:32 Jennifer: Anything more to do on that first route before you could move on?
08:38 Participant: I'd passed the time and the thing that was still open. We didn't need to sit there together until the call came.
09:45 Jennifer: What happened next?
09:50 Participant: I went back to the same route list. I was finished with the first route and wanted the other one.
10:31 Jennifer: Can you show me that bit?
10:49 [Participant returns to the route list. The first route is still the selected route.]
11:42 Jennifer: When you went back, what were you looking for there?
11:49 Participant: The second route. I selected its row. I wasn't going back to compare the first route's old time with the current time. I'd finished explaining the first one.
12:35 [Participant selects a different route. The Stops panel changes with the selection.]
13:06 Jennifer: How did you know that was the one to open?
13:12 Participant: That was the other route on my list to hand over. I was finding the row so I could tell her about that route. I didn't go back over the old time.
14:37 Jennifer: What did the next dispatcher need from that second route?
14:43 Participant: One of the stops had a door instruction. If the driver called, she needed to be able to tell him which door to use.
16:02 Jennifer: Show me where she would find it.
16:08 Participant: With the second route selected, I opened its stops. This line here. I read it to her from the Note.
17:21 Jennifer: What did you say?
17:27 Participant: “Use the east receiving door.” I told her to pass that on if the driver called. I pointed to which stop it went with.
18:43 Jennifer: How did that fit with the overall status of the route?
18:50 Participant: It was for that stop. I wasn't explaining the route's status with it.
20:03 Jennifer: What did the incoming dispatcher do with it?
20:10 Participant: She wrote “east receiving door” beside that stop. She said she'd give it to the driver if he called. I didn't hear a call between them before I left.
21:28 Jennifer: Was there a previous version of the instruction you needed to check?
21:35 Participant: No. I needed to get to the stop and read her the instruction. I didn't have another door written down that I was checking against it.
23:02 Jennifer: What happened after you showed her the stop?
23:09 Participant: We finished that route. We could get on with the rest of the desk handover.
24:08 Jennifer: Would the incoming person have been able to act on both things just from the route list?
24:27 Participant: She could see the time for the first route. The call was still open, and I'd have to tell her that. On the second she'd find the door in the stop, but she'd need to know which stop I was talking about.
25:20 Jennifer: Did you need her to settle anything about the second route?
25:27 Participant: No. Just pass on where to go if the driver asked. I don't know if he called later.
27:18 Jennifer: What did you want her to carry forward on the first one?
27:24 Participant: The current window. Receiving still had to call back. If the call didn't come, she knew which side to follow up with.
29:01 Jennifer: If I only saw you go back to the list between those two, what would I miss?
29:10 Participant: That I was done with one route and opening another. The list was where I could select it.
31:04 Jennifer: Do you always go back to move to the next route?
31:11 Participant: No, I might go back to check something. This time I went for the other route. I didn't need another look at the first to finish what I'd said.
32:28 Jennifer: How do you decide what the next person needs?
32:35 Participant: I try to leave them with what to do next. If someone's supposed to get back to us, say who it is. With the door, make sure you've got the right stop and the right instruction.
34:06 Jennifer: What would you have done if you didn't have any overlap?
34:13 Participant: I'd leave those things in the handover notes. I'd want the call marked as outstanding and the door instruction beside its stop. This time we could talk through it.
36:02 Jennifer: Let me read the order back. You gave her the current window and the outstanding call for the first route. You opened the list to switch to the second, selected it, and passed on the door instruction from its stop.
36:17 Participant: That's right. The call was for the first one. The door was for the second.
38:01 Jennifer: Anything I've left out about the return to the list?
38:09 Participant: It was just where I could open the other route. If you'd seen the first row come back onto the screen and stopped there, you'd have missed the part where I picked the other one.
40:10 Jennifer: That's everything I needed on the routes. Is there anything from this conversation you'd like the team to leave out?
40:20 Participant: No. We didn't say where the routes were going, so that's fine.
42:02 Jennifer: You can close the shared view. Thanks for taking me through what you said to her and where you left things.
42:15 Participant: Sure. Thanks.
44:30 Jennifer: I'll stop the recording. We're done here.
45:00 [End of session.]
