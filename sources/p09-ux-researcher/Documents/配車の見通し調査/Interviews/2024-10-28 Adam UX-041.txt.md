UX-041 | Adam Morales, Oakridge Dispatch Cooperative
October 28, 2024 · 13:00–13:45 CDT
Jennifer Salazar, interviewer. Times are elapsed from the start of the session. Opening before the recorder starts is from the room notes. Brackets mark pauses and actions observed during the session.

00:00 Jennifer: Thanks for coming, Adam. I'd like you to take me through one recent handoff, starting wherever it started for you. We'll have about forty-five minutes. If there's something you'd rather leave out, just say so and we'll go past it.
00:24 Adam: Sure. You want one where I was on the way out, or can it be one where I was taking over?
00:35 Jennifer: Either is fine. Pick one you remember well enough to go through in order. Before we get into it, may I record the audio for the research team to transcribe and review?
01:02 Adam: Yes, you can record it.
01:06 [Jennifer starts the audio recorder.]
01:18 Jennifer: You can ask me to pause it at any point. We can look at the route view together. I'm recording our voices only. Please keep shipper names and actual delivery destinations out of sight. If one comes up, we'll stop sharing and pause the recorder.
01:43 Adam: Okay. I've got the route view where we can talk about it without any of that.
02:05 Jennifer: What part of the handoff were you responsible for in the one you're thinking of?
02:20 Adam: I was the one coming off the desk. I had to tell the next dispatcher where the plan stood so they could take over. Some things were set, some things were still in progress, and I wasn't going to be there to answer for them later.
02:44 Jennifer: When you say “take over,” were they picking up the same run from you?
02:51 Adam: The same run, yes. I still had it when they came in. They were taking it from there.
03:04 Jennifer: In the last five shifts, how many times did you have a shift where you gave or received a handoff?
03:18 Adam: Four of the five. Let me count that again. Yes, four. There was one where I stayed with it and didn't have to hand it off.
03:51 Jennifer: For the example today, where do you start?
04:08 Adam: R-308. I had a time written down for it from earlier. The window had moved by the time I was handing it over. That's the one I remember because I still had the old time right in front of me.
04:32 Jennifer: Walk me through what you had at the desk before you started checking it. We can leave the list where it is for now.
04:44 [Adam moves a piece of paper beside him.]
04:56 Adam: There was this note I'd made for myself. The route was on the list. There was a replacement being worked out for it, and I'd had a message from dispatch about a call back. Those are the bits I had.
05:24 Jennifer: What was the first part you'd picked up yourself?
05:37 Adam: The time I'd written down. I write down the span we're working to when I'm going to have to refer to it a few times. I hadn't made that note for the next desk. It was just sitting where I could see it.
06:02 Jennifer: What did you know had changed when you came to hand over?
06:15 Adam: The window, for one. I wouldn't put the replacement and the call in the same bucket. Someone had said a number to me on one of those, and the other was still an answer we were waiting for.
06:41 Jennifer: Do you want to show me the part with the window now?
06:48 Adam: Yeah. Let me get my note where I can read it alongside this.
07:02 [Adam unfolds the note and traces a line with his finger. Jennifer waits while he reads.]
07:24 Adam: There's only one window written down here. If I handed you this paper, you'd have no way to tell that the plan had moved since I put it there.
07:43 Jennifer: Had you crossed the time out while you were working?
07:52 Adam: No, I left it. I knew that was the one I'd started with. I was going to give the next person the current one, so I needed to get that off the plan.
08:16 Jennifer: Take me through the check as you do it.
08:26 [Adam brings his attention back from the paper to the shared view. He looks through the list and pauses before pointing.]
09:00 Adam: Here's R-308 again. This is the current Window. I'm putting it alongside the time I wrote down earlier, this one on the paper.
09:24 Jennifer: What's the use of keeping the earlier one in view?
09:33 Adam: I can tell whether the time I'm about to give them is the one we were working to before, or the one we're on now. It's the one here on the screen that I want them to work to.
09:55 [Adam sets the note down. Jennifer writes while he waits.]
10:12 Jennifer: Where does the vehicle fit into that same handoff?
10:27 Adam: There was another vehicle being arranged for the run. Somebody from the vehicle side had told me the relief number out loud. I wanted to see which number the plan showed for R-308.
10:55 Jennifer: When you heard it, were you looking at the same row?
11:05 Adam: I don't remember exactly where I was looking right then. I got the number from them speaking to me. I remember going back to the row to see it there.
11:29 Jennifer: Show me what you did with the number you heard.
11:39 Adam: Give me a second. I need to get my place in the list again. I can hold the number in my head long enough to go and look for it.
12:02 [Adam shifts from the note to the screen. A pause while he locates the route.]
12:22 Jennifer: Say when you've reached the place you used.
12:29 Adam: Almost. Let me get the right line. With two things moving at once I want to be sure I'm saying which run it's for.
13:00 Adam: Back on R-308. Vehicle, there. That's the number I'd heard for the replacement. I'm comparing what they said with what's in Vehicle on the line.
13:24 Jennifer: What could you tell from seeing them match?
13:32 Adam: That we're talking about the same number. If I need to know where the arrangement stands, I have to go to the people doing the arranging. Reading it here doesn't tell me what they've said to each other.
13:58 [Jennifer notes Vehicle and the spoken number separately.]
14:15 Jennifer: What was still outstanding on the route itself?
14:29 Adam: Receiving was due to call back. Dispatch had sent that on to me. I didn't have the return call, just the message that it was expected.
14:53 Jennifer: Do you remember how that reached you?
15:06 Adam: It was passed along by the dispatcher. I'd want the next person to know that because otherwise they're left guessing where to ask when a call doesn't come through.
15:29 Jennifer: At that point what were you able to tell them about the situation?
15:42 Adam: “We're waiting for receiving to call back.” That's where I would stop. I couldn't tell them what receiving was going to say. I was trying to give them the part they could use.
16:10 [Adam pauses and looks down at the note. Jennifer waits.]
16:31 Jennifer: What did you do with that message before you passed it on?
16:45 Adam: I had it in mind when I went back to the route. I wanted to see how the route stood. Let me bring that up for you.
17:04 [Adam moves back toward the shared list. He pauses while finding R-308.]
17:31 Adam: This is the one where it's important to keep the message with it. If you just give them the word on the screen, they'll have to ask what we're waiting on all over again.
17:52 [Adam locates the route row and points to the status.]
18:00 Adam: There, Held on R-308. That's what I was looking at with the message from dispatch. The message was that we were still waiting for receiving to call back.
18:29 Jennifer: If the next person came to just this row, how much of that would they have?
18:40 Adam: They'd know the route was held. The message about the call back came from dispatch, so if I walk away without telling them that, they still have to find it out somewhere.
19:08 Jennifer: Had you established who would release the hold?
19:18 Adam: I hadn't checked that. I could say who gave me the waiting message. I couldn't say who was going to take the hold off.
19:42 [Jennifer writes. Adam waits with his hand on the trackpad.]
20:00 Jennifer: Show me how you got from that part of the work to what you handed over.
20:14 Adam: I was deciding how much the next person needed right then. Give them the run, give them the time, tell them what needs following. I don't want to make them read back through my whole shift.
20:41 Jennifer: Take a moment if you need to get back to your place.
20:52 [Adam looks down and moves a sheet beside the note. Both are quiet while he reads.]
21:16 Adam: Hang on a second. I want to go back through it in the order I did it. I keep thinking of the end because that's the bit I said to the other dispatcher.
21:43 Jennifer: Okay. Go on when you're ready.
22:00 [Adam returns to the same R-308 row. Jennifer marks the return in her notes.]
22:24 [Adam leaves the pointer still briefly, then moves on.]
22:39 Jennifer: What happened after that?
22:48 Adam: I was thinking about the stops too. There's the run as a whole, and then there's the part you need for a particular stop. If I'm taking over, I'd rather know which one somebody means.
23:15 Jennifer: What do you mean by “the part you need”?
23:28 Adam: Sometimes there's an instruction that only makes sense at that stop. If all you give me is the status for the run, I'm still missing that instruction. It depends on what the next person has to do.
23:57 Jennifer: Can you carry on with where you went in this one?
24:12 Adam: Yeah. I want to show you the place I mean. Let me leave the other details closed.
24:35 [Adam stops speaking and checks which part of the shared view is visible. Jennifer waits.]
24:58 Adam: Okay, I can show the stop row. I won't read out the destination.
25:13 Jennifer: That's fine. Show me the place you went to and tell me as much as you need to about the handoff.
25:33 [Adam pauses over the view, then moves back toward the route list.]
26:05 Adam: It's under the route. It takes a moment to get the right stop in view.
26:27 Jennifer: Take your time.
26:44 [Adam moves the pointer back toward the route list.]

27:53 Jennifer: Tell me which bit the call belongs to, in your words.

30:37 Jennifer: How would you handle that if they were already sitting there?
30:47 Adam: I'd put the current one in front of them and say which time I'm giving them. I don't want to leave two pieces of paper around with two times and make them decide.
31:15 [Adam keeps his place in the draft and looks back up. A pause while Jennifer finishes her note.]
31:40 Jennifer: Go ahead with the check you made then.
31:48 Adam: Let me get back to the route so I can put the two in front of me. I've still got the draft here.
