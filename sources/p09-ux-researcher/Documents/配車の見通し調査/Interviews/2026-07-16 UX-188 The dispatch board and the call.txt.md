# The dispatch board and the call

UX-188 · Interview 23 · July 16, 2026, 10:00–10:45 CDT
Jennifer Salazar and participant · 3 of last 5 shifts with a handoff
Team transcript. Team re-listening through January 12, 2027. No permission for customer sharing or use in another study.

Both calls to the departure coordinator are recounted by the participant. Brackets describe events in the interview. Times are elapsed from the start; 25:35 (24:35 in the recording) is where the participant gives the answer from the second call.

00:45 [Before recording: participant agreed to take part and to recording for transcription and re-listening within the research team.]

## The shifts and the route

01:00 [Recording starts. Jennifer waits for the participant to settle back before asking the first question.]
01:05 Jennifer: How many of your last five shifts included giving or receiving a handoff?
01:16 Participant: Three shifts. I'm counting each shift once.
01:27 Jennifer: Could you talk me through how you counted? You don't need to give me the days or anybody's name. I just want to check what you included in a shift with a handoff.
01:43 Participant: I'm thinking of when I actually gave somebody the work or took it from them. If somebody asked me a question and then kept working their own list, I didn't count that. Sometimes you're both there and talking and it sounds like a handoff, but nothing has changed hands.
02:07 Jennifer: And if somebody gave you the work and later you gave that same work to the next person?
02:21 Participant: That's still one shift on this count. The reason I said three is that I'm putting a mark by each shift where it happened. I'm not adding up the number of people I spoke to. [Brief pause while the participant counts back through the five shifts.] Yes, three.
02:53 Jennifer: Let's pick one route you actually had to pass on. Take a second to put yourself back at the board.
03:10 Jennifer: What was the route, and what did you see first?
03:22 Participant: R-501. It was Held on the dispatch board when I looked. I was trying to work out what the incoming dispatcher would need to know.
03:40 Jennifer: Start with the board and stop when you reach the first thing you did away from it. If you remember looking but don't remember what was in a field, leave that field unread for me.
03:58 Participant: Sure. I would start with the number, because that's what the next person can actually find. I don't start by saying “that held one.” [Pauses while locating the route on the displayed list.] Here, I'd be reading across this line. R-501, Held. That's the part I remember from that handoff.
04:27 Jennifer: What were you trying to decide by reading across there?
04:37 Participant: How much I could write down without sending the person off to do the same checks I was doing. If I can only give them the status, they at least know which route I mean. But if they ask “what are we waiting on?” and I just point at the board again, we're both looking at the same thing.
05:08 Jennifer: Show me where you'd stop reading the entry and go looking for an answer. You can narrate it without opening anything from that shift.
05:23 Participant: After Held. I'd take the route number with me. I wouldn't carry a reason over just because some other route on the list happens to be held, too. The number is what ties my next question to this row.
05:48 [Jennifer repeats the route number quietly while entering it in her notes.]
06:00 Jennifer: What did the board tell you about the hold?
06:12 Participant: It told me it wasn't ready to go. I couldn't see why, so I called the departure coordinator.

## The first call

06:26 Jennifer: Before we get to what they said, what were you hoping they'd tell you?
06:38 Participant: I wanted something I could leave with the route. If it was only that I wasn't to send it, I already had that much. I don't remember going into the call with a particular thing I thought it was. It was more that I was coming up to the handoff and this was an open item.
07:07 Jennifer: When you say an open item, does that mean you were about to change the board, or that you still had a question?
07:21 Participant: A question. I didn't have anything that told me to change the board. I was looking for what to tell the other dispatcher, so I wanted the answer to stick to the route number. It's easy to remember that you called and forget exactly what you asked.
07:50 Jennifer: Think of what you could write with the phone still in your hand. What would already be there before you heard the answer?
08:05 Participant: The number and Held. I could say I'd called the departure coordinator once I made the call. I couldn't yet put down that they gave me a reason. Those are different lines for me, even if I'm saying them all out loud at the end.
08:32 [Jennifer gives the participant a moment to recall the opening of the call.]
08:44 Jennifer: You don't have to reproduce how the coordinator said every word. If all you remember is the short answer, we can work with that.
09:05 Jennifer: Tell me about that first call.
09:16 Participant: I asked about R-501. The answer was, “It can't go yet.” That matched the status, but it didn't explain the wait.
09:34 Jennifer: Did you take that as a fresh update, or did it leave you where you were before you called?
09:46 Participant: It left me with the same thing for the handoff. Hearing somebody say it is helpful in the sense that I don't think I've misread Held. But imagine I turn to the other dispatcher and say, “I called. It can't go yet.” They still have to ask me the next question.
10:16 Jennifer: What would that question be?
10:23 Participant: What's stopping it? Or what am I supposed to check before I tell anybody it can go? I didn't have either answer from that call. I could tell them who I'd reached, but that's just my contact, not an explanation of the hold.
10:50 Jennifer: If you had to stop the example right there, what would you put down as the source for what you knew?
11:05 Participant: The Held on the board was what I saw. The first call was what I was told. They both pointed to keeping it from going out. I'd put both down if I needed to tell somebody I'd checked, but I wouldn't put a cause next to them. There's no extra piece of information in the words “can't go yet.”
11:37 Jennifer: Let me read back what I have. You could establish the state and that the call agreed with it. I'm leaving a space after “waiting for” because you haven't given me anything to put there yet.
11:57 Participant: Right. I'd leave that out of the handoff if that were all I had. A blank is better than sending somebody to check the wrong thing.
12:20 Jennifer: At that point did you know what was blocking it?
12:30 Participant: No. I knew not to send it, but not what had to happen before it could leave.
12:44 Jennifer: Say you had handed over at that exact point. Can you give me the words you would have used then, just for comparison?
12:58 Participant: “R-501 is Held. I checked with departure and they said it can't go yet.” Then if they said, “Did they say why?” I'd have to say no. I could admit I hadn't got any further. I wouldn't tell them to look for something specific just to make it sound like I'd finished.
13:30 Jennifer: That's what you could have said then. I'll keep it separate from your account of what you eventually did say. Did the first call tell you who could make the decision to release it?
13:48 Participant: It didn't tell me that. I know who I called. That doesn't mean that person made the decision or could change the status. I wouldn't tell the next dispatcher that just because that's the person who answered me.
14:15 Jennifer: Can you take me from the short answer to the point where you decided to try again? If the time between the calls is fuzzy, the order is enough.
14:30 Participant: I don't have the minutes between them. The first call was done and I still had the same thing to write. [Pauses to think.] When I thought about what the next person would need to carry, I realized I'd basically be telling them that a Held route was held. That was the gap.
15:01 Jennifer: You had enough to leave it on the board, then.
15:06 Participant: Yes. I just didn't have anything new to tell them.
15:15 Jennifer: What did you do with that answer?
15:27 Participant: I kept it as a status, not a reason. Later I called the departure coordinator again because I still needed something useful to pass on.
15:44 Jennifer: What would count as useful there? Try to answer with what you were looking for at the time, before you knew what they'd say back.
16:00 Participant: Some condition, I suppose. Something to hear back about or something to see change, so the person taking the list isn't just refreshing it and wondering. It could be that the coordinator doesn't know, either. In that case “we don't know yet” is still more honest than a guess. I needed to ask the question in a way that let them actually answer it.
16:36 Jennifer: How did you know you were still talking about R-501 as you got ready to call again?
16:51 Participant: That's the route number I had with the Held line. I wouldn't ask “any news?” and expect somebody to pick out the right route for me. Even if I just spoke to someone, a different route could be the one they're thinking about now.
17:17 Jennifer: When you went to call again, did you still have the number in front of you, or were you dialing from memory?
17:31 Participant: I went back to the line. I could remember that it ended in one, but I didn't want to get on the phone and suddenly doubt the rest of it. I'd rather take a second there and ask the whole question in one go. [Looks back at the route list before continuing.] Here it's R-501.
18:00 Jennifer: When you reached the coordinator the second time, did you start by reading anything from the board?
18:17 Participant: The number. The question after the number was different this time.
18:35 Jennifer: What did you ask the second time?
18:46 Participant: I asked what we were waiting for on R-501, not just whether it could go. I wanted to distinguish the answer from the board's Held.

## Getting the reason on the second call

19:08 Jennifer: You said you changed the question. If you'd heard “can't go yet” again, would you have treated that as a reason because it was the second call?
19:23 Participant: No. I'd still have to ask what we're waiting on. The fact that I called twice only tells you about my work. It doesn't tell you what the route was waiting for.
19:45 Jennifer: Let's keep the calls on separate lines. I'll read the first line and you tell me if I put anything on it too early. R-501, Held when you checked the board. You called the departure coordinator. They said the route couldn't go yet.
20:12 Participant: Yes. I hadn't heard the thing about the bay then. It sounds obvious now that I know the end of the story, but I didn't leave that first call knowing I'd have to wait for a bay.
20:36 Jennifer: On the next line I have “called again and asked what we were waiting for.” Did the second check replace the route number, or did you stay with that same entry?
20:55 Participant: Same entry. The question was about R-501 both times. I wasn't checking whether the first person had given me the status for a different route. I was trying to get more than a status for this one.
21:21 Jennifer: If we were listening only for where you found the reason, should the board entry be part of that answer?
21:36 Participant: It got me to the question, sure. It didn't answer it. That's what I'd want somebody taking over to know. Don't look for a line saying that on the board just because I told you the reason while I was pointing at the board.
22:02 [Jennifer checks her notes and points to the first-call line.]
22:10 Jennifer: Did the first call mention a departure bay?
22:20 Participant: No. That's why I wouldn't put the later answer into the first call. At first all I had was “can't go yet.”
22:38 Jennifer: Did you come away from that first call with anything else that could tell you what was happening at departure? I'm asking about what you actually had then.
22:56 Participant: No. If I tried to give you more now, I'd be putting what I know from later back into it. At the time I just held the route and didn't have a condition to pass on yet. So I'd stick with the words I remember from that call.
23:25 Jennifer: Show me where you'd have the number in front of you for the second question. Then take me through the reply from that point.
23:40 [The participant locates R-501 on the board and traces the line with a finger before looking back up.]
23:54 Participant: I'd read it from here. I'd ask about this route, what are we waiting on for it, and then I'd listen for something that went beyond “can't go.” I didn't need them to describe the whole board back to me. It was that one missing bit I was trying to get.
24:26 Jennifer: When you repeat it for the next person, how do you make clear it came from a call? Say it first as you remember hearing it, then we'll come back to what you told the dispatcher.
24:47 Participant: Let me think. I want to say “waiting for” here. I don't want to make it sound like I'd been told to use a particular bay or that one had opened. They were explaining the wait. [Pauses to recall the wording.] And that was on the second call, after I'd asked the more specific question.
25:21 Jennifer: Okay. In your own words, what did you learn then?
25:35 Participant: On the second call the departure coordinator told me we were waiting for a departure bay to become free. That's when I knew what the wait was. The first “can't go yet” didn't say that.
25:57 Jennifer: Did they give you a bay number?
26:01 Participant: No, I didn't get a number.
26:10 Jennifer: Did they say the bay was free by then?
26:19 Participant: No, it was still a wait. I didn't take it as permission to send the route.
26:33 Jennifer: If you were to put the reason alongside the status, how would you keep the next person from hearing it as an instruction to send it?
26:49 Participant: I'd start with Held, because that's where I left it. Then I'd explain what departure said it was waiting for. I wouldn't say “use a departure bay,” because that sounds like you've got a bay and I'm telling you where to take the route. I was leaving the next person with the condition, not an instruction that the condition had been met.
27:25 Jennifer: Is there anything you can tell me about when the condition would be met, based on that conversation alone?
27:37 Participant: No. Waiting for one to become free is all I had. It's a better answer than the first one for the handoff, but it's not an estimate. I don't want to stick a time to it because somebody would then plan on that time.
28:05 Jennifer: Let me try saying that back as a note. “Held. Departure says it's waiting for a bay to open.” What would you want me to keep if I only had room for a short line?
28:24 Participant: Keep R-501 on it. And keep that it was departure that told me. If you put “bay” with no other words, it could sound like directions to a bay. Keep “waiting for a bay.” That's the part I came back with.
28:53 [Jennifer reads the short line under her breath and finishes writing.]
29:06 Jennifer: I've got that as something you heard before you handed over. Let's go forward to the handoff.

## The handoff and the limit of the account

29:20 Jennifer: What did you pass to the incoming dispatcher?
29:32 Participant: R-501 was Held, and the departure coordinator had said it was waiting for a departure bay to open. I said that came from the second call.
29:53 Jennifer: Did you have to show the dispatcher anything to get that across, or are you describing what you told them?
30:07 Participant: I'm describing what I told them. I can point you to the Held line now because that's where I started, but the part about the bay is from the conversation. I didn't show them a bay on the board. I wouldn't want you to write down that I did.
30:35 Jennifer: Suppose they look at that same line after you leave and all it gives them is Held. What do they know from you that they wouldn't get by opening the route?
30:53 Participant: What was still outstanding when I left it, and who I'd heard it from. They could check if that had changed. I don't know whether they did. The thing I wanted to avoid was leaving them to start out with the same “what's going on with this one?” I had at the beginning.
31:23 Jennifer: If I write “departure's update” beside the reason, does that also tell me who had the authority to release the route?
31:37 Participant: No. That's just where the answer came from. I wasn't passing over a release decision. Whoever was handling it after me would still have to check what had happened.
32:05 Jennifer: Did you have a time for when that would happen?
32:14 Participant: No. I didn't give a time, and I didn't say I'd seen it released.
32:27 Jennifer: Can you remember what you deliberately left out of your handoff? We've covered the time. Is there anything else you might have known in another case that you didn't know in this one?
32:47 Participant: I didn't give them a bay number. I didn't give them the name of a person who'd cleared it, either. Those would both have sounded like something had been decided. I had enough to explain the wait, and I tried to leave it there.
33:14 Jennifer: How would you answer if somebody asks you now, “Was the bay the cause of the delay?”
33:27 Participant: I'd say departure told me they were waiting for a bay. I didn't go see it myself. I couldn't tell you what was occupying the bays or what made one take longer to clear. I'm telling you what I had to work with.
33:55 Jennifer: That's helpful. If we go back to the board one last time, show me the entry as you would have left it. We don't need to open any other route.
34:13 [The participant finds R-501 again. Jennifer waits while the participant reads the row rather than prompting with a status.]
34:28 Participant: This line here. The part I want you to see is Held. What I learned halfway through the calls didn't turn the line into a different kind of entry. I'd have left the hold on the list just as it was while I handed the work over.
34:56 Jennifer: When I put “board unchanged” here, I mean what you saw up to the handoff. Is that as far as you can take me?
35:11 Participant: Yes, up to when I gave it to the next dispatcher.
35:20 Jennifer: Did the board change before you handed over?
35:29 Participant: I still had it as Held when I passed it on. What changed for me was the information I got on the call.
35:49 Jennifer: You've got the list open again now. Without looking at my notes, show me exactly where you'd send the incoming dispatcher for each part of what you told them.
36:02 Participant: I'd point right here for the number and the state. If they wanted to know whether a bay had opened since the call, I'd send them to departure. I'd tell them I spoke to departure twice, because I don't want them to think the first answer was about the bay.
36:30 Jennifer: Did you use the window or the vehicle value to decide which explanation to pass on?
36:44 Participant: No. Whatever else was on that line, I couldn't get the bay from it. I needed the number to ask about the right route. The rest of what I told the next person came from what I heard after I asked again.
37:12 Jennifer: I'm going to look at the two entries in my notes before I read them back. [Paper shifts. Jennifer checks both entries against the route number while the participant keeps the board open.]
37:33 Participant: Can you make sure the first one still says “can't go yet”? If it just says “called departure” in both places, it won't show why I tried twice.
37:52 Jennifer: Yes, I've kept those words with the first one. The second entry has “waiting for a departure bay to become free.”
38:15 Jennifer: Let me check: first call, “can't go yet”; second call, waiting for a free departure bay. The reason came from the coordinator, not from the row.
38:33 Participant: That's right. The first answer and the second aren't the same amount of information.
38:47 Jennifer: Which of those would you have felt able to carry forward if you only had time for the first call?
38:58 Participant: The state only. I could put in that I'd asked departure and got no reason back, so the incoming person knew where I'd tried. I couldn't give them the same note I actually ended up giving them.
39:24 Jennifer: There are two things I want to leave open on my side. Whether you saw it leave, and whether you know how the next person handled it. You said the first was outside what you saw. How much of their side did you see?
39:45 Participant: I was there to hand it to them. I'm not saying I watched them go through all their next checks. Once I told them where I'd got the wait from, the rest of it was on their list.
40:09 Jennifer: Did they report anything back to you that you're using to fill in the rest of this account?
40:22 Participant: No. What I'm giving you as the reason is what the coordinator said to me the second time. I wouldn't take a later guess about what happened on the next shift and tack it onto that.
40:44 Jennifer: All right. I'll read back the last part separately so I don't accidentally record a follow-up that you didn't see.
41:05 Jennifer: Do you know what the incoming dispatcher did afterward?
41:14 Participant: No, I only know what I told them.
41:27 Jennifer: Before we stop, I'd like to make sure the wording still sounds like your experience. “Saw Held, heard the state confirmed, asked what was outstanding, heard the bay was still pending, and handed over the reason with the source.” Is any step missing or a bit too strong?
41:55 Participant: “Bay was still pending” might sound like one was about to be assigned to us. I'd keep the wording about waiting for one to become free. That's what departure told me. Other than that, yes. And we didn't take anything off hold in the part I'm telling you about.
42:27 Jennifer: I'll keep “waiting for a departure bay to become free” and leave the release and whatever happened after your handoff open. [Jennifer makes the edit and reads the changed phrase back quietly.]
42:49 Participant: That's what I meant.
43:00 Jennifer: Anything you'd like to correct or leave out?
43:09 Participant: No. That distinction between the calls is right.
43:22 Jennifer: All right. The recording stays with the research team, as we discussed before I started it. I'll finish my notes with the route number and the point where you first had something you could pass along.
43:44 Participant: Okay. Glad we went through it in order. I think if I'd just told you we were waiting for a bay, it would have sounded as if there was nowhere else I had to look.
44:05 Jennifer: I have the board first and the coordinator's two answers in order. I won't need you to pull up anything else.
44:20 Participant: Okay. I'll close the route list.
44:30 Jennifer: Thank you for taking me through it.
44:39 Participant: Sure. Thanks.
44:45 [The participant closes the list. Jennifer waits for the screen share to clear and reaches for the recorder.]
45:00 [Recording ends.]
