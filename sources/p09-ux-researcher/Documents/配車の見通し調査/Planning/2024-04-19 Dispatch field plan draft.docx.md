# Dispatch handoff observation plan
CWR-DR-20240419-OBS-01 | v0.1 | April 19, 2024 | Jennifer Salazar

For Patrick's review

## Purpose
Follow how a dispatcher arrives at a handoff decision, and what the next person can use. Christine's four situations give us places to look, not a count of problems. We will not rate a handoff by operation time.

## Observation focus

| Situation | Receiver's decision | Possible clues | What the interviewer records |
|---|---|---|---|
| Changed window | Which window should be carried into the next shift? | Current window in the plan; the value passed by the sender; the source of either value. | The values in sequence, where each came from, and what the receiver repeats or uses. |
| Held | What needs attention before the route can proceed? | Status, the person's account of the reason, and another person or record consulted. | What is visible, what the receiver understands, and where they look for the reason. Did they call the sender? Record the answer and what was learned. |
| Replacement vehicle | Can the incoming dispatcher treat the vehicle as settled? | Vehicle shown in the plan and any spoken confirmation. | Vehicle checked: note the displayed assignment and oral confirmation together, including the number and who said it was settled. |
| Exception stop | Does the next person need to act on a stop instruction? | Stop row, Note and the outgoing handoff. | Whether the stop was opened, what was read, and the words passed to the next person. |

## Session approach
Invite the dispatcher to walk through a recent handoff, starting with the situation as they remember it. Ask what they saw and what they did next; let them use their own terms. Follow the information through to the next person rather than stopping at the screen. Record what is actually said or shown alongside our interpretation, and mark gaps for follow-up. Do not infer a hold reason from the stop Note.

## Questions for Patrick
Is the table specific enough to distinguish a visible assignment from a decision the receiver can act on? Are there places where the question itself supplies a likely route to an answer?
