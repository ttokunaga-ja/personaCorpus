Overnight handoff read-through — notes for Mihoko
December 5, 2024, 17:00–17:35 CST / December 6, 08:00–08:35 JST
Jennifer Salazar and Mihoko Kaneko

We took turns as speaker and receiver and read the six planned examples in English and Japanese. This was our preparation, not a participant session or a record of actual trips. The first task asked for a weekday and time window; the route sheet was the source.

R-611 and R-614: one receiver repeated “Friday, six to seven”; in Japanese the corresponding readback was 「金曜の6時から7時」. Both are consistent with the task's weekday-and-clock table. The words alone do not distinguish Detroit December 6 from December 13. Pointing to a card afterwards would not establish which calendar date was understood from that readback.

R-612: Friday 05:45–06:30 EST. R-613: Friday 04:30–05:00 CST. R-616: Friday 08:00–08:45 CST. We read each in turn; these are the windows on the sheet, not evidence of an actual arrival.

R-615 was a different question. The window starts Thursday December 12 at 23:30 EST in Indianapolis and ends at 00:30. We asked separately which date belonged to the end: Friday December 13. This is an endpoint crossing midnight, not the same ambiguity as the two matching Friday clock windows.

For discussion: the first prompt collects too little to distinguish the two Fridays. Keep the initial words separate from any answer after the sheet is checked. Consider asking for both local calendar dates and, when a window crosses midnight, a date for each end.
