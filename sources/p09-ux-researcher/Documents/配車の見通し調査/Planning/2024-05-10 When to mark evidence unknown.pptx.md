Speaker notes: Jennifer and Ayesha, use these short cases to practice separating what we heard from our interpretation. They are exercises, not participant records.

## Slide 2
Layout: Table
Title: Case 1 — explanation only
| Decision element | Evidence in this case | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle displayed | Person says Van 28 was on the plan; screen not shown. | Reported number: Van 28; display not observed. |
| Basis for settled status | Person says “I knew it was settled”; no source or process given. | Basis unknown. |
| Confirmation source shown | None shown or named. | Unknown. |

Observation: “I knew it was settled”; participant reports Van 28 on plan.
Interpretation: Person describes the assignment as settled; how they knew is unknown.
Speaker notes: An explanation of the conclusion is not yet an account of its basis. Ask an open follow-up if possible; do not mark confirmation absent.

## Slide 3
Layout: Table
Title: Case 2 — source shown
| Decision element | Evidence in this case | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle displayed | Person points to Van 28 on the plan. | Van 28 observed on plan. |
| Basis for settled status | Person identifies the reply they used to treat the assignment as settled. | Basis reported: that reply. |
| Confirmation source shown | Person shows where the reply can be checked. | Source identified and shown; record what it actually says. |

Observation: Person points to Van 28 and shows the source of the reply they relied on.
Interpretation: The person used the identified reply as their basis; record its content before interpreting it as confirmation.
Speaker notes: Showing where to check and explaining the basis are evidence of the person's process. Read the reply itself before asserting what it establishes.

## Slide 4
Layout: Table
Title: Case 3 — could not ask
| Decision element | Evidence in this case | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle displayed | Van 28 visible on the plan. | Van 28 observed on plan. |
| Basis for settled status | Session ended before the question could be asked. | Unknown. |
| Confirmation source shown | Not elicited. | Unknown. |

Observation: Van 28 was visible; the question was not asked before the session ended.
Interpretation: We do not know whether or how the person confirmed it.
Speaker notes: We could not ask is not the same as the person did not check. Never convert missing evidence into a negative action.

## Slide 5
Layout: Heading and text
Title: Keep the boundary visible
Body: Record the displayed number, the person's words and any source separately. Mark each missing element unknown. If the source is shown, preserve what it says before deciding what it supports. Use the same distinctions when comparing cases.
Speaker notes: Ask Jennifer and Ayesha to point to the exact observation behind each entry. The three scenarios are practice examples, not study findings.
