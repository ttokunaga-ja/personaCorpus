# Slide 1 — Cover

Title: Four ways a handoff can break

Subtitle: Dispatch handoff study · CWR-DR-20240422-BRF-01 · v1.0 · April 22, 2024 · Jennifer Salazar

Audience: Patrick Delgado and Mihoko Kaneko

Speaker notes: Christine brought these situations to our April 16 discussion. They frame what we need to look for; they are not participant findings.

# Slide 2 — Two columns

Title: What the list shows, and what the next person needs

Left column — Visible on the current plan:
- Route, Plan status, Window and Vehicle in the route list.
- For a selected route, Stops and the free-text Note on each stop.
- R-214: Held, 14:00–16:00, Van 28; stop 03 Note: “Use east receiving door.”

Right column — Still to establish in the handoff:
- Which window the sender used and where it came from.
- What explains a hold and where the receiver checks.
- What makes a replacement assignment confirmed.
- Whether a stop instruction was actually passed to the next person.

Speaker notes: Window and Vehicle show current values, not a history or confirmation. There is no dedicated hold-reason or reviewer field. The stop Note is an instruction for that stop, not the reason for Held.

# Slide 3 — Diagram

Title: 1. A changed window, an old handoff

Left diagram: Three boxes in a single left-to-right chain. Box 1 says “Earlier window”; box 2 says “Sender's handoff: earlier window”; box 3 says “Next dispatcher: earlier window”. An arrow from box 1 to box 2 and another from box 2 to box 3 show the old value being passed along. Above the chain, a separate box says “Current plan: changed window”; a downward arrow from it points to box 2, labelled “check whether used”. The current-plan box is not connected directly to the receiver.

Right text: The list shows the current Window. A prior value can still be the one handed on. Trace where each value came from and which one reached the receiver.

Speaker notes: We do not yet know how often this happens. Ask for the actual sources, rather than assuming the list was consulted.

# Slide 4 — Diagram

Title: 2. Held without its reason

Left diagram: Box “Route list: Held” points to box “Next dispatcher sees status”. From that box a question arrow points to box “Where is the reason checked?” No reason or reviewer is shown inside the route list.

Right text: Held is visible; the reason is not in a dedicated field. Follow what the receiver sees and whom they approach to understand the hold.

Speaker notes: We have not established who can release a hold. Do not supply a person or contact method when asking.

# Slide 5 — Diagram

Title: 3. Replacement visible, confirmation uncertain

Left diagram: Box “Vehicle: Van 28” points to box “Receiver reads vehicle number”. A separate dashed arrow from the receiver box points to box “Evidence assignment is confirmed?” Label the dashed arrow “ask how they know”.

Right text: A vehicle number can be read from the current plan without establishing that the replacement is settled. Record the displayed assignment and the basis for a confirmation judgment separately.

Speaker notes: Do not treat seeing Van 28 as proof of confirmation. The diagram describes the distinction Christine asked us to examine, not an observed participant action.

# Slide 6 — Diagram

Title: 4. An exception stays at the stop

Left diagram: Three boxes in sequence: “Open R-214 Stops” → “Read stop 03 Note: Use east receiving door” → “Tell next dispatcher”. Mark the final arrow with a question mark, indicating that transfer needs checking, not that it is known to have happened.

Right text: Opening the route, reading the Note and passing its instruction on are distinct actions. A Note may remain on the stop while the handoff omits it.

Speaker notes: The Note describes the receiving door. It is not a reason for the Held status. Ask what actually reached the next dispatcher.

# Slide 7 — Table

Title: Evidence to retain for each scene

| Scene | Evidence to capture |
|---|---|
| Changed window | The source of the value actually passed to the next dispatcher, alongside the current value. |
| Held | The person or source the receiver first checks for the reason. |
| Replacement vehicle | The evidence the receiver uses to regard the assignment as confirmed, separate from the number displayed. |
| Exception stop | The instruction actually passed to the next dispatcher, if any. |

Speaker notes: These are questions prompted by Christine's report, not findings from research participants. Leave a judgment unconfirmed if its basis cannot be established. Our measure of success is understanding the handoff, not reducing operation time.
