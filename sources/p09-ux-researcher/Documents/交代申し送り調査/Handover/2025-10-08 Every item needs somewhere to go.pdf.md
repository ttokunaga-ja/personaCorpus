# Every item needs somewhere to go

CWR-HO-20251008-RPT-01 · v1.0  
8 October 2025 · Jennifer Salazar  
To: Jeremy Sutton and Patrick Delgado

## What the explanations leave open

In the September comparison the same ten people used both prototypes. Saving within seven minutes was the completion measure; a saved handoff did not establish that every status was understood. These are two explanations given after saving under the list condition, not observations of work completed by the external party.

| Observation and answer | Design question |
|---|---|
| HH-03 treated the item with no deadline as outside today's handoff. | Can the list communicate that a task still needs to be handed over even when its time has not been set? |
| HH-10 opened the replacement-vehicle row and thought a reply had arrived. The detail still showed a reply was awaited. | Can the list distinguish reading a row from receiving the other party's reply? |

## Two fields to explore

For a deadline-free item, show that action remains required and that the time is not yet set. Do not allow an empty time to imply that the item can be put aside today. Check after saving: ask the participant which items need to be handed over today and why.

For the replacement vehicle, show the response state separately from whether the recipient has opened or reviewed the row. Check after saving: ask whom the participant is waiting to hear from and what response they believe has arrived. Neither opening the list nor reading a row should change the response state.

## Decision still to make

These are proposals for the next prototype, not evidence that either wording has worked. Take the decision on deploying the same list with its present wording to the meeting; do not infer it from the count of saves. The two examples identify different misunderstandings and need separate checks.
