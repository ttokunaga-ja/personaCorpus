# Practice the silence after an unknown
CWR-DR-20260921-QST-01 v1.0 · September 21, 2026 · Jennifer Salazar
For: Ayesha Siddiqui

## Before you begin
Use this sheet on your own: read one situation, cover the response below it and say your next question aloud. Pause after the response before looking at the suggested continuation. The aim is to hear what the receiver knows at each point, not to supply an explanation for the hold. Mark the checks after each round and write the words you would change.

## 1. Back to the same route
The receiver returns to the list after an interruption and checks the route number. The receiver says: “Yes, this is the same one. I still don't know why it is held.”

1. What would you ask next? Write your question: __________________________________
2. Leave a quiet space. What did you want to learn before speaking again? ______________
3. If the receiver cannot name a reason, ask: “What did you look at when you came back?” Record the source or place, without assuming it contained the reason: __________________
4. Separately ask: “Who, if anyone, would you check with next?” Record the answer or “not known”: __________________

## 2. The first report repeats the status
The receiver's first report is “It's held.” Nothing yet tells you whether that status was new, where it came from, or who could explain it.

1. What would you ask next? Write your question: __________________________________
2. Wait. If the receiver has finished, ask: “What did you see or hear before you said that?” Record what was seen or heard: __________________
3. Ask separately: “Who, if anyone, would you check with next?” Record the answer or “not known”: __________________
4. If a reason emerges, ask when it became known and from which source. Do not treat the status itself as the reason.
