# One wrong answer still matters

June 19, 2026

Yesterday I almost let the comparison do all the talking: five of eight in February, one of eight this week. The groups were different people, but the same first-stop task gave us a useful reason to keep the new wording in contention. When I put the counts beside one another, I caught myself thinking, “only one left,” and moving directly to the reading-order hold.

TCR-06 stopped after saving and said the training was complete. The recorded cue was 「確認へ進む」: they understood it as an optional review. That is a different observation from Amanda's separate continuous-reading check. Amanda heard the heading after the action and did not hear the explanatory sentence on that path. We need to fix and check those two items first, but I cannot use that result to explain TCR-06's answer. The record does not establish that reading order caused it.

For the next trial I want to ask what happens if we change only the word 「確認」: can someone describe the next action in their own words before being prompted? Can they distinguish saving their progress from completing submission? Keep the first answer and its screen cue separate from anything said after help. A lower count is useful evidence, but it should not erase the remaining answer or turn a plausible explanation into an observed one.
