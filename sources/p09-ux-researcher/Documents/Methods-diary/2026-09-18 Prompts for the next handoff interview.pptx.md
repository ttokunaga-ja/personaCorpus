# Slide 2

Layout: Heading and text

Title: A 45-minute conversation

- 0–8 min · Entry: establish the last five worked shifts and invite one concrete handoff.
- 8–28 min · Route and source: follow the selected route, what can be read, and where else the person looked or asked.
- 28–45 min · Handoff over time: distinguish what was known before the list, what the list added, and what another contact added.
- Follow the participant's sequence; these are prompts to select, not a script to exhaust.

Presenter notes: Leave time for the participant to locate the same route and correct our account. If the route has no known reason, keep it unknown rather than supplying one.

# Slide 3

Layout: Two columns

Title: Entry, then route and source

Left — Entry, 0–8 minutes:
- Q01: Ask how many of the last five shifts included giving or receiving a handoff; count a shift with both once.
- Q02: Ask for a route they can show, their first sentence to the incoming person, and what they would check next.

Right — Route and source, 8–28 minutes:
- Q03: Ask what a note lets them pass on and what it adds to the hold explanation.
- Q04: Ask how they check they are back on the same line.
- Q05: After detail, ask what they see and what is additional for the handoff.
- Q06: Ask whether they can name another source for this particular route.

Presenter notes: In R-476 the participant could pass on the stop 02 west loading door instruction, but could not infer the reason for the hold from it. Keep the stop instruction and the unknown reason separate.

# Slide 4

Layout: Two columns

Title: Trace the handoff, 28–45 minutes

Left — Route and source:
- Q07: Who did you ask next? Record the source, not an assumed answer.
- Q08: What did you say when you repeated it? Check how the two people established the same route.
- Q09: What can be passed on from each item? Check the date as well as the route number.

Right — Order of knowledge:
- Q10: Reconstruct the order from before opening the list, including the route number.
- Q11: Ask what was learned at the later contact in the participant's own words.
- Ask separately what remains unknown at each point.

Presenter notes: One participant knew R-484 was waiting for a receiving-side reply from the previous dispatcher before seeing Held on the list. In a different account a second call established that departure bay availability was pending. Do not combine the paths or imply that the list supplied either reason.

# Slide 5

Layout: Table

Title: Four prompts to leave out

| Prompt | What the answer showed / why to change the move |
|---|---|
| Q12 · source examples | The participant would use any source that stated what was outstanding. Offering screen or writing first can narrow the source search; ask openly. |
| Q13 · two reasons for returning | The participant was checking the route's position, a third answer outside the offered choices. Ask what they checked after the action. |
| Q14 · cause and release together | Adam said he could see Held but could identify neither the cause nor whose word could release it from the screen. Ask these separately so each answer and its evidence can be recorded. |
| Q15 · relief as explanation | The participant corrected the proposed explanation: not seeing the previous shift did not establish that a regular dispatcher would have known. Ask what was found before proposing a cause. |

Presenter notes: Q14's response contains two distinct unknowns, not evidence that the participant gave only a bare no. Each replacement should leave room for an unknown answer.
