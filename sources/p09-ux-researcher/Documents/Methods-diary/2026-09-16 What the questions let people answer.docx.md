# What the questions let people answer

CWR-DR-20260914-DEC-01 · v1.0  
September 16, 2026 · Jennifer Salazar  
Decision with Patrick Delgado · Research team

## Decision and scope

Patrick and I reviewed the fifteen prompts taken from the July dispatch interviews on September 16. Keep Q01–Q11 for reuse when the situation calls for them; retire Q12–Q15 in the forms spoken. This is a count of prompts, not participants. The 2024 trial of eighteen questions is a separate exercise and is not part of this count.

The times below are from the start of the interview. Responses are team summaries, not quotations. An answer shows what was available to that person at that point; it does not establish a cause or a source they did not name.

## Decisions on the prompts

| Q01 — In your last five shifts, how many included giving or receiving a handoff? Think of a shift where you did both as one shift. We can go backward from the last one if that makes it easier. | Reuse | UX-184 01:05: four of five shifts included a handoff, counting a shift with both directions once. | Define the five shifts and count each shift once; use the backward walk only if helpful. |
| Q02 — Can we try it with a route you can show me? I'd like to hear the first sentence you'd be able to say and then follow whatever you check for the second. | Reuse | UX-184 06:08: for R-472 he could first say Held, then look for what remained outstanding. | Keep the first answer separate from what a subsequent check adds; let the participant choose a route to show. |
| Q03 — Got it. Read me what you'd pass along from that note. Then tell me what it adds to your answer about the hold. | Reuse | UX-184 20:47: the R-476 stop 02 note supplied a west loading door instruction, not the reason for the hold. | Ask what the note adds without treating an instruction as an explanation of the hold. |
| Q04 — When you go back, can you show me how you check it's the same line? I want to know what you'd compare before you said nothing had changed. | Reuse | UX-184 31:14: he compared the route number when returning to the same line. | Use after a return to the list; observe the comparison before concluding that any state is unchanged. |
| Q05 — I'm marking that you've been in the detail. When you get back, start with what you see, then tell me if you have anything additional for the handoff. | Reuse | UX-184 32:07: back on R-481 he saw Held but had no additional reason to pass on. | Separate the visible state from any new information; accept that the return may add no reason. |
| Q06 — If someone asked you which other source, do you know where to send them for this particular run? | Reuse | UX-184 35:49: he could not name a specific source and would ask using the route number. | Allow an unknown source and record the proposed next action separately from a known answer. |
| Q07 — その後、どなたに聞きましたか。 | Reuse | UX-176 08:15: the participant had first asked the previous dispatcher. | Ask who was consulted to identify the source; do not infer the substance of the later reply from the person named. |
| Q08 — 言い直すときは何を伝えましたか。 | Reuse | UX-176 16:20: the participant repeated the full R-421 number so the previous dispatcher could identify the route; the later answer concerned a reply from the loading lead. | Ask what was conveyed to establish the target route. Keep that separate from whom the participant asked and from the subsequent reply. |
| Q09 — 一度、二件それぞれから次に渡せることだけ抜き出してもらえますか。 | Reuse | UX-181 19:20: the participant compared dates as well as the route number and set aside the previous day's message. | Keep both items available and ask what can be passed on from each; record the date distinction instead of treating matching route numbers as sufficient. |
| Q10 — Let's put the paper back the way it was before you opened the list. If we start there, can you give me the order, with the number included? | Reuse | UX-185 11:21: for R-484 the participant heard a receiving-side reply was pending before opening the list and seeing Held. | Establish the starting point and route number; distinguish what was learned orally before opening the list from what the list showed. |
| Q11 — Okay. In your own words, what did you learn then? | Reuse | UX-188 25:21: the second call established that departure bay availability was pending. | Anchor “then” to the particular contact and ask what was newly learned at that point. |
| Q12 — Before you go in, what would make you say you've found it? Would you be looking for a particular place on the screen or for something someone had written? | Retire | UX-184 10:51: he would use any source that said what was outstanding; his answer did not limit it to the offered examples. | The examples preceded his account and narrowed the possible sources. Ask what would tell him instead, without supplying a source. |
| Q13 — Did you come back because you wanted to reread the status itself or because you thought the list might have something the detail hadn't given you? | Retire | UX-184 34:13: he was checking that he had returned to the same route. | Neither offered explanation captures that answer. Ask what he checked after returning before asking whether anything new was learned. |
| Q14 — Would you be able to say anything about what originally caused the hold, or who could clear it, from what you just checked? | Retire | UX-184 35:08: he could see Held, but could identify neither the cause nor whose word would release the route from that view. | Cause and release authority are separate unknowns. Ask each separately and retain unknown answers. |
| Q15 — ご自身は代務だから知らなかった、というふうにまとめていいでしょうか。 | Retire | UX-187 22:30: the participant corrected the summary; they had not seen the previous shift, but could not say the regular dispatcher would have understood the message. | The summary supplied a causal explanation the participant did not endorse. Ask what was missing and what the person knows without attributing the uncertainty to substitution. |

## Use in the next interview

Choose prompts to follow the participant's account, rather than treating the eleven as a sequence to complete. Keep the source of information, the identity of the route, and the date of a message as distinct checks. After a return or a call, ask what the person checked or learned there before asking for an explanation. Where an answer remains unknown, record that limit and ask separately about the next person or source to consult.
