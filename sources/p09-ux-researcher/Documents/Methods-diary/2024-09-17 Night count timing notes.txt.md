September 17

I started the imagined stopwatch too early. N02 stopped at 21:12 to move a cart, returned at 21:20, asked a colleague at 21:21 and resumed at C-08 at 21:22. The interval from the interruption includes the other task as well as the time after returning. It is not a measure of looking for the place.

N05 returned, heard C-12 from a colleague and resumed in the same recorded minute, 21:14. We did not see conspicuous searching, but a minute-resolution note cannot tell me that searching took zero seconds. It also doesn't turn the colleague's cue into no cue.

Stop trying to calculate an average duration or estimate time saved from these notes. If we observe again, mark return and the first reconciliation separately, with the cue and its time between them where visible. Record unknowns as unknowns; a more precise clock alone would not tell us what happened outside the observation.
