Hi Christine,

For your prototype team: the count is one person per category. The person who took us through three held routes is counted once, not three times. The 15 people whose observations support the recommendation comprise eight with the reason still unconfirmed, one who learned it after the handoff, and six who first learned what was pending through an additional question and answer. The other nine are four who found the reason in existing material, two who supplemented or checked the currency of an existing explanation, one who heard it before opening the list, and two whose described routes stayed Ready.

The comparison examples are useful for the next tasks. A known reason gives us a contrast for asking what someone can pass on and how they use its source and checked time. A route that stays Ready gives us a different contrast: it does not tell us that a hold reason was easy to find, because no hold was encountered in that scene. We can include both perspectives when deciding what a task should ask, alongside the unknown and previous-day conditions. They do not establish how the prototype will perform.

The 24 interviews were allocated by handoff frequency, so these counts should not be read as the proportion of all dispatchers who need the display.
