# Notes for the next handoff read

CWR-DR-20250417-RPT-01 · v1.0

Author: Jennifer Salazar  
For: Mihoko Kaneko and Ayesha Siddiqui

## Get the shift count after the account

Let the person finish describing a particular handoff. Then ask, “Thinking about your last five shifts, on how many of those shifts did you hand work over to someone or take over from someone?” Count each shift once, including a shift when the person did both. Write down what the person counted and the period they meant.

| What you hear | What to put beside the record |
|---|---|
| “Four of my last five. On one of those I took over and handed off again.” | **4 of 5 shifts.** The shift with both directions counts once. |
| “I counted the double as two shifts.” | Keep the reported count and write **double counted as two**. Ask which five work periods they included. If the periods are still unclear, leave the count unassigned for the read. |
| “I can’t remember how often. That time I checked my paper window against the one on the list.” | **Frequency unknown.** Record the described comparison separately: earlier window from paper against current Window from the route list. |

## Keep the action in view

On the handoff we’re reading, mark the first information the person had, the two things they compared, and what they passed to the next person. State how we know each part. For example, log a visible Vehicle value as an observation and the vehicle coordinator’s reported reply as the participant’s account. If all we saw was a stop Note being opened, record the opening and leave the comparison open.

The April 10 split (three or more versus two or fewer of the last five shifts) is for organizing observations during the read. Put confirmed counts beside the relevant cases. Keep an unknown count unassigned and read that case’s actions alongside the others. The account can still tell us where a comparison started and which evidence the person needed.
