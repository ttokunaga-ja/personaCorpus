# New to the desk — a working sketch

CWR-DR-20241031-DEC-01 · v0.1 · October 31, 2024 · Jennifer Salazar

## Working hypothesis

Someone new to the dispatch desk may have to find the basis for a decision afresh at each shift handoff. The person leaving knows which calls have happened and which values were only penciled in. The person coming on may inherit the same route and current values with very little of that history. I want to see whether being new to the desk explains the repeated look back, or whether the handoff itself leaves any dispatcher with the same work.

## Where the responsibility begins and ends

This person takes over the routes left on the desk. They need to know which plan the next shift is working from and which arrangements are still open. By the time they hand the desk on, they should be able to say what the next dispatcher can act on and whose answer is still needed. Taking the desk does not give them authority to release a hold or settle a vehicle arrangement somebody else owns.

| Moment | What this person can do |
|---|---|
| Taking over a route | Find the route and current Window, open a particular stop Note, and check a value against a note already in hand. Say what the list shows as of the handoff. |
| Getting ready to pass it on | Read the current Window against the handoff draft. Correct the time in the draft if the plan has changed. Keep the source of any open item with the item. |
| A separate answer is still due | Say a relief number is displayed, and wait for the vehicle coordinator before passing it as confirmed. If the receiving side still owes a call, pass on that message and where it came from. Leave the release owner open if nobody has established it. |

## What I want Brian to look for

On Monday Adam went back to R-308 six times. He could say what he checked against the screen on four of those visits. At 22 minutes I have the same row, and at 27 minutes I have stop 04's Note. For those two, I still cannot say what he compared. Before using this sketch in another discussion, check what the person knew on arrival, what they went back to establish, and whether they needed somebody else's answer. The number of visits by itself won't tell us who is having to reconstruct the handoff.
