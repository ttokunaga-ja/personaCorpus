# Dispatch handoffs — two working profiles

CWR-DR-20250415-DEC-01 | v1.0 | April 15, 2025 | Jennifer Salazar

For Brian Chen, Jeremy Sutton and the dispatch research team · Carver Workplace Research LLC

## How we're using the profiles

On April 10 Patrick and I agreed to read the dispatch cases by frequency of handoff. Keep the job title on the case for responsibility. To decide where to read it, count the shifts in which the participant gave or received a handoff, once per shift, over the last five shifts at the time of the interview. Three or more and two or fewer are working groups for these 12 interviews.

Use each user persona for the situation the person was in. The same person can fit a different profile when their schedule changes. Look to their account of the work for evidence of experience, including whether they handle routine and exception handoffs independently.

## Handoff is part of the shift · 3 or more of 5

**Starting condition.** When the outgoing and incoming dispatchers can work from a known route state, they need to get quickly to the changed or pending item. Start with what they can carry forward from the previous shift. Establish the route and planned window first whenever that context is what they actually receive.

**Evidence to make available.** Pair the current item with the source the dispatcher relied on. UX-037 (4/5) compared the previous dispatcher's message about a pending route with Held. Keep the message attributable to the prior dispatcher. A status on the list establishes what the plan shows. To explain why the item is pending or whether a new value is settled, the dispatcher may need a message or an answer from someone else.

**What to pass.** Identify the route, the item that changed or is pending, the source for its disposition and any outstanding question. If the display is ahead of the answer, carry the question forward with the contact who can answer it.

**Where this profile needs another entry.** UX-042 (5/5) received the route and planned window while a replacement vehicle was still being arranged. The participant described reading the displayed number, then comparing Vehicle with the coordinator's oral answer once it came. Only then did the participant pass the number as confirmed. We have the participant's account of that conversation. Keep the case here by count and keep its route-first entry in the April 14 diagram.

## Covering today · 2 or fewer of 5

**Starting condition.** When the incoming dispatcher has to rebuild the previous state, let them locate the route and bring the earlier plan or stop instruction into view. They need to know whose account they have and who last checked the open item.

**Evidence to make available.** UX-033 (1/5) recovered the earlier window from paper and compared it with the current Window. UX-043 (1/5) compared a paper exception memo with the stop Note. The participant described a condition that was on the paper and passed it orally to the next dispatcher. Kaneko's notes identify the paper as coming from the previous dispatcher. We did not retain the paper.

**What to pass.** Give the route and stop where relevant, the current value or instruction, and the source of any older or additional detail. When a condition remains open, state it and give the person or source to check. A window value and a condition for making a stop are separate handoff details.

**Where this profile stops explaining the action.** UX-044 (2/5) went back to the route list to select the next route. The interview gives us a count and a purpose for that action. It gives us no cross-check of an earlier value. A lower count is useful for finding cases to read, including experienced dispatchers such as UX-033 and UX-043.

## Cases to leave unassigned

UX-034, UX-036 and UX-040 have no established count for the last five shifts. Leave their frequency fields open. Keep Brian's January classifications with the interviews. For example, UX-041 stays Cross-check at interview level and the two unexplained returns at 22 and 27 minutes stay open at event level. Use the two profiles to ask what evidence the next dispatcher needs. External criteria and recruitment remain open.
