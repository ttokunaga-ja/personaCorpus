Jeremy and I separated the two Desk Dispatcher cards yesterday. I'd keep the job title on each card because it tells us something about the decision the person was responsible for. The 4/5 and 5/5 cases give us two sorts of evidence to look for in the same part of the count sheet. A previous dispatcher's message was available from the start in UX-037. In UX-042 the coordinator's answer arrived later. Seeing Vehicle filled in earlier didn't settle the number for that participant.

## Boundary to try

Try three handoffs in the last five shifts as the working boundary: three or more on one side, two or fewer on the other. A shift counts once if the participant gave or received a handoff. Lay UX-035 (3) next to UX-032 and UX-044 (2 each) before we decide what the boundary buys us. UX-035 checked a vehicle number against a prior note. UX-032 read out a route number. UX-044 went back to select the next route. Read their actions with the counts still in view and see whether the two sides make it easier to retrieve accounts of a changed item or of having to recover the route context.

My tentative conclusion is that someone handing over three or more times knows the procedure and can start with the changed item. I want Patrick to challenge that sentence as well as the cut: do the records actually support it, or only a difference in where this reading begins? UX-042 is the case I'd put alongside that sentence. Five handoff shifts and the first step was still route and planned window.
