# Three ways to open the interview

CWR-OPS-20251103-MOD-01 · v0.1 · November 3, 2025 · Jennifer Salazar

To: Ayesha Siddiqui and Patrick Delgado

## Purpose and roles

Ayesha will practice the opening in each scenario. Patrick and I will take turns as the person being interviewed and give feedback on what we heard before we discuss the next opening. Use a blank conversation and no participant screens or recordings.

| Practice | Time | Person playing interviewee | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | November 4, 15:00 | Jennifer | Agrees to recording |
| 2 | November 5, 14:00 | Patrick | Declines recording |
| 3 | November 6, 10:30 | Jennifer | Identifying information is about to appear in screen share |

## Opening

Say: “Thank you for joining. We are learning how people do this work and where the process is difficult. You can pause or stop at any point, and you can choose not to answer a question.”

Say: “May we record the conversation so the research team can listen again?” Wait for an answer. Do not start recording while asking.

If the answer is yes, say: “We keep the audio for 180 days after the interview.” Check that the person still agrees, then start recording. If the answer is no, say: “That's fine. I will take notes instead, without recording.” Continue without pressing for a reason.

## Role cues

**November 4 — Jennifer:** Agree to recording. Leave a small pause before answering; notice whether Ayesha waits and says when recording will begin.

**November 5 — Patrick:** Decline recording. Listen for what happens next and whether the choice is explained clearly enough to make it without rushing.

**November 6 — Jennifer:** Agree to recording at the opening. During the description of a task, indicate that a delivery name or destination is about to appear in the shared view. Do not show an actual name or screen.

**Ayesha:** If identifying details are about to appear, stop sharing and recording. Ask for a description without the name or destination. Before resuming audio, ask again and wait for agreement. Do not reconstruct the identifying details.

## Debrief

After each opening, ask the person playing the interviewee what was clear and where they hesitated. Note the words used and the change of action separately; bring any question about use outside the research back to Jennifer.
