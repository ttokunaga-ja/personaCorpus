# Payment entries — what goes in the report

18 August 2026

Use the Bernard example. CR/12/B f.043r l.4 records 0.50 franc on 10 April 1863; f.044r l.6 records 1.00 franc on 8 May. The second entry (receipt 20) refers back to the first (receipt 4). Both belong to Marguerite Bernard's account. Keep both dates in the example so the reader can see why there are two receipts against one balance.

The work for the report is the correction to Table 2.1 and its counting rule. In January Julie and I went back to the receipt lines, retained the quarterly counts of 42, 49, 45 and 48, and corrected the heading to “Payment entries”. The 184 is a count of recorded receipts. Where one handover covers three subscriptions, as at f.049r l.5, that is still one receipt. I have calculated neither a new headcount nor an attendance rate. There is no linked record from which to establish the relevant population and visits.

Put the April work in a separate paragraph if Roger wants it. Stefan and I reviewed 57 identification items: 51 with a documentary link and six kept under separate headings. The six are the Giraud entries (046r l.5 and 048r l.7), the Martin entries (049r l.8 and 051r l.4) and the Petit entries (052r l.9 and 054r l.8).
