# Morris — York rules and door sheets

14 March 2025

## Rule, February 1874

My extract from the York members' reading-room rules gives a quarterly subscription of six pence. An evening guest is to be accompanied by a member, who signs for the guest. Borrowing books outside the room is confined to members whose subscriptions have been paid. The signature belongs to the prescribed procedure for a guest; the payment condition belongs to a member's borrowing. I should not make the latter a charge on the guest.

## Attendant's sheets, March 1874

The surviving duty sheets I have looked at cover four evenings. Their guest column contains marks made by the attendant. It has no column for the guest's name or the accompanying member's signature. The sheets are not the record in which the rule requires a member to sign. They record marks on occasions, not identified people; a person returning on another evening cannot be recognised in them.

The subscription book is a different record. Without names or another shared identifier in the attendant's guest column I cannot link a mark to an individual entry in that book. Four evenings are the span examined, not a count of guests, and the marks cannot be turned into distinct visitors or compared with receipts as if both counted attendance.
