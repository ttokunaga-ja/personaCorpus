Questions for tomorrow — 17 July

Is 61% of the whole elapsed time?
No. It is 610 hours of approval waiting divided by 1,000 hours of total waiting in the 38 records. The 76 hours of recorded work are separate; receipt to settlement totals 1,076 hours across cases. These are elapsed case-hours, including nights and holidays, not staff hours or calendar duration.

Is fixing entry a waste of time?
No. We observed 19 hours of entry work at 03. There may be improvements there, but we have not measured their effect. The reason to examine the 07 and 09 hand-offs first is that they account for 560 of the 610 hours waiting for approval. That is a sequencing choice, not proof that entry cannot improve.

Are you proposing one fewer approver?
No. The double check required before bank release authorisation at 12 stays. We are asking how a complete item reaches the next reviewer and when review starts, not proposing to remove an approval.

Does the same 61% apply outside the 38?
We cannot say. Connor selected 40 standard payments received and settled between 8 April and 31 May in Japan time. Thirty-eight had the necessary pairs, including 28 without and 10 with returns. Two lacked approval-start pairs and were excluded, not given zero waiting time. The interviews explain the hand-off conditions; they do not measure the prevalence of a cause. We need further evidence before generalising.
